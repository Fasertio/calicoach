# Block 2 — Advanced Tuck Front Lever + Shoulder Rebuild · Marco

> Dates: 2026-09-07 to 2026-10-18 (5 weeks on + 1 deload)
> Days/week: 3 (Mon / Wed / Sat) · Session length: 60 min · Archetype: skill
> Review due: 2026-10-19

## Block aim

Take the front lever from a 12-second tuck to a 10-second advanced tuck, and
rebuild pain-free pressing capacity inside the shoulder's available range —
without provoking the right shoulder or the right wrist.

## Goals this block serves

| Goal | Marker at start | Target at review |
|---|---|---|
| Straddle front lever (target Sep 2027) | adv. tuck 5 s max | adv. tuck 10 s max, clean |
| Train pressing pain-free | painful arc 70–110° R, 3/10 | pain-free full abduction, SHOULDER-01 lifted |
| Hold pulling strength | weighted pull-up 5RM +10 kg | +12.5 kg |
| Wrist load tolerance | flat-hand front support 30 s, mild pain | 60 s pain-free, WRIST-01 lifted |

## Active constraints

<!-- copied verbatim from athlete/screening.md, 2026-09-01 -->

- **[SHOULDER-01]** No overhead pressing. No dips below humerus-parallel. No
  behind-neck work.
  Reason: painful arc 70–110° abduction, right shoulder, 3/10 at worst (screen
  item A3, Symptomatic).
  Forbids: overhead-press, overhead-load, behind-neck, dip
  Instead: landmine press, incline dumbbell press within the pain-free range,
  parallette push-ups, ring support holds.
  Earns it back: pain-free full abduction **and** a 20 s ring support hold with
  no front-shoulder pinch.
  Re-test: 2026-10-13. Refer if unchanged.

- **[WRIST-01]** No flat-hand floor loading beyond 30 s per set.
  Reason: wrist extension 65° right (screen item B3, Limited), mild pain in the
  flat-hand front support.
  Forbids: flat-hand-loading
  Instead: parallettes or push-up handles for all pressing and planche-adjacent
  work.
  Earns it back: 60 s pain-free flat-hand front support.
  Re-test: 2026-10-13.

**Consequence for this block:** no handstand work, no planche work, no deep dips.
Vertical pushing is reduced to one arced press — the constraint removes the whole
overhead quadrant. This is also why the block leans on the front lever: it is the
skill goal the constraints leave fully trainable.

**Programming band:** intermediate for pull, beginner-intermediate for push
(constraint-limited), intermediate for legs and core. Band is set by demonstrated
ability, not by years trained.

## Notation

| | |
|---|---|
| `3 e 4` | 3 sets weeks 1–3, 4 sets weeks 4–5 |
| `5 x 8-12"` | 5 sets, hold 8 to 12 seconds |
| `x l` | per side (*per lato*) |
| `RIR 2` | stop the set with 2 reps left in the tank |
| `RPE 8` | hold until the position is *about* to break — never until it breaks |
| `2-0-X-1` | tempo: 2 s down, no pause, **X = explosive up**, 1 s squeeze at the top |
| `A1 / A2` | a **paired set** — do A1, half rest, A2, half rest, repeat |
| `loop M / L` | band assistance, medium / light — we scale the band **down** as you get stronger |

Tempo is written eccentric-pause-concentric-pause. If you read Contreras'
*Bodyweight Strength Training Anatomy*, note that he writes tempo the other way
round (concentric first, three digits) — ours is always the four-digit form
above.

**Vertical push is the one pattern under its landmark**, at 3 sets against 6–12,
because SHOULDER-01 removes the entire overhead quadrant and leaves exactly one
usable arced press. It is not hidden: it rises to the landmark in block 3 the
week the constraint is lifted, and until then the ring support hold is doing the
work that gets us there.

Anti-extension and anti-rotation sit at 3 sets each — maintenance, deliberately,
because the front lever is already loading the anterior core hard twice a week
and that TUT is not in the core budget.

## The three rules that override this whole table

1. **Any red pain** (>4/10, sharp, worsening, or still there after 24 h) stops
   that movement immediately, regardless of what the plan says.
2. **No band scale-down, no leverage advance and no load increase** in a week
   where sleep or stress was materially worse.
3. **Straight-arm volume never climbs more than 10% week over week.** Front lever
   TUT plan: 120 → 125 → 135 → 145 → 150 s. If a session overshoots, the next
   one comes down.

# Autoregulation

**Bad day** (slept under 6 h, stress high, heavy DOMS)
Keep the exercises and keep the intensity. Cut the last set of every paired and
prehab exercise. Front lever work at 3 sets instead of 5. No band scale-down, no
load increase. Log it.

**Short day** (30 minutes)
Warm-up in full (10 min), then the skill work, then the **A pair** only, then the
prehab. Never compress by cutting rest below the paired-set interval — cut
exercises instead.

**Amber pain (3–4/10)**
Take the written regression for that exercise, keep it under 4/10 for the rest
of the session, finish the session, and report it in the log with the site and
the number. Two amber sessions on the same structure is treated as red.

**Red pain (>4/10, sharp, worsening, or lasting more than 24 h)**
Stop that movement for the session. Substitute:

| Instead of | Do |
|---|---|
| Front lever iso or tuck raise | ring row at the prescribed sets, and message the coach — straight-arm work is out until we talk |
| Weighted pull-up | bodyweight pull-up at RIR 3 |
| Landmine press | ring row (skip the press entirely) |
| Parallette push-up or incline press | ring row, and report it — that is both your horizontal pushes gone |
| Ring support hold | nothing — report it, this is the constraint we are tracking |
| Goblet squat or Bulgarian | wall sit in the pain-free range |
| Dumbbell RDL or single-leg RDL | glute bridge |
| Ab wheel | dead bug |

**Inner-elbow ache, any level**
The one signal with its own rule. Stop all straight-arm work for the session —
front lever iso, tuck raise and straight-arm pulldown. Run the elbow protocol.
Front lever volume restarts at 60% the following week. Do not wait for it to
reach 3/10.

**Missed sessions**
One missed: continue where you left off. Two or more in a week: repeat the week.
A full week missed: repeat the week at 70% of the sets.

## Front Lever Iso — Advanced Tuck, Band-Assisted

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | vertical pull — straight-arm |
| **Type** | isometric · straight-arm · assisted |
| **Primary muscles** | latissimus dorsi, teres major, long head of triceps, lower trapezius |
| **Secondary muscles** | rectus abdominis and obliques (anti-extension), biceps and brachialis (isometric at length), posterior deltoid, glutes |
| **Equipment** | pull-up bar, one resistance band |
| **Difficulty** | intermediate |
| **Skill prerequisite** | 12 s clean tuck front lever, 45 s hollow hold, 5 clean scapular pull-ups, straight-arm tolerance probe passed |

**Why it is here:** this is the block's primary target. The band lets us load the
advanced tuck position at a *fine* granularity — a band step is a fraction of a
leverage step, which is exactly what a straight-arm progression needs.

### Setup
1. Loop the band over the bar and let it hang. Grip the bar just outside
   shoulder width, pronated.
2. Pull to an inverted hang, then place the band under the **hips** — not the
   lower back, which lets the hips break the line and hides the core failure you
   need to see. The band should be snug, not slack.
3. Set the position: arms locked straight, scapulae depressed and retracted,
   pelvis in posterior tilt, knees tucked to the chest, then open the hips until
   the shins are parallel to the floor and the back is flat.

### Execution
1. **Start:** advanced tuck, back parallel to the floor, arms locked, band
   supporting the hips.
2. **Hold:** actively pull down on the bar with straight arms throughout. Ribs
   down, pelvis tucked, shoulders away from the ears.
3. **Breathe:** shallow and continuous. The position must not change as you
   breathe.
4. **Exit:** tuck the knees back in, then lower to a hang under control. Never
   drop out of the position.

### Cues
- "Arms locked — if an elbow bends, the set is over."
- "Shoulders down and back, chest open."
- "Pelvis tucked, ribs down — no arching."
- "Pull the bar down, don't just hang in the shape."

### Breathing
Shallow, continuous, through the nose. Breath-holding is how athletes fake an
extra three seconds; those seconds do not count.

### Tempo
Isometric. Enter over 2 s, hold, exit over 3 s.

### Range of motion standard
The **quality clock**: the timer stops at the first loss of position — an elbow
bending, the hips sagging, the scapulae collapsing, the shoulders shrugging. A
14-second set with 4 collapsed seconds is a 10-second set, and you record 10.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hips sagging below parallel | the core failed, not the lats; the low back is now loaded in extension | more ab wheel and hollow work; go back one band step |
| Elbows bending | it becomes a bent-arm hold and the biceps tendon takes an uncontrolled load | go to a heavier band immediately |
| Body above horizontal | the position drifts 20–30° up and stops being a front lever | film side-on; shoulders, hips and heels in one line, parallel to the floor |
| Scapulae retreating (shrugged) | trains the wrong pattern for the skill and stresses the shoulder | scapular pull-ups before the first set |
| Band under the lower back | changes the leverage and lets the hips break the line | band under the hips, always |
| Chasing seconds | the whole point is quality time | the clock stops at the break, and you record the honest number |

### Risk notes
Straight-arm loading is **elbow-tendon loading**. The tissues at risk are the
distal biceps tendon at the elbow and the long head of the biceps at the
shoulder. Warning sign: a deep ache at the front of the elbow, during the hold or
the next morning.

**Stop signal:** any inner-elbow ache, at any level. Not 3/10 — any. End all
straight-arm work for the session and tell the coach. This is the single most
important stop rule in the block.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Advanced tuck with a **heavier** band.
2. Tuck front lever, no band (the position Marco already owns at 12 s).
3. Tuck front lever with a light band, longer holds.
4. Scapular pull-ups with a 2 s hold plus hollow holds — if the position keeps
   collapsing, the problem is upstream.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Scale the band down one step (medium → light → none).
2. Extend the hold window: 8–12" → 10–14" → 12–15".
3. Advanced tuck unassisted, 5 x 10".
4. One-leg front lever, alternating sides evenly.
5. Straddle front lever.

### Substitutes
Straight-arm band pulldown with a 3 s hold at the bottom, plus front lever tuck
raises — same pattern, no full-body straight-arm exposure. Use these when the
elbow needs a week off.

### References
- `[OG2]` — front lever progression order, straight-arm volume guidance and the
  elbow warnings.
- `[OT]` — why straight-arm work is tendon-limited rather than muscle-limited.
- Video search terms: "advanced tuck front lever band assisted", "front lever
  scapular depression cue", "front lever posterior pelvic tilt".

## Weighted Pull-up

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | vertical pull |
| **Type** | compound · bent-arm · loaded |
| **Primary muscles** | latissimus dorsi, teres major, biceps brachii, brachialis |
| **Secondary muscles** | lower and middle trapezius, rhomboids, posterior deltoid, forearm flexors, core (anti-extension) |
| **Equipment** | pull-up bar, dip belt |
| **Difficulty** | intermediate |
| **Skill prerequisite** | 9+ strict bodyweight pull-ups (met), no active elbow or shoulder constraint on pulling |

**Why it is here:** the bent-arm base under the front lever. A skill block that
abandons heavy pulling produces a temporary skill and a weaker athlete.

### Setup
1. Load the dip belt and let it hang between the legs. Check the chain clears the
   floor at the bottom of the rep.
2. Grip pronated, hands just outside shoulder width, thumbs around the bar.
3. Hang with arms straight, legs together and slightly forward, ribs down,
   glutes engaged — a shallow hollow. This stops the swing before it starts.

### Execution
1. **Start:** active hang — scapulae already depressed, arms straight.
2. **Concentric:** drive the elbows down and slightly back toward the ribs. Lead
   with the chest, not the chin. The load must not swing.
3. **Top:** chin clearly over the bar, chest close to it, elbows pointing down.
   1 s squeeze.
4. **Eccentric:** lower over 2 s to full extension, scapulae releasing last.
5. **Bottom:** full extension. Re-establish the active hang before the next rep.

### Cues
- "Chest to the bar, not chin over the bar."
- "Elbows into your back pockets."
- "Glutes on — don't let the weight swing you."
- "Reach the bottom, every rep."

### Breathing
Inhale at the bottom, exhale through the second half of the pull. Braced trunk
throughout.

### Tempo
`2-0-X-1` — 2 s down, no pause at the bottom beyond re-setting the hang, an
explosive pull, 1 s squeeze at the top. The intent to move fast is what recruits
the high-threshold motor units; the bar may still move slowly under load.

### Range of motion standard
Dead-hang start with elbows fully extended, chin clearly above the bar, no kip,
no leg drive, controlled descent. A rep that breaks this does not count toward
the progression.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Partial range at the bottom | the most protective part of the range goes untrained, and the biceps tendon stays untrained at length | 1 s pause at full extension every rep; drop 2.5 kg |
| Swinging the load | momentum replaces strength and loads the low back | hollow body, 2 s pause between reps |
| Shrugged start | upper-trap dominance and shoulder stress | start every rep from a scapular pull-up |
| Elbows flaring wide | shifts load onto the shoulder joint | "elbows toward the ribs" |
| Load progressing faster than technique | this is how elbows get hurt | +2.5 kg only on the written trigger |

### Risk notes
Loads the biceps distal tendon, the long head of the biceps at the shoulder, and
the common flexor origin at the medial elbow. In this block it sits alongside
front lever work, so the **combined** load on the elbow is the thing to watch.
Any medial elbow pain: cut the load 10%, switch to a neutral grip, and reduce
front lever TUT 30% that week.

Grip is rotated deliberately across the week — pronated on Monday, neutral on
Saturday. Single-grip high-volume pulling is the most reliable way to produce
medial elbow pain in an otherwise healthy athlete.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Bodyweight pull-up at RIR 2.
2. Bodyweight pull-up, tempo `5-0-1-0`.
3. Ring row with feet elevated, weighted.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. +2.5 kg on the written trigger.
2. 4 x 5 instead of 3 x 5.
3. Cluster sets: `3+3+3` with 20" intra-set rest at a heavier load.
4. L-sit pull-up, bodyweight.

### Substitutes
Neutral-grip weighted pull-up if the medial elbow is irritable; lat pulldown at a
matched load if the bar is unavailable.

### References
- `[OG2]` — weighted pulling progression and volume caps.
- `[NSCA]` — loading principles and technique standards.
- Video search terms: "strict weighted pull up form", "dip belt pull up setup".

## Ring Row, Feet Elevated

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | horizontal pull |
| **Type** | compound · bent-arm |
| **Primary muscles** | middle trapezius, rhomboids, latissimus dorsi, posterior deltoid |
| **Secondary muscles** | biceps brachii, brachialis, forearm flexors, glutes and hamstrings (isometric), spinal erectors |
| **Equipment** | rings, a box for the feet |
| **Difficulty** | beginner–intermediate (angle-adjustable) |
| **Skill prerequisite** | 60 s front plank with a flat low back |

**Why it is here:** the only horizontal pull in the block, and the scapular
retraction work that keeps the shoulder healthy under all the vertical pulling
and the front lever holds. It is also the substitution named for three different
red-pain scenarios, so its technique has to be solid.

### Setup
1. Rings at roughly hip height. Grip neutral (palms facing each other).
2. Feet on a box so the body is below the rings and roughly horizontal.
3. Body in one straight line: heels, hips, shoulders, ears. Glutes squeezed,
   ribs down. Arms straight, shoulders allowed to protract at the bottom.

### Execution
1. **Start:** hanging at full arm extension, shoulder blades released forward.
2. **Initiate:** retract the shoulder blades first — the chest lifts a few
   centimetres before the elbows bend.
3. **Concentric:** pull the rings to the lower ribs, elbows tracking at roughly
   30–45° from the torso, not flared to 90°.
4. **Top:** chest touches or nearly touches the rings, scapulae fully retracted
   and depressed, body still one line. 1 s squeeze.
5. **Eccentric:** lower over 3 s to full extension, letting the scapulae
   protract at the very end.

### Cues
- "Lead with the chest, not the hands."
- "Shoulder blades into your back pockets, then bend the elbows."
- "One straight line — squeeze your glutes."
- "Reach at the bottom."

### Breathing
Exhale on the pull, inhale on the descent.

### Tempo
`2-0-X-1`. Explosive pull, and the 1 s squeeze at the top is where the retractor
work happens.

### Range of motion standard
Full arm extension with scapular protraction at the bottom; chest to the rings
with retraction at the top; body straight throughout. Hips sagging voids the rep.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hips sagging | turns it into an arm exercise and loads the low back | "glutes on"; drop the feet to the floor and finish the set there |
| Elbows flared to 90° | shifts load to the shoulder joint and away from the back | "elbows at 45 degrees", ribs down |
| Pulling with the arms only | the scapular retractors stay untrained | initiate with the shoulder blades, hold the 1 s squeeze |
| Short range at the bottom | the protraction range is the useful part | full extension every rep, reach at the bottom |
| Neck jutting forward | compensating for insufficient range | "long neck, chin tucked" |

### Risk notes
One of the safest loaded pulls, which is why it is the named substitute when the
shoulder or the elbow rules out something else. Check the ring straps and the
anchor before every session — a slipped ring from a feet-elevated position is a
real fall. Neutral grip is deliberate: it is the kindest wrist and elbow position
in a block already heavy with straight-arm work.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Ring row with the feet on the floor, body at 45°.
2. Ring row with the knees bent, feet flat.
3. Standing row against a band.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Add a 5 kg weight vest.
2. Tempo: 5 s eccentric, 3 s hold at the top.
3. Archer row.
4. One-arm ring row.

### Substitutes
Chest-supported dumbbell row, seated cable row, barbell row (only with a clean
lumbar screen), TRX row.

### References
- `[OG2]` — row progressions.
- `[NSCA]` — rowing mechanics.
- Video search terms: "feet elevated ring row form", "ring row scapular
  retraction".

## Ring Support Hold

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | constraint work — isometric shoulder tolerance |
| **Type** | isometric · straight-arm · closed chain |
| **Primary muscles** | anterior and lateral deltoid, pectoralis major, triceps (lockout) |
| **Secondary muscles** | serratus anterior, lower trapezius, rotator cuff (stabilising), core |
| **Equipment** | gymnastic rings at chest height, a box to step from |
| **Difficulty** | intermediate |
| **Skill prerequisite** | pain-free shoulder in the support position — this is the whole point of the exercise |

**Why it is here:** it is not push volume; it is the criterion that lifts
SHOULDER-01. A 20-second clean hold is half of the 13 October re-test. The
program contains the exercise that ends the constraint.

### Setup
1. Rings at chest height, straps checked. Box underneath so you can step in and
   out without jumping.
2. Step up into support: arms locked, rings close to the hips, wrists neutral.
3. **Depress the shoulders** — actively push down away from the ears. Ribs down,
   glutes engaged, legs together and slightly forward.

### Execution
1. **Start:** support position, elbows locked, shoulders depressed, rings turned
   slightly outward if that is comfortable (do not force turnout this block).
2. **Hold:** keep the rings still. Resist the urge to let the shoulders creep up
   toward the ears as the set goes on — that creep is the set ending.
3. **Exit:** step down to the box under control. Never drop off the rings.

### Cues
- "Shoulders away from your ears, the whole time."
- "Elbows locked, rings by your hips."
- "Ribs down, squeeze the glutes."
- "Still rings — no wobble."

### Breathing
Normal and continuous. Do not hold the breath — this is a tolerance drill, not a
maximal effort.

### Tempo
Isometric. Step in over 2 s, hold, step out under control.

### Range of motion standard
Elbows locked and shoulders demonstrably depressed for the whole hold. The clock
stops when the shoulders shrug, an elbow bends, or the rings start swinging.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Shrugged shoulders | loads the joint instead of the muscle — the opposite of what this drill is for | shorten the hold to 10 s and keep it perfect |
| Elbows soft | takes the triceps out and destabilises the position | lock them; if you cannot, use the feet-supported regression |
| Rings drifting away from the hips | lengthens the lever and stresses the anterior shoulder | "rings by your hips" |
| Forcing ring turnout | adds significant anterior shoulder stress, and this shoulder is constrained | no turnout until SHOULDER-01 is lifted |
| Pushing through a pinch | the pinch is the finding, not an obstacle | stop, report it, take the regression |

### Risk notes
Loads the anterior shoulder capsule and the long head of the biceps. Under
SHOULDER-01 this is prescribed **at the top of the range only** — no dipping, no
descending, no turnout. Any front-shoulder pinch stops the set and gets reported
the same day; it changes the re-test date, and that is useful information rather
than a setback.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Support hold with the feet lightly on a box, taking 30–50% of bodyweight.
2. Support hold on parallel bars (stable, no ring wobble).
3. Band-assisted support hold.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Extend the hold to 20 s, then 30 s.
2. Rings free of the box, legs straight and still.
3. Once SHOULDER-01 is lifted: ring support with turnout, then ring dips to
   parallel.

### Substitutes
Parallel-bar support hold if the ring wobble is provoking the shoulder — but note
in the log that the re-test criterion is specifically the ring version.

### References
- `[OG2]` — ring support and shoulder preparation.
- `[NEU]` — anterior shoulder loading in the support position.
- Video search terms: "ring support hold shoulders depressed", "ring support hold
  progression box".

## Landmine Press

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | vertical push |
| **Type** | compound · bent-arm · loaded |
| **Primary muscles** | anterior deltoid, pectoralis major (clavicular), triceps brachii |
| **Secondary muscles** | serratus anterior, upper and lower trapezius, core (anti-extension and anti-rotation), glutes |
| **Equipment** | barbell in a landmine attachment, or the bar end wedged into a padded corner |
| **Difficulty** | beginner–intermediate |
| **Skill prerequisite** | none — this is the constraint-friendly press |

**Why it is here:** SHOULDER-01 removes overhead pressing. The landmine press
travels an arc that avoids the painful 70–110° range while still loading the
shoulder in flexion, so the pressing pattern keeps progressing while the shoulder
settles. It is the single most useful press to know under a shoulder constraint.

### Setup
1. Wedge the bar end in a landmine sleeve or a padded corner. Load the free end.
2. Half-kneeling, knee down on the pressing side. Hold the bar end at the front
   of the shoulder, elbow tucked in, forearm vertical.
3. Ribs down, glutes engaged, trunk braced. The trunk does not lean back to help.

### Execution
1. **Start:** bar end at the shoulder, elbow close to the ribs.
2. **Concentric:** press up and **forward** along the bar's natural arc,
   finishing with the arm at roughly 45° in front of the head — not overhead. The
   elbow extends fully.
3. **Top:** full elbow lockout, scapula rotating up naturally, shoulder never
   entering the painful range. Brief squeeze.
4. **Eccentric:** return along the same arc over 2 s to the shoulder.

### Cues
- "Press up and away, along the bar's arc."
- "Ribs down — don't lean back to reach the top."
- "Elbow in, not flared."
- "Stop at the top of the arc, not overhead."

### Breathing
Inhale at the shoulder, exhale on the press. Braced trunk; no maximal Valsalva
needed at these loads.

### Tempo
`2-0-X-0`. Controlled down, explosive up along the arc.

### Range of motion standard
Full elbow lockout at the top of the bar's arc, with the trunk still upright and
the shoulder pain-free throughout. **If any point of the range produces pain
above 2/10, that is the end of the range for this block** — shorten it and tell
the coach.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Leaning back at the top | the lumbar spine takes the load and the shoulder avoids the work | brace, reduce load, stay half-kneeling |
| Pressing straight up | defeats the point — it puts the shoulder back into the painful range | "up and away"; check the bar path from the side |
| Elbow flaring out | anterior shoulder stress | "elbow past your ribs" |
| Rushing the eccentric | the controlled return is half the exercise | count the 2 seconds |
| Progressing load through pain | this is a rebuild, not a max-out | the 2/10 rule is absolute |

### Risk notes
Chosen specifically because it works **around** SHOULDER-01. It is not a free
pass: if the shoulder complains at any point in the arc, the range shortens or
the exercise comes out and an incline dumbbell press in the pain-free range takes
its place. Do not use this exercise to sneak up on overhead pressing before the
13 October re-test.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Band press along the same arc.
2. Landmine press seated, to remove the trunk demand.
3. Incline dumbbell press within the pain-free range.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. +2.5 kg on the written trigger.
2. Single-arm landmine press (adds anti-rotation).
3. Half-kneeling to a split-stance standing press.
4. Once SHOULDER-01 is lifted: seated neutral-grip dumbbell press, then overhead
   pressing, reintroduced at 60% of the landmine-equivalent load.

### Substitutes
Incline dumbbell press in the pain-free range; machine chest press; low-angle
cable press.

### References
- `[NSCA]` — pressing mechanics and shoulder loading.
- `[NEU]` — scapulohumeral rhythm, and why an arced press avoids the painful
  range.
- Video search terms: "landmine press half kneeling technique", "landmine press
  shoulder friendly pressing".

## Parallette Push-up

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | horizontal push |
| **Type** | compound · bent-arm · closed chain |
| **Primary muscles** | pectoralis major (sternal), anterior deltoid, triceps brachii |
| **Secondary muscles** | serratus anterior, core (anti-extension), glutes and quadriceps (isometric) |
| **Equipment** | parallettes or push-up handles |
| **Difficulty** | beginner–intermediate |
| **Skill prerequisite** | 30 s front plank with a flat low back |

**Why it is here:** the only horizontal push in the block. Parallettes are not a
convenience — WRIST-01 makes them mandatory, and they let the pressing pattern
keep progressing while the wrist tolerance is rebuilt separately.

### Setup
1. Parallettes at shoulder width, handles running parallel to the body so the
   wrists stay neutral.
2. Hands under the shoulders. Arms straight, elbows soft-locked, scapulae
   protracted — push the floor away.
3. Feet together. Glutes squeezed, ribs tucked, body one straight line from heels
   to head in a shallow hollow.

### Execution
1. **Start:** top of the push-up, body straight, scapulae protracted.
2. **Eccentric:** lower over 3 s, elbows tracking back at roughly 45° from the
   torso — not flared to 90°, not pinned to the ribs.
3. **Bottom:** chest arrives level with the handles. 1 s pause. The body stays
   one line — hips do not sag or pike.
4. **Concentric:** push the floor away, elbows extending, scapulae protracting at
   the very top.
5. **Top:** full lockout with active protraction. 1 s.

### Cues
- "Squeeze the handles and screw them outward."
- "Elbows at 45 degrees, arrow shape not T shape."
- "Squeeze your glutes, tuck your ribs."
- "Push the floor away at the top."

### Breathing
Inhale on the descent, exhale on the press. Ribs stay down on the inhale.

### Tempo
`2-1-X-1`. The pause at the bottom removes the bounce and exposes the true
strength level; the press itself is explosive.

### Range of motion standard
Chest level with the handles at the bottom, full lockout with protraction at the
top, body straight throughout. Hips sagging or piking voids the rep.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hips sagging | lumbar extension under load, and it hides weak pressing | glutes on, ribs down; regress to an incline |
| Hips piking | shortens the lever — you are avoiding the hard part | film side-on; regress the angle rather than cheat the line |
| Elbows flared to 90° | anterior shoulder stress — and this shoulder is constrained | "elbows to 45", narrow the hands slightly |
| No protraction at the top | the serratus stays untrained, which matters for the shoulder | 1 s "push away" at the top of every rep |
| Going deeper than handle level | extra shoulder extension for no benefit, straight at the constrained tissue | stop level with the handles |

### Risk notes
Under WRIST-01 this is prescribed on parallettes only; the flat-hand version
comes back when the wrist re-tests clean on 13 October. Depth is capped at handle
level to keep the anterior shoulder out of extension — deeper is not better here.
Any front-shoulder pinch means stop and substitute an incline dumbbell press.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Parallettes on a bench or box (incline).
2. Knee push-up on parallettes, straight line from knees to head.
3. Eccentric-only: 5 s down, knees to reset.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Feet elevated 20 cm.
2. Tempo: 5 s eccentric, 2 s pause.
3. Ring push-up.
4. Archer push-up on parallettes.

### Substitutes
Dumbbell floor press, machine chest press, incline dumbbell press — all keep the
wrist neutral and the shoulder out of extension.

### References
- `[OG2]` — push-up progressions and the pressing ladder.
- `[EXRX]` — push-up variations and muscle involvement.
- Video search terms: "parallette push up form", "push up elbows 45 degrees
  serratus protraction".

## Goblet Squat

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | knee-dominant |
| **Type** | compound · bilateral · loaded |
| **Primary muscles** | quadriceps, gluteus maximus |
| **Secondary muscles** | adductor magnus, hamstrings (stabilising), spinal erectors, core, calves |
| **Equipment** | one dumbbell or kettlebell |
| **Difficulty** | beginner |
| **Skill prerequisite** | knee-to-wall dorsiflexion ≥ 8 cm, or heel elevation |

**Why it is here:** the block's only knee-dominant work. The goblet position
self-corrects most trunk faults and needs no rack, which matters for a garage
setup and a 60-minute session.

### Setup
1. Feet between hip and shoulder width, toes turned out 10–30° — whatever lets
   the knees track over the toes.
2. Hold the weight at the sternum, elbows in, ribs down. Brace as if about to be
   pushed.
3. Weight spread across the whole foot: big toe, little toe, heel.

### Execution
1. **Start:** standing tall, braced, knees soft.
2. **Eccentric:** break at the hips and knees together over 3 s, sitting down
   between the feet rather than back behind them. The knees travel forward over
   the toes — normal and safe for a healthy knee.
3. **Bottom:** 1 s pause at the deepest position that keeps a neutral spine and
   the heels down.
4. **Concentric:** drive the floor away, hips and chest rising together.
5. **Top:** stand tall, glutes squeezed, no lumbar hyperextension.

### Cues
- "Sit down between your feet, not back."
- "Knees out over your second toe."
- "Spread the floor with your feet."
- "Chest and hips rise together."

### Breathing
Inhale and brace at the top, hold through the descent and the first half of the
ascent, exhale at the top.

### Tempo
`3-1-X-0`. Slow down, explosive up.

### Range of motion standard
The deepest depth at which the spine stays neutral and the heels stay down. Do
not force depth with a butt wink; do not accept a quarter squat.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Heels lifting | ankle dorsiflexion limit | elevate the heels 2 cm, add ankle mobility work, re-test in 4 weeks |
| Knees collapsing inward | hip abductor weakness or foot collapse | "knees out", band around the knees, glute-medius work |
| Butt wink at the bottom | loaded lumbar flexion at end range | stop above the wink, widen the stance |
| Hips shooting up first | quads giving up — it becomes a good morning | pause squats, reduce load |
| Chest collapsing forward | trunk or thoracic fatigue | keep the elbows in, reduce load |

### Risk notes
Low risk at this load. Depth is a prescription, not a moral achievement — the
pain-free, neutral-spine range is the range. Marco has no knee constraint; if
anterior knee pain appears, reduce to the pain-free depth, add isometric quad
work (wall sit), and tell the coach.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Box squat to a high box.
2. Heel-elevated goblet squat.
3. Bodyweight squat holding a TRX for balance.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. +4 kg on the written trigger.
2. Tempo: 5 s down, 2 s pause.
3. Front squat or Bulgarian split squat, once there is session time for it.

### Substitutes
Leg press or hack squat if available; wall sit and Spanish squat when only
isometric quad loading is tolerated.

### References
- `[NSCA]` — squat mechanics and loading.
- `[NEU]` — knee and hip mechanics through the squat.
- Video search terms: "goblet squat technique", "ankle mobility squat depth".

## Dumbbell RDL

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | hip hinge |
| **Type** | compound · bilateral · eccentric-biased |
| **Primary muscles** | hamstrings, gluteus maximus |
| **Secondary muscles** | spinal erectors, latissimus dorsi (load control), grip, adductor magnus |
| **Equipment** | two dumbbells |
| **Difficulty** | beginner–intermediate |
| **Skill prerequisite** | passes the dowel hinge screen; no active lumbar constraint |

**Why it is here:** the block's only hinge. The posterior chain is what holds the
front lever body line together and keeps the low back healthy under everything
else.

### Setup
1. Feet hip width, a dumbbell in each hand at arm's length in front of the
   thighs.
2. Knees soft — unlocked but not bent. Shoulders back and down, lats engaged so
   the dumbbells stay close to the legs.
3. Neutral spine: head, upper back and sacrum in line.

### Execution
1. **Start:** standing tall, braced, dumbbells against the thighs.
2. **Eccentric:** push the hips straight back over 3 s, letting the torso
   descend. The dumbbells slide down the thighs, staying within a couple of
   centimetres. Shins stay nearly vertical.
3. **Bottom:** 1 s pause at the point where the hamstrings are at full tension
   **and the spine is still neutral** — for most people mid-shin. Depth is
   decided by spinal position, never by the floor.
4. **Concentric:** drive the hips forward, squeezing the glutes, standing tall.
5. **Top:** hips fully extended, ribs down, no lumbar hyperextension.

### Cues
- "Push your hips back to the wall behind you."
- "Dumbbells stay on your legs."
- "Long spine, chest proud."
- "Stop when your back would round."

### Breathing
Inhale and brace at the top, hold through the descent, exhale at the top of the
ascent.

### Tempo
`3-1-X-0`. The slow eccentric is the point of the exercise; the drive back up is
explosive.

### Range of motion standard
The lowest position with a demonstrably neutral spine, hips loaded, shins near
vertical. Any rounding ends the range.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Rounding the low back | loaded lumbar flexion — the highest-risk fault in the catalogue | shorten the range, use a dowel to check, drop 30% of the load |
| Squatting instead of hinging | the quads take over and the hamstrings go untrained | "hips back, not down"; wall-touch drill |
| Dumbbells drifting forward | large lever arm on the low back | "lats on, weights on the legs" |
| Hyperextending at the top | lumbar stress at lockout | "ribs down, squeeze glutes, stop at tall" |
| Chasing the floor | depth beyond your hamstring length | depth is decided by spinal position |

### Risk notes
Loads the lumbar spine through the erectors if the neutral position is lost.
Marco has no lumbar constraint, but this is the exercise where one would appear
first. Any radiating leg symptoms mean stop and refer. Grip may fail before the
hamstrings at higher loads — use straps rather than shortening the set.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Dowel hinge drill, bodyweight.
2. Hip hinge to a wall touch.
3. RDL from a 30 cm box (shortened range).

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. +4 kg per hand on the written trigger.
2. Single-leg RDL.
3. Tempo: 5 s eccentric.

### Substitutes
Hip thrust, cable pull-through, back extension to neutral, seated leg curl.

- `[NEU]` — hip extension and lumbopelvic control.

## Side Plank

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | anti-rotation / lateral |
| **Type** | isometric · unilateral |
| **Primary muscles** | obliques (internal and external), quadratus lumborum |
| **Secondary muscles** | gluteus medius, latissimus dorsi, transversus abdominis, adductors |
| **Equipment** | floor, mat |
| **Difficulty** | beginner |
| **Skill prerequisite** | none |

**Why it is here:** the block's lateral-chain work, and the side-to-side
comparison it gives is worth as much as the training effect. Asymmetry here shows
up later as a crooked front lever.

### Setup
1. Lie on one side, forearm on the floor, elbow directly under the shoulder.
2. Legs straight and stacked, feet together.
3. Lift the hips so the body is one straight line from ankle to ear. Top hand on
   the top hip or reaching to the ceiling.

### Execution
1. **Start:** hips lifted, body in a straight line, shoulders and hips stacked
   vertically.
2. **Hold:** push the floor away with the forearm — do not sink into the
   shoulder. Squeeze the glutes. Ribs down.
3. **Breathe:** normally and continuously. The position does not change.
4. **Exit:** lower the hips under control, do not collapse.

### Cues
- "Push the floor away — don't sag into your shoulder."
- "Stack your hips, one on top of the other."
- "Squeeze your glutes, ribs down."
- "Long body, from ankle to ear."

### Breathing
Continuous and normal. Breath-holding shortens the hold and does not transfer.

### Tempo
Isometric. Prescribe seconds.

### Range of motion standard
Hips stacked and lifted, body in one line, for the whole hold. The clock stops
when the hip drops or the shoulders rotate.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hips dropping | the obliques have stopped working | end the set — that is the honest time |
| Rotating toward the floor | the anti-rotation demand disappears | stack the shoulders, top hand on the hip |
| Sinking into the bottom shoulder | loads the joint instead of the muscle | "push the floor away" |
| Holding the breath | unsustainable, and it does not transfer | count out loud |
| Ignoring the weaker side | asymmetry compounds | match the weak side's time on both sides |

### Risk notes
Very low risk. Shoulder discomfort on the supporting side means the shoulder is
sinking — fix the position rather than pushing through, particularly with
SHOULDER-01 live. Match the time on both sides to the weaker side; a >20%
difference gets reported.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Side plank with the knees bent, shins on the floor.
2. Side plank against a wall with the feet braced.
3. Side-lying hip abduction.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Extend the hold to 45 s per side.
2. Add a 5 kg plate on the top hip.
3. Top leg lifted (star side plank).
4. Copenhagen plank progression.

### Substitutes
Suitcase carry, Pallof press, bird dog.

### References
- `[NSCA]` — trunk stability and lateral chain training.
- `[BK]` — lateral chain and its role in load tolerance.
- Video search terms: "side plank form hips stacked", "side plank common
  mistakes".

## Front Lever Tuck Raise

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | vertical pull — straight-arm, dynamic |
| **Type** | compound · straight-arm · concentric-eccentric |
| **Primary muscles** | latissimus dorsi, teres major, long head of triceps |
| **Secondary muscles** | rectus abdominis and obliques, lower trapezius, biceps and brachialis (isometric at length), hip flexors |
| **Equipment** | pull-up bar |
| **Difficulty** | intermediate |
| **Skill prerequisite** | 10 s clean tuck front lever, controlled inverted hang |

**Why it is here:** the isometric hold builds tolerance in one position; the
raise builds strength through the range and teaches the entry Marco will need for
the straddle later.

### Setup
1. Grip the bar just outside shoulder width, pronated. Pull to an inverted hang.
2. Tuck the knees to the chest, pelvis in posterior tilt, arms locked straight,
   scapulae depressed.

### Execution
1. **Start:** inverted tuck hang, hips above the shoulders, arms locked.
2. **Eccentric:** lower under control over 3 s until the back is parallel to the
   floor — the tuck lever position. Arms stay locked; the movement happens at the
   shoulder.
3. **Bottom:** 1 s pause in the tuck lever. This pause is the exercise.
4. **Concentric:** pull back up to the inverted tuck hang over 1 s, driving the
   straight arms down against the bar.
5. Repeat without releasing the tuck.

### Cues
- "Elbows locked the whole way — the shoulders do the work."
- "Pause at parallel, don't bounce through it."
- "Ribs down, knees glued to the chest."
- "Pull the bar toward your hips."

### Breathing
Exhale on the way up, inhale at the top. Do not hold the breath — this is a
4-rep set, not a maximal effort.

### Tempo
`3-1-X-0` — 3 s down, 1 s pause at parallel, explosive up.

### Range of motion standard
From hips-above-shoulders to back-parallel, with the elbows locked and the tuck
held. A rep that ends above parallel, or that bends an elbow, does not count.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Elbows bending on the way up | becomes a bent-arm pull; the straight-arm stimulus disappears | reduce to 3 reps, or take the isometric only for two weeks |
| Not reaching parallel | the useful part of the range is skipped | film side-on, reduce reps |
| Opening the tuck under fatigue | the leverage changes silently and the load spikes | stop the set when the tuck opens |
| Dropping through the eccentric | the eccentric is most of the adaptation, and dropping spikes tendon load | count the 3 seconds out loud |
| Adding reps too fast | straight-arm volume caps apply here too | maximum 4 reps per set, 4 sets |

### Risk notes
Higher peak elbow-tendon load than the isometric hold, because the tissue is
loaded through a range rather than at one angle. It shares the block's
straight-arm budget with the front lever iso and the straight-arm band pulldown;
all three come out together at the first inner-elbow ache.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Front lever tuck **negative** only: 3 x 1 rep, 5 s descent, reset from a hang.
2. Tuck raise with a band under the hips.
3. Isometric tuck hold only.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Add a 2 s pause at parallel.
2. 4 sets instead of 3.
3. Advanced tuck raise.
4. One-leg raise.

### Substitutes
Straight-arm band pulldown with a 3 s hold at the bottom — same joint action,
much lower tendon exposure.

### References
- `[OG2]` — front lever dynamic progressions.
- `[OT]` — straight-arm tendon loading and recovery.
- Video search terms: "front lever tuck raise tutorial", "front lever negative
  controlled descent".

## Pull-up, Neutral Grip

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | vertical pull |
| **Type** | compound · bent-arm |
| **Primary muscles** | latissimus dorsi, teres major, brachialis, biceps brachii |
| **Secondary muscles** | lower and middle trapezius, rhomboids, posterior deltoid, forearm flexors, core |
| **Equipment** | neutral-grip pull-up handles, or rings |
| **Difficulty** | intermediate |
| **Skill prerequisite** | 6+ strict pull-ups |

**Why it is here:** the week's second vertical pull, deliberately in a different
grip from Monday's. Grip rotation is the cheapest medial-elbow insurance
available in a block this heavy on pulling.

### Setup
1. Neutral handles at roughly shoulder width, palms facing each other. Rings work
   if no handles are available — they rotate freely, which is kinder still.
2. Hang with arms straight, legs together and slightly forward, ribs down, glutes
   engaged.

### Execution
1. **Start:** active hang — scapulae depressed, arms straight.
2. **Concentric:** drive the elbows down toward the ribs. The neutral grip lets
   the elbows stay closer to the body than a pronated pull-up.
3. **Top:** chin clearly above the hands, chest close to them. 1 s squeeze.
4. **Eccentric:** lower over 2 s to full extension, scapulae releasing last.
5. **Bottom:** full extension, re-establish the active hang.

### Cues
- "Elbows down to your ribs."
- "Chest up to your hands."
- "Glutes on, no swing."
- "All the way down, every rep."

### Breathing
Inhale at the bottom, exhale through the second half of the pull.

### Tempo
`2-0-X-1`.

### Range of motion standard
Dead-hang start, chin clearly above the hands, no kip, controlled descent.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Cutting the bottom range | the biceps tendon stays untrained at length | 1 s pause at full extension |
| Kipping under fatigue | momentum replaces strength and loads the low back | stop the set at the first kipped rep |
| Shrugging at the start | upper-trap dominance | start from a scapular pull-up |
| Chin craning | hides an incomplete rep | "chest to your hands" |

### Risk notes
The most elbow-friendly of the pull-up grips, which is exactly why it is here on
the heavier straight-arm day. If medial elbow symptoms appear anyway, this is the
last pulling variation to remove — cut the weighted pull-up and the straight-arm
work first.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Band-assisted neutral-grip pull-up.
2. Eccentric-only: step to the top, lower over 5 s.
3. Ring row, feet elevated.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. +2.5 kg on the written trigger.
2. 4 sets instead of 3.
3. Tempo: 5 s eccentric.
4. L-sit pull-up.

### Substitutes
Ring pull-up; lat pulldown with a neutral handle.

### References
- `[OG2]` — pulling progressions and grip selection.
- `[EXRX]` — grip variations and muscle involvement.
- Video search terms: "neutral grip pull up form", "pull up grip rotation elbow
  health".

## Straight-Arm Band Pulldown

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | vertical pull — straight-arm |
| **Type** | isolation · straight-arm |
| **Primary muscles** | latissimus dorsi, teres major, long head of triceps (as a shoulder extensor) |
| **Secondary muscles** | posterior deltoid, lower trapezius, core (anti-extension) |
| **Equipment** | resistance band anchored overhead |
| **Difficulty** | beginner |
| **Skill prerequisite** | none — and it is the mandatory accessory for front lever work |

**Why it is here:** the highest-value front lever accessory. It trains shoulder
extension with a locked elbow at a load you can progress in small steps, and it
is the earliest warning system for the elbow — an ache here shows up before an
ache in the lever.

### Setup
1. Band anchored high, well above head height. Stand facing the anchor, feet hip
   width, one foot slightly back for balance.
2. Grip the band with straight arms, hands shoulder width, arms at roughly 45°
   overhead.
3. Hinge slightly at the hips, ribs down, trunk braced. Elbows locked — they stay
   locked for the whole set.

### Execution
1. **Start:** arms straight overhead-forward, lats stretched.
2. **Concentric:** drive the straight arms down in an arc toward the thighs,
   thinking about pulling the humerus back and down rather than about the hands.
3. **End:** hands arrive at the thighs, lats fully shortened, scapulae depressed.
   2 s hold. Do not lean back to get further.
4. **Eccentric:** return along the same arc over 3 s, letting the lats lengthen
   fully without shrugging.

### Cues
- "Elbows locked the whole time."
- "Push the band away in an arc, don't pull it down."
- "Ribs down — no leaning back."
- "Feel it in the armpit, not the triceps."

### Breathing
Exhale on the pull-down, inhale on the return.

### Tempo
`3-0-X-2` — 3 s return, an explosive pull, 2 s hold at the bottom.

### Range of motion standard
Elbows locked throughout, full overhead stretch at the top, hands reaching the
thighs at the bottom without trunk lean.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Elbows bending | becomes a triceps pushdown and the straight-arm stimulus is lost | drop a band, film side-on |
| Leaning back at the bottom | the trunk does the work, not the lats | brace, stand closer, lighter band |
| Shrugging on the return | trains the wrong pattern for the lever | slow the return, "shoulders down" |
| Band too heavy | this is connective-tissue work; heavy loads with a locked elbow spike biceps-tendon stress | stay in the 12–15 rep range at RIR 3 |

### Risk notes
Straight-arm loading is elbow-tendon loading. Progress in small steps and never
take it to failure. **Any inner-elbow ache here is a leading indicator that front
lever volume needs to come down** — it is the cheapest early warning in the
block, so report it the same day rather than waiting to see if it settles.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Lighter band.
2. Seated version, to remove the trunk demand.
3. Prone Y-to-I raises on the floor, bodyweight.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Next band up on the written trigger.
2. Extend the hold at the bottom to 3 s.
3. Kneeling single-arm pulldown.
4. Progress to front lever tuck pulls.

### Substitutes
Cable straight-arm pulldown if a machine is available — it loads in cleaner
increments than a band.

### References
- `[OG2]` — straight-arm scapular strength and front lever accessories.
- `[EXRX]` — straight-arm pulldown muscle involvement.
- Video search terms: "straight arm pulldown for front lever", "band straight arm
  pulldown technique".

## Ab Wheel from Knees

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | anti-extension |
| **Type** | compound · isometric-dynamic |
| **Primary muscles** | rectus abdominis, external and internal obliques |
| **Secondary muscles** | transversus abdominis, latissimus dorsi, hip flexors, serratus anterior, triceps |
| **Equipment** | ab wheel, mat for the knees |
| **Difficulty** | intermediate |
| **Skill prerequisite** | 45 s hollow hold with a flat low back |

**Why it is here:** the front lever fails at the core before it fails at the lats
for most athletes at this rung. This is the most efficient anti-extension loading
available in the time the session has.

### Setup
1. Kneel on a mat, hands on the wheel directly under the shoulders.
2. Posterior pelvic tilt — tuck the pelvis under and pull the ribs down. Hold
   that shape; it is the whole exercise.
3. Squeeze the glutes. Shoulders protracted, arms straight.

### Execution
1. **Start:** kneeling, wheel under the shoulders, pelvis tucked, low back flat.
2. **Eccentric:** roll forward over 3 s, extending only as far as the low back
   stays flat. The moment the low back starts to arch, that is your range.
3. **Bottom:** brief pause at full range, body in one line from knees to hands.
4. **Concentric:** pull back over 1 s using the abdominals — think about dragging
   the wheel back with your ribs, not your hip flexors.

### Cues
- "Pelvis tucked, ribs down — hold that shape the whole way."
- "Roll out only as far as your back stays flat."
- "Pull back with your stomach, not your hips."
- "Squeeze your glutes throughout."

### Breathing
Exhale on the way out, inhale on the way back. Do not let the ribs flare on the
inhale.

### Tempo
`3-0-X-0`. Slow out, explosive back.

### Range of motion standard
The furthest extension with the low back demonstrably flat. Any lumbar arch ends
the range — and the rep.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Low back arching | loaded lumbar extension, the exact thing this exercise exists to prevent | shorten the range; film side-on |
| Hips piking on the return | the hip flexors take over from the abdominals | "pull with your ribs" |
| Rolling out too far too soon | the range is the load; overreaching is a load spike | +5 cm per progression step, not per session |
| Shoulders shrugging | loses the protraction and destabilises | "push the floor away" |
| Holding the breath | unsustainable and it raises intra-abdominal pressure unnecessarily | exhale on the way out |

### Risk notes
Loads the lumbar spine in extension if the neutral position is lost — the fault
*is* the risk. Marco has no lumbar constraint; if low back discomfort appears,
shorten the range immediately and substitute dead bug. Also loads the shoulders
in flexion at the end range, so stop if the constrained shoulder complains.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Ab wheel to a wall stop, limiting the range.
2. Ring or TRX fallout with the feet on the floor.
3. Dead bug.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Extend the range 5 cm on the written trigger.
2. Full range from the knees.
3. Standing ab wheel (advanced — a long way off).

### Substitutes
Ring fallout, body saw on sliders, hollow hold.

### References
- `[OG2]` — anti-extension progressions and their role in lever work.
- `[NSCA]` — trunk stability training.
- Video search terms: "ab wheel rollout knees form", "ab wheel low back flat
  cue".

## Bulgarian Split Squat

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | knee-dominant |
| **Type** | compound · **unilateral** |
| **Primary muscles** | quadriceps, gluteus maximus of the front leg |
| **Secondary muscles** | adductor magnus, hip flexors of the rear leg (stretched), gluteus medius and minimus (frontal-plane stability), hamstrings, core |
| **Equipment** | a bench, box or chair; dumbbells to load it |
| **Difficulty** | intermediate |
| **Skill prerequisite** | 10 clean bodyweight split squats per side, no active knee constraint |

**Why it is here:** the block's unilateral knee work. Bilateral squatting never
asks the hip abductors and adductors to co-contract to keep a knee tracking, and
that stabilising demand is exactly what a single-leg athlete needs `[BSTA]`. It
also exposes a side-to-side gap that a goblet squat hides.

### Setup
1. Stand about one stride in front of a bench. Place the top of the rear foot on
   the bench, laces down.
2. Adjust the stride: the front shin should be near vertical at the bottom, and
   the rear knee should travel down, not back.
3. Dumbbells at the sides, or one held at the chest. Ribs down, trunk tall but
   allowed a slight forward lean.

### Execution
1. **Start:** tall, front foot flat, weight through the mid-foot and heel.
2. **Eccentric:** lower over 2 s, the rear knee travelling straight down toward
   the floor. The front knee tracks over the second toe.
3. **Bottom:** rear knee stops 2–5 cm above the floor, or at the deepest
   pain-free position. Front heel stays down.
4. **Concentric:** drive through the whole front foot, explosively, back to tall.
   The rear leg is a kickstand, not a driver.
5. Complete all reps on one side before switching.

### Cues
- "Rear knee straight down, not back."
- "Front heel glued to the floor."
- "Knee over your second toe."
- "Rear leg is a kickstand — drive with the front."

### Breathing
Inhale at the top, brace, exhale on the drive up.

### Tempo
`2-0-X-0`. Explosive concentric — the intent to move fast is the point.

### Range of motion standard
Rear knee to within ~5 cm of the floor, or the deepest pain-free position, with
the front heel down and the trunk stable. Both sides get the same depth.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Front knee collapsing inward | valgus load on the knee, and the glute medius is not doing its job | "knee over the second toe"; add banded lateral walks; drop the load |
| Stride too short | the knee travels far past the toe and the quad takes an unnecessary spike | step further from the bench |
| Pushing off the rear foot | defeats the unilateral purpose | "kickstand, not a driver"; lighten the rear-foot contact |
| Trunk collapsing forward | usually load, sometimes ankle range | reduce load; check knee-to-wall |
| One side quietly trained less | asymmetry compounds | start with the weaker side and match the stronger to it |

### Risk notes
High patellofemoral load at the bottom, and the balance demand means the last
reps of a set degrade fastest. With any anterior-knee constraint, reduce to the
pain-free depth and add isometric quad work rather than pushing depth. The rear
foot position stretches the rear hip flexor — a sharp pinch at the front of the
rear hip means shorten the stride.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Split squat with both feet on the floor.
2. Rear foot on a low step rather than a bench.
3. Holding a support with one hand.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Add load, 4 kg steps.
2. Front foot elevated for more range.
3. Tempo: 4 s eccentric with a 2 s pause.
4. Pistol squat progressions.

### Substitutes
Reverse lunge, step-up, split squat, leg press single-leg.

### References
- `[BSTA]` — unilateral training and the stabiliser demand bilateral work misses.
- `[NSCA]` — single-leg mechanics and loading.
- Video search terms: "bulgarian split squat setup stride length", "split squat
  knee tracking cue".

## Single-Leg Romanian Deadlift

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | hip hinge |
| **Type** | compound · **unilateral** · eccentric-biased |
| **Primary muscles** | hamstrings and gluteus maximus of the standing leg |
| **Secondary muscles** | gluteus medius (frontal-plane and rotational control), spinal erectors, obliques (anti-rotation), foot and ankle stabilisers, grip |
| **Equipment** | one or two dumbbells or a kettlebell |
| **Difficulty** | intermediate |
| **Skill prerequisite** | clean bilateral RDL pattern, 30 s single-leg balance |

**Why it is here:** the block's unilateral hip work, and the best available test
of whether the two sides of the posterior chain match. It also trains
anti-rotation at the hip and trunk, which no bilateral hinge does.

### Setup
1. Stand on one leg, knee soft. Hold the weight in the **opposite** hand (or
   both hands for more load and less rotational demand).
2. Square the hips and shoulders to the floor. Neutral spine — head, upper back
   and sacrum in line.
3. The free leg extends straight back as a counterweight.

### Execution
1. **Start:** standing tall on one leg, braced.
2. **Eccentric:** hinge at the standing hip over 3 s, pushing it straight back.
   The torso and the free leg descend together, staying in one line.
3. **Bottom:** torso near parallel, or the deepest position with a neutral spine
   and square hips. The weight stays close to the standing leg.
4. **Concentric:** drive the standing hip forward, explosively, back to tall.
5. Complete all reps on one side before switching.

### Cues
- "Hips square — do not let the free hip open to the ceiling."
- "Torso and back leg move as one bar."
- "Push the hip back, not down."
- "Grip the floor with your standing foot."

### Breathing
Inhale and brace at the top, hold through the descent, exhale at the top.

### Tempo
`3-0-X-0`. Slow down, fast up. Balance improves with the slow descent.

### Range of motion standard
The lowest position with a neutral spine **and** level hips. Rotation of the
pelvis ends the range, not the position of the dumbbell.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Free hip rotating open | the exercise stops being a hinge and becomes a twist; the glute medius is skipped | "hips square"; reduce range; film from behind |
| Rounding the low back | loaded lumbar flexion | shorten the range, lighten the load |
| Standing knee locked rigid | balance gets worse and the hamstring takes a shock at the bottom | "soft knee, not bent" |
| Rushing to keep balance | balance is a trained quality, not a nuisance | slow down; touch a wall with one finger while learning |
| Uneven load between sides | a real asymmetry gets hidden | match both sides to the weaker one |

### Risk notes
Lower absolute load than a bilateral RDL, so lumbar risk is lower — but the
rotational component means a lost neutral spine happens sideways as well as
forward. Any low back or hamstring-insertion pain means shorten the range. If
balance is the limiter rather than the hamstring, hold a support: the training
effect is in the hinge, not in the wobbling.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Kickstand RDL — rear toe lightly on the floor for balance.
2. Single-leg RDL holding a doorframe or rack with one hand.
3. Bilateral dumbbell RDL.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Add load, 4 kg steps.
2. Deficit — stand on a 5 cm plate.
3. Contralateral load only (one dumbbell, opposite hand) for more anti-rotation.
4. Tempo: 5 s eccentric.

### Substitutes
Single-leg hip thrust, kickstand RDL, cable pull-through on one leg, back
extension.

### References
- `[BSTA]` — hip-dominant unilateral work and its role in structural balance.
- `[NSCA]` — hinge mechanics and unilateral loading.
- Video search terms: "single leg romanian deadlift hips square", "single leg RDL
  form check".

## Incline Dumbbell Press

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | horizontal push |
| **Type** | compound · bent-arm · loaded |
| **Primary muscles** | pectoralis major (clavicular head), anterior deltoid, triceps brachii |
| **Secondary muscles** | serratus anterior, rotator cuff (stabilising), core |
| **Equipment** | adjustable bench, dumbbells |
| **Difficulty** | beginner–intermediate |
| **Skill prerequisite** | pain-free pressing in the available range; bench angle set below the painful arc |

**Why it is here:** the second horizontal push, chosen because dumbbells let the
shoulder find its own path and the bench angle can be dialled to stay under the
painful arc. It is also the named substitute if the parallette push-up has to
come out.

### Setup
1. Bench at **30°** — low enough to stay out of the overhead range that
   SHOULDER-01 removes. Higher angles push toward the painful arc.
2. Dumbbells at the shoulders, palms facing forward or slightly rotated in
   (neutral is kinder if the shoulder complains).
3. Feet flat, ribs down, a small natural arch in the low back — not a big one.
   Shoulder blades set back and down against the bench.

### Execution
1. **Start:** arms extended, dumbbells above the upper chest, elbows soft.
2. **Eccentric:** lower over 2 s, elbows tracking at roughly 45° from the torso.
3. **Bottom:** dumbbells stop **level with the chest** — no deeper while the
   shoulder is constrained. 1 s pause.
4. **Concentric:** press explosively back up and slightly together, without
   clashing the dumbbells.
5. **Top:** elbows extended, shoulder blades still set — do not let them roll
   forward at the top.

### Cues
- "Elbows at 45, not flared to a T."
- "Shoulder blades stay set in the bench."
- "Stop level with your chest."
- "Ribs down — do not arch to press more."

### Breathing
Inhale on the descent, exhale on the press. No maximal Valsalva at these loads.

### Tempo
`2-1-X-0`. The pause at the bottom removes the bounce and keeps the depth honest.

### Range of motion standard
Dumbbells to chest level, full elbow extension at the top, shoulder blades set
throughout, no pain anywhere in the range. **Pain above 2/10 ends the range for
this block.**

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Going below chest level | end-range shoulder extension is the constrained position | set a visual marker; film from the side |
| Elbows flaring to 90° | anterior shoulder stress | "elbows at 45"; reduce load |
| Arching the low back to press heavier | lumbar load, and it turns the incline into a decline | ribs down, feet flat, lighter dumbbells |
| Shoulder blades rolling forward at the top | loses the stable base and stresses the anterior shoulder | "blades in your back pockets, top and bottom" |
| Bench angle creeping up | higher angles move toward the painful arc | 30°, checked each session |

### Risk notes
Chosen because the bench angle and the free-moving dumbbells let the shoulder
stay out of the constrained range. That advantage disappears if the angle creeps
up or the depth creeps down — both are the athlete unconsciously chasing more
stretch. Neutral-grip dumbbells are the fallback if the standard grip provokes
the front of the shoulder.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Machine chest press with a fixed path.
2. Dumbbell floor press — the floor caps the depth automatically.
3. Lighter dumbbells with a longer pause.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. +2 kg per hand on the written trigger.
2. Neutral-to-pronated rotation through the press.
3. Once SHOULDER-01 is lifted: flat dumbbell press, then a return to dips.

### Substitutes
Dumbbell floor press, machine chest press, low-angle cable press, parallette
push-up.

# Design commentary

Why this block looks like this. Walk the eleven steps from
[program-design](../SKILL.md) and check your own draft against the same
reasoning.

**1. Block aim.** One sentence, naming both the skill target *and* the constraint
work. A block that only chased the front lever would leave the shoulder exactly
where it was in six weeks.

**2. Constraints.** Two live constraints, copied verbatim from the screen. They
did real work: they removed handstands, planche and deep dips, which is *why* the
front lever became the primary skill. The constraints did not shrink the block —
they chose it.

**3. Archetype.** Skill, not hypertrophy or strength. Marco has two years of base
and a clean elbow screen, so he passes the skill gate. Straight-arm volume caps
therefore govern the whole block.

**4. Frequency and split.** 3 days, 60 minutes, from the profile — the number
that survives a bad week. Whole-body sessions rather than a split, which is the
right default for bodyweight work. Front lever gets two exposures (Mon, Sat) with
Wednesday between them, so straight-arm work is never on consecutive days.

**5. Volume budget and balance.** Counted from the sessions, not guessed. Skill
work is budgeted as TUT because it runs on the tendon clock; the ring support
hold sits outside the pattern budget because it is constraint work, not push
volume. Push:pull is 9:16 — pull-dominant, as it must be, because bodyweight
training is structurally biased toward pushing and only pulling needs equipment.

All six balance axes are covered, including the two most often missed:
**core in more than one plane** (ab wheel is linear, side plank is lateral) and
**unilateral work** (Bulgarian split squat, single-leg RDL, Copenhagen). Only
vertical push is under its landmark, and only because a constraint deleted the
overhead quadrant — that is written into the budget with the date it comes back.

**6. Exercise selection.** The front lever iso and the tuck raise are the skill.
The weighted pull-up and the neutral-grip pull-up are the bent-arm base under it,
in two different grips on purpose. The straight-arm band pulldown loads the same
joint action at one band step of granularity and doubles as the elbow's early
warning system. The landmine press and the incline dumbbell press exist because a
constraint removed the obvious choices. The ring support hold exists because it
*is* the criterion that lifts SHOULDER-01 — the program contains the exercise
that ends the constraint.

**7. Dose.** Every line has sets, reps or seconds, intensity, tempo and a real
rest number. Hold times are derived from a **tested maximum** and set at 60–75%
of it, not guessed. Tempo defaults to an explosive concentric — slow eccentrics
appear only where there is a reason (the RDLs, the ab wheel, the pulldown).

**8. Paired sets are what make it fit.** Four of the six strength slots are
antagonist pairs — vertical pull with horizontal push, horizontal pull with
vertical push, knee with hip, unilateral knee with unilateral hip. Each
exercise's rest is the other's work, which roughly doubles what an hour holds.
Nothing is paired with a movement that shares its muscles, and the skill work is
never paired: it goes first, fresh, with the long rest.

**9. Autoregulation.** Bad day, short day, amber, red, missed sessions — plus a
rule that exists only because of this athlete's exercise mix: the inner-elbow
rule, with a zero-tolerance threshold, because three exercises in this block load
the same tendon.

**10. Deload and review.** Both scheduled with real dates. The deload names which
lever it pulls and why, and testing happens at the **end** of the deload week so
block 3 is built on post-supercompensation numbers. The review lists its decision
points before the data exists — deciding the rule in advance is what stops a
review becoming a rationalisation.

**11. The checks that were actually run.** Not by hand — by
`npx calicoach check`:

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Budget totals match the sets written in the sessions | pass |
| Skill TUT matches the skill rows | 120 s declared, 120 s written |
| Push : pull ≥ 1:1 in favour of pull | 9 : 16 |
| All six balance axes represented | pass |
| Horizontal pulling present | ring row |
| Unilateral work present | 3 movements |
| Every exercise has a card, with every required subsection | 16 cards |
| Every exercise has a progression row with both triggers | 16 rows |
| Sessions fit the stated 60 minutes | 60 / 54 / 60 |
| Constraints have a substitution, an earn-it-back and a re-test date | both |
| Autoregulation covers bad day, short day, amber, red, missed | pass |
| Dates real, review after the block ends | pass |

## What earlier drafts of this example got wrong

Worth knowing, because these are the failures a program actually makes.

**Draft 1 did not fit in 60 minutes.** Sessions A and C estimated 78 and 95
minutes against a 60-minute budget, and the commentary confidently claimed 62.
Nine working exercises in a session is a sheet of paper, not a session. It also
had progression rows whose abbreviated names did not match the session tables,
and one exercise with no progression row at all.

**Draft 2 fitted the time, and paid for it with balance.** Cutting exercises
until the arithmetic worked left six of ten patterns under their landmark, one
knee exercise, one hinge, no unilateral work at all, and core covered in a single
plane. It passed the time check and would have produced an athlete with a good
front lever and nothing else.

The fix was not a longer session. It was **antagonist pairing** — the same three
hours a week, holding roughly twice the work, because each exercise rests inside
the other's set. Horizontal push went from 3 sets to 6, knee-dominant from 3 to
6, hip hinge from 3 to 6, and the unilateral axis went from absent to three
movements — with sessions still at 60, 54 and 60 minutes.

None of that was visible by reading. The time overrun and the missing balance
axes were both found in a second of arithmetic. That is what the checker is for,
and it is why a program is not finished until it passes one.

## What this example deliberately does not do

- It does not chase two skills. Planche is not in the block, and not only because
  of the wrist — one primary skill per block.
- It does not add "one more thing" because it looked useful. Every exercise maps
  to a budgeted pattern, to a balance axis, to the skill, or to a constraint.
- It does not hide the compromises. The one under-dosed pattern, the constraint
  that blocks a stated goal, the weeks that run five minutes long, and two
  earlier drafts that failed are all written down.
