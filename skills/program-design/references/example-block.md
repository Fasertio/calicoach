# Block 2 — Advanced Tuck Front Lever + Shoulder Rebuild · Marco

> Dates: 2026-09-07 to 2026-10-18 (5 weeks on + 1 deload)
> Days/week: 3 (Mon / Wed / Sat) · Session length: 60 min · Archetype: skill
> Review due: 2026-10-19
> Cards: example-block-cards.md

## Block aim

Take the front lever from a 12-second tuck to a 10-second advanced tuck, and
rebuild pain-free pressing capacity inside the shoulder's available range —
without provoking the right shoulder or the right wrist.

## Goals this block serves

| Goal | Marker at start | Target at review |
|---|---|---|
| Straddle front lever (target Sep 2027) | adv. tuck 5 s max | adv. tuck 10 s max, clean |
| Train pressing pain-free | painful arc 70–110° R, 3/10 | pain-free full abduction, SHOULDER-01 lifted |
| Hold pulling strength | weighted pull-up 5RM +10 kg | +12.5 kg |
| Wrist load tolerance | flat-hand front support 30 s, mild pain | 60 s pain-free, WRIST-01 lifted |

## Active constraints

<!-- copied verbatim from athlete/screening.md, 2026-09-01 -->

- **[SHOULDER-01]** No overhead pressing. No dips below humerus-parallel. No
  behind-neck work.
  Reason: painful arc 70–110° abduction, right shoulder, 3/10 at worst (screen
  item A3, Symptomatic).
  Forbids: overhead-press, overhead-load, behind-neck, dip
  Instead: landmine press, incline dumbbell press within the pain-free range,
  parallette push-ups, ring support holds.
  Earns it back: pain-free full abduction **and** a 20 s ring support hold with
  no front-shoulder pinch.
  Re-test: 2026-10-13. Refer if unchanged.

- **[WRIST-01]** No flat-hand floor loading beyond 30 s per set.
  Reason: wrist extension 65° right (screen item B3, Limited), mild pain in the
  flat-hand front support.
  Forbids: flat-hand-loading
  Instead: parallettes or push-up handles for all pressing and planche-adjacent
  work.
  Earns it back: 60 s pain-free flat-hand front support.
  Re-test: 2026-10-13.

**Consequence for this block:** no handstand work, no planche work, no deep dips.
Vertical pushing is reduced to one arced press — the constraint removes the whole
overhead quadrant. This is also why the block leans on the front lever: it is the
skill goal the constraints leave fully trainable.

**Programming band:** intermediate for pull, beginner-intermediate for push
(constraint-limited), intermediate for legs and core. Band is set by demonstrated
ability, not by years trained.

## Notation

| | |
|---|---|
| `3 e 4` | 3 sets weeks 1–3, 4 sets weeks 4–5 |
| `5 x 8-12"` | 5 sets, hold 8 to 12 seconds |
| `x l` | per side (*per lato*) |
| `RIR 2` | stop the set with 2 reps left in the tank |
| `RPE 8` | hold until the position is *about* to break — never until it breaks |
| `2-0-X-1` | tempo: 2 s down, no pause, **X = explosive up**, 1 s squeeze at the top |
| `A1 / A2` | a **paired set** — do A1, half rest, A2, half rest, repeat |
| `loop M / L` | band assistance, medium / light — we scale the band **down** as you get stronger |

Tempo is written eccentric-pause-concentric-pause. If you read Contreras'
*Bodyweight Strength Training Anatomy*, note that he writes tempo the other way
round (concentric first, three digits) — ours is always the four-digit form
above.

**Vertical push is the one pattern under its landmark**, at 3 sets against 6–12,
because SHOULDER-01 removes the entire overhead quadrant and leaves exactly one
usable arced press. It is not hidden: it rises to the landmark in block 3 the
week the constraint is lifted, and until then the ring support hold is doing the
work that gets us there.

Anti-extension and anti-rotation sit at 3 sets each — maintenance, deliberately,
because the front lever is already loading the anterior core hard twice a week
and that TUT is not in the core budget.

*Top to bottom. A shared letter is a paired set: A1, rest, A2, rest, repeat. A
bare number is unpaired — finish all its sets first.*

**How to run it**

- `1` — Unpaired and fully rested. This is the block's priority, so it goes
  first and it gets the long rest. Your max with the medium band is 16 s, so
  the working band is 8–12 s — that is 60–75% of max, which is where holds
  actually build strength. Quality clock: the timer stops the moment an elbow
  bends or the hips drop. Film it week 1 and week 5, side-on.
- `A1/A2` — Vertical pull against horizontal push. The pairing is what lets
  both patterns fit in one hour. A1 pronated today; Saturday is neutral grip —
  rotating the grip is what keeps your medial elbow quiet under this much
  pulling. Explosive up: intent matters more than speed.
- `A2` — Parallettes, not the floor — **WRIST-01**. Elbows at 45°, hard
  protraction at the top.
- `B1/B2` — Horizontal pull against vertical push. B1, 75", B2, 75", repeat.
  B1 is the only horizontal pull in your week: it is doing the work for your
  scapular retractors and rear delts, so lead with the chest, 1 s squeeze at
  the top, and do not rush it.
- `B2` — Press up **and away** along the bar's arc. Any pain above 2/10 at any
  point in the range ends the range there — tell me.
- `P1` — The highest-value thing you do for that shoulder. Slow, no momentum.

**How to run it**

  most, and unpaired so it gets a clean effort. Shoulders actively down, elbows

  chase the floor. A2's bottom is decided by your spine, not by the dumbbells
  reaching the floor.

  second toe; if one side is more than 2 reps behind, tell me. B2: hips square

- `B3` — Straight line, hips stacked. Match the weaker side's time on both
  sides. Run it after the pair, not inside it.
- `P1` — Knee on the bench, not the ankle. Best groin insurance there is.

## The three rules that override this whole table

1. **Any red pain** (>4/10, sharp, worsening, or still there after 24 h) stops
   that movement immediately, regardless of what the plan says.
2. **No band scale-down, no leverage advance and no load increase** in a week
   where sleep or stress was materially worse.
3. **Straight-arm volume never climbs more than 10% week over week.** Front lever
   TUT plan: 120 → 125 → 135 → 145 → 150 s. If a session overshoots, the next
   one comes down.

# Autoregulation

**Bad day** (slept under 6 h, stress high, heavy DOMS)
Keep the exercises and keep the intensity. Cut the last set of every paired and
prehab exercise. Front lever work at 3 sets instead of 5. No band scale-down, no
load increase. Log it.

**Short day** (30 minutes)
Warm-up in full (10 min), then the skill work, then the **A pair** only, then the
prehab. Never compress by cutting rest below the paired-set interval — cut
exercises instead.

**Amber pain (3–4/10)**
Take the written regression for that exercise, keep it under 4/10 for the rest
of the session, finish the session, and report it in the log with the site and
the number. Two amber sessions on the same structure is treated as red.

**Red pain (>4/10, sharp, worsening, or lasting more than 24 h)**
Stop that movement for the session. Substitute:

| Instead of | Do |
|---|---|
| Front lever iso or tuck raise | ring row at the prescribed sets, and message the coach — straight-arm work is out until we talk |
| Weighted pull-up | bodyweight pull-up at RIR 3 |
| Landmine press | ring row (skip the press entirely) |
| Parallette push-up or incline press | ring row, and report it — that is both your horizontal pushes gone |
| Ring support hold | nothing — report it, this is the constraint we are tracking |
| Goblet squat or Bulgarian | wall sit in the pain-free range |
| Dumbbell RDL or single-leg RDL | glute bridge |
| Ab wheel | dead bug |

**Inner-elbow ache, any level**
The one signal with its own rule. Stop all straight-arm work for the session —
front lever iso, tuck raise and straight-arm pulldown. Run the elbow protocol.
Front lever volume restarts at 60% the following week. Do not wait for it to
reach 3/10.

**Missed sessions**
One missed: continue where you left off. Two or more in a week: repeat the week.
A full week missed: repeat the week at 70% of the sets.

# Exercise cards

## Front Lever Iso — Advanced Tuck, Band-Assisted

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | vertical pull — straight-arm |
| **Type** | isometric · straight-arm · assisted |
| **Primary muscles** | latissimus dorsi, teres major, long head of triceps, lower trapezius |
| **Secondary muscles** | rectus abdominis and obliques (anti-extension), biceps and brachialis (isometric at length), posterior deltoid, glutes |
| **Equipment** | pull-up bar, one resistance band |
| **Difficulty** | intermediate |
| **Skill prerequisite** | 12 s clean tuck front lever, 45 s hollow hold, 5 clean scapular pull-ups, straight-arm tolerance probe passed |

**Why it is here:** this is the block's primary target. The band lets us load the
advanced tuck position at a *fine* granularity — a band step is a fraction of a
leverage step, which is exactly what a straight-arm progression needs.

### Setup
1. Loop the band over the bar and let it hang. Grip the bar just outside
   shoulder width, pronated.
2. Pull to an inverted hang, then place the band under the **hips** — not the
   lower back, which lets the hips break the line and hides the core failure you
   need to see. The band should be snug, not slack.
3. Set the position: arms locked straight, scapulae depressed and retracted,
   pelvis in posterior tilt, knees tucked to the chest, then open the hips until
   the shins are parallel to the floor and the back is flat.

### Execution
1. **Start:** advanced tuck, back parallel to the floor, arms locked, band
   supporting the hips.
2. **Hold:** actively pull down on the bar with straight arms throughout. Ribs
   down, pelvis tucked, shoulders away from the ears.
3. **Breathe:** shallow and continuous. The position must not change as you
   breathe.
4. **Exit:** tuck the knees back in, then lower to a hang under control. Never
   drop out of the position.

### Cues
- "Arms locked — if an elbow bends, the set is over."
- "Shoulders down and back, chest open."
- "Pelvis tucked, ribs down — no arching."
- "Pull the bar down, don't just hang in the shape."

### Breathing
Shallow, continuous, through the nose. Breath-holding is how athletes fake an
extra three seconds; those seconds do not count.

### Tempo
Isometric. Enter over 2 s, hold, exit over 3 s.

### Range of motion standard
The **quality clock**: the timer stops at the first loss of position — an elbow
bending, the hips sagging, the scapulae collapsing, the shoulders shrugging. A
14-second set with 4 collapsed seconds is a 10-second set, and you record 10.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hips sagging below parallel | the core failed, not the lats; the low back is now loaded in extension | more ab wheel and hollow work; go back one band step |
| Elbows bending | it becomes a bent-arm hold and the biceps tendon takes an uncontrolled load | go to a heavier band immediately |
| Body above horizontal | the position drifts 20–30° up and stops being a front lever | film side-on; shoulders, hips and heels in one line, parallel to the floor |
| Scapulae retreating (shrugged) | trains the wrong pattern for the skill and stresses the shoulder | scapular pull-ups before the first set |
| Band under the lower back | changes the leverage and lets the hips break the line | band under the hips, always |
| Chasing seconds | the whole point is quality time | the clock stops at the break, and you record the honest number |

### Risk notes
Straight-arm loading is **elbow-tendon loading**. The tissues at risk are the
distal biceps tendon at the elbow and the long head of the biceps at the
shoulder. Warning sign: a deep ache at the front of the elbow, during the hold or
the next morning.

**Stop signal:** any inner-elbow ache, at any level. Not 3/10 — any. End all
straight-arm work for the session and tell the coach. This is the single most
important stop rule in the block.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Advanced tuck with a **heavier** band.
2. Tuck front lever, no band (the position Marco already owns at 12 s).
3. Tuck front lever with a light band, longer holds.
4. Scapular pull-ups with a 2 s hold plus hollow holds — if the position keeps
   collapsing, the problem is upstream.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Scale the band down one step (medium → light → none).
2. Extend the hold window: 8–12" → 10–14" → 12–15".
3. Advanced tuck unassisted, 5 x 10".
4. One-leg front lever, alternating sides evenly.
5. Straddle front lever.

### Substitutes
Straight-arm band pulldown with a 3 s hold at the bottom, plus front lever tuck
raises — same pattern, no full-body straight-arm exposure. Use these when the
elbow needs a week off.

### References
- `[OG2]` — front lever progression order, straight-arm volume guidance and the
  elbow warnings.
- `[OT]` — why straight-arm work is tendon-limited rather than muscle-limited.
- Video search terms: "advanced tuck front lever band assisted", "front lever
  scapular depression cue", "front lever posterior pelvic tilt".

## Weighted Pull-up

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | vertical pull |
| **Type** | compound · bent-arm · loaded |
| **Primary muscles** | latissimus dorsi, teres major, biceps brachii, brachialis |
| **Secondary muscles** | lower and middle trapezius, rhomboids, posterior deltoid, forearm flexors, core (anti-extension) |
| **Equipment** | pull-up bar, dip belt |
| **Difficulty** | intermediate |
| **Skill prerequisite** | 9+ strict bodyweight pull-ups (met), no active elbow or shoulder constraint on pulling |

**Why it is here:** the bent-arm base under the front lever. A skill block that
abandons heavy pulling produces a temporary skill and a weaker athlete.

### Setup
1. Load the dip belt and let it hang between the legs. Check the chain clears the
   floor at the bottom of the rep.
2. Grip pronated, hands just outside shoulder width, thumbs around the bar.
3. Hang with arms straight, legs together and slightly forward, ribs down,
   glutes engaged — a shallow hollow. This stops the swing before it starts.

### Execution
1. **Start:** active hang — scapulae already depressed, arms straight.
2. **Concentric:** drive the elbows down and slightly back toward the ribs. Lead
   with the chest, not the chin. The load must not swing.
3. **Top:** chin clearly over the bar, chest close to it, elbows pointing down.
   1 s squeeze.
4. **Eccentric:** lower over 2 s to full extension, scapulae releasing last.
5. **Bottom:** full extension. Re-establish the active hang before the next rep.

### Cues
- "Chest to the bar, not chin over the bar."
- "Elbows into your back pockets."
- "Glutes on — don't let the weight swing you."
- "Reach the bottom, every rep."

### Breathing
Inhale at the bottom, exhale through the second half of the pull. Braced trunk
throughout.

### Tempo
`2-0-X-1` — 2 s down, no pause at the bottom beyond re-setting the hang, an
explosive pull, 1 s squeeze at the top. The intent to move fast is what recruits
the high-threshold motor units; the bar may still move slowly under load.

### Range of motion standard
Dead-hang start with elbows fully extended, chin clearly above the bar, no kip,
no leg drive, controlled descent. A rep that breaks this does not count toward
the progression.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Partial range at the bottom | the most protective part of the range goes untrained, and the biceps tendon stays untrained at length | 1 s pause at full extension every rep; drop 2.5 kg |
| Swinging the load | momentum replaces strength and loads the low back | hollow body, 2 s pause between reps |
| Shrugged start | upper-trap dominance and shoulder stress | start every rep from a scapular pull-up |
| Elbows flaring wide | shifts load onto the shoulder joint | "elbows toward the ribs" |
| Load progressing faster than technique | this is how elbows get hurt | +2.5 kg only on the written trigger |

### Risk notes
Loads the biceps distal tendon, the long head of the biceps at the shoulder, and
the common flexor origin at the medial elbow. In this block it sits alongside
front lever work, so the **combined** load on the elbow is the thing to watch.
Any medial elbow pain: cut the load 10%, switch to a neutral grip, and reduce
front lever TUT 30% that week.

Grip is rotated deliberately across the week — pronated on Monday, neutral on
Saturday. Single-grip high-volume pulling is the most reliable way to produce
medial elbow pain in an otherwise healthy athlete.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Bodyweight pull-up at RIR 2.
2. Bodyweight pull-up, tempo `5-0-1-0`.
3. Ring row with feet elevated, weighted.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. +2.5 kg on the written trigger.
2. 4 x 5 instead of 3 x 5.
3. Cluster sets: `3+3+3` with 20" intra-set rest at a heavier load.
4. L-sit pull-up, bodyweight.

### Substitutes
Neutral-grip weighted pull-up if the medial elbow is irritable; lat pulldown at a
matched load if the bar is unavailable.

### References
- `[OG2]` — weighted pulling progression and volume caps.
- `[NSCA]` — loading principles and technique standards.
- Video search terms: "strict weighted pull up form", "dip belt pull up setup".

# Design commentary

Why this block looks like this. Walk the eleven steps from
[program-design](../SKILL.md) and check your own draft against the same
reasoning.

**1. Block aim.** One sentence, naming both the skill target *and* the constraint
work. A block that only chased the front lever would leave the shoulder exactly
where it was in six weeks.

**2. Constraints.** Two live constraints, copied verbatim from the screen. They
did real work: they removed handstands, planche and deep dips, which is *why* the
front lever became the primary skill. The constraints did not shrink the block —
they chose it.

**3. Archetype.** Skill, not hypertrophy or strength. Marco has two years of base
and a clean elbow screen, so he passes the skill gate. Straight-arm volume caps
therefore govern the whole block.

**4. Frequency and split.** 3 days, 60 minutes, from the profile — the number
that survives a bad week. Whole-body sessions rather than a split, which is the
right default for bodyweight work. Front lever gets two exposures (Mon, Sat) with
Wednesday between them, so straight-arm work is never on consecutive days.

**5. Volume budget and balance.** Counted from the sessions, not guessed. Skill
work is budgeted as TUT because it runs on the tendon clock; the ring support
hold sits outside the pattern budget because it is constraint work, not push
volume. Push:pull is 9:16 — pull-dominant, as it must be, because bodyweight
training is structurally biased toward pushing and only pulling needs equipment.

All six balance axes are covered, including the two most often missed:
**core in more than one plane** (ab wheel is linear, side plank is lateral) and
**unilateral work** (Bulgarian split squat, single-leg RDL, Copenhagen). Only
vertical push is under its landmark, and only because a constraint deleted the
overhead quadrant — that is written into the budget with the date it comes back.

**6. Exercise selection.** The front lever iso and the tuck raise are the skill.
The weighted pull-up and the neutral-grip pull-up are the bent-arm base under it,
in two different grips on purpose. The straight-arm band pulldown loads the same
joint action at one band step of granularity and doubles as the elbow's early
warning system. The landmine press and the incline dumbbell press exist because a
constraint removed the obvious choices. The ring support hold exists because it
*is* the criterion that lifts SHOULDER-01 — the program contains the exercise
that ends the constraint.

**7. Dose.** Every line has sets, reps or seconds, intensity, tempo and a real
rest number. Hold times are derived from a **tested maximum** and set at 60–75%
of it, not guessed. Tempo defaults to an explosive concentric — slow eccentrics
appear only where there is a reason (the RDLs, the ab wheel, the pulldown).

**8. Paired sets are what make it fit.** Four of the six strength slots are
antagonist pairs — vertical pull with horizontal push, horizontal pull with
vertical push, knee with hip, unilateral knee with unilateral hip. Each
exercise's rest is the other's work, which roughly doubles what an hour holds.
Nothing is paired with a movement that shares its muscles, and the skill work is
never paired: it goes first, fresh, with the long rest.

**9. Autoregulation.** Bad day, short day, amber, red, missed sessions — plus a
rule that exists only because of this athlete's exercise mix: the inner-elbow
rule, with a zero-tolerance threshold, because three exercises in this block load
the same tendon.

**10. Deload and review.** Both scheduled with real dates. The deload names which
lever it pulls and why, and testing happens at the **end** of the deload week so
block 3 is built on post-supercompensation numbers. The review lists its decision
points before the data exists — deciding the rule in advance is what stops a
review becoming a rationalisation.

**11. The checks that were actually run.** Not by hand — by
`npx calicoach check`:

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Budget totals match the sets written in the sessions | pass |
| Skill TUT matches the skill rows | 120 s declared, 120 s written |
| Push : pull ≥ 1:1 in favour of pull | 9 : 16 |
| All six balance axes represented | pass |
| Horizontal pulling present | ring row |
| Unilateral work present | 3 movements |
| Every exercise has a card, with every required subsection | 16 cards |
| Every exercise has a progression row with both triggers | 16 rows |
| Sessions fit the stated 60 minutes | 60 / 54 / 60 |
| Constraints have a substitution, an earn-it-back and a re-test date | both |
| Autoregulation covers bad day, short day, amber, red, missed | pass |
| Dates real, review after the block ends | pass |
| Citation keys defined | 7 keys |

## What earlier drafts of this example got wrong

Worth knowing, because these are the failures a program actually makes.

**Draft 1 did not fit in 60 minutes.** Sessions A and C estimated 78 and 95
minutes against a 60-minute budget, and the commentary confidently claimed 62.
Nine working exercises in a session is a sheet of paper, not a session. It also
had progression rows whose abbreviated names did not match the session tables,
and one exercise with no progression row at all.

**Draft 2 fitted the time, and paid for it with balance.** Cutting exercises
until the arithmetic worked left six of ten patterns under their landmark, one
knee exercise, one hinge, no unilateral work at all, and core covered in a single
plane. It passed the time check and would have produced an athlete with a good
front lever and nothing else.

The fix was not a longer session. It was **antagonist pairing** — the same three
hours a week, holding roughly twice the work, because each exercise rests inside
the other's set. Horizontal push went from 3 sets to 6, knee-dominant from 3 to
6, hip hinge from 3 to 6, and the unilateral axis went from absent to three
movements — with sessions still at 60, 54 and 60 minutes.

None of that was visible by reading. The time overrun and the missing balance
axes were both found in a second of arithmetic. That is what the checker is for,
and it is why a program is not finished until it passes one.

## What this example deliberately does not do

- It does not chase two skills. Planche is not in the block, and not only because
  of the wrist — one primary skill per block.
- It does not add "one more thing" because it looked useful. Every exercise maps
  to a budgeted pattern, to a balance axis, to the skill, or to a constraint.
- It does not hide the compromises. The one under-dosed pattern, the constraint
  that blocks a stated goal, the weeks that run five minutes long, and two
  earlier drafts that failed are all written down.
