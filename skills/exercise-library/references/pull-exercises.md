# Pulling Catalogue

Vertical pull, horizontal pull, straight-arm pull, and their weight-room
counterparts. Cards marked **[full]** are written out completely as models —
reproduce that depth for every exercise you prescribe, including the compact
entries below.

Pull volume should equal or exceed push volume in every program.

## Scapular Pull-up (trazione scapolare) **[full]**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | vertical pull — scapular |
| **Type** | isolation · straight-arm · dynamic-into-isometric |
| **Primary muscles** | lower trapezius, latissimus dorsi (as a depressor), rhomboids |
| **Secondary muscles** | teres major, serratus anterior (eccentrically), forearm flexors (grip) |
| **Equipment** | pull-up bar or rings |
| **Difficulty** | beginner |
| **Skill prerequisite** | 20 s pain-free dead hang |

**Why it is here:** it teaches the athlete to move the shoulder blade
independently of the elbow. Without this, every pull-up is a shrug and every
straight-arm skill is unsafe.

### Setup
1. Hang from the bar, hands just outside shoulder width, thumbs around the bar.
2. Arms completely straight, elbows locked. Legs together, slightly in front of
   the body, toes pointed. Ribs down, glutes lightly squeezed.
3. Let the shoulders rise toward the ears — this is the passive start position.

### Execution
1. **Start:** passive hang, shoulders at the ears, arms straight.
2. **Concentric:** without bending the elbows at all, pull the shoulder blades
   down and slightly together. The body rises 3–5 cm. The chest lifts slightly;
   the head stays neutral.
3. **Top:** hold 2 s. Shoulders are actively away from the ears, chest open, arms
   still locked straight.
4. **Eccentric:** lower under control over 3 s back into the passive hang.
   Control the descent — do not drop into it.

### Cues
- "Elbows stay locked — only the shoulder blades move."
- "Pull your shoulders away from your ears."
- "Lift your chest without bending your arms."
- "Ribs down, don't arch."

### Breathing
Exhale on the pull, inhale on the descent. Do not hold the breath.

### Tempo
`3-0-1-2` — 3 s down, 1 s up, 2 s hold at the top. Slow is the point.

### Range of motion standard
The rep counts if the elbows stay locked throughout and the body moves at least
3 cm. Any elbow bend voids the rep.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Elbows bend | it becomes a partial pull-up and the scapular work disappears | film side-on; hold a 2 s pause at the top and check the elbow angle |
| Shrugging up instead of pulling down | trains upper trap dominance, the opposite of the goal | cue "away from the ears", reduce to 5 reps and go slower |
| Ribs flare / low back arches | loses the trunk position needed for lever work | squeeze glutes, tuck the ribs, keep the legs slightly forward |
| Swinging | momentum replaces the muscle | start from a fully still hang; pause 3 s before the first rep |
| Chin poking forward | neck compensation | "long neck, eyes on the bar" |

### Risk notes
Loads the grip and the shoulder in a passive-to-active transition. Athletes with
a painful arc or a history of instability should start with feet lightly on the
floor to reduce load. Stop at any front-of-shoulder pinch. Grip fatigue should
never be the reason the set ends — use straps only if the grip is the limiter and
the shoulder work is the point.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Standing scapular depression against a low bar, feet on the floor taking 50%
   of bodyweight.
2. Band-assisted hang with a foot in the band.
3. Scapular push-up on the floor (same skill, unloaded, prone).

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Add a 3 s hold at the top.
2. Scapular pull-up on rings (adds stability demand).
3. One-arm scapular pull-up with the free hand on the wrist.
4. Weighted scapular pull-up (vest or belt), 5–8 reps.

### Substitutes
Straight-arm cable pulldown with an emphasis on scapular depression; prone Y and
T raises for athletes who cannot hang.

### References
- Overcoming Gravity, Steven Low — scapular strength chapter and the "shoulder
  girdle" progressions.
- Gymnastics Strength / GymnasticBodies methodology — scapular strength as the
  prerequisite to straight-arm work.
- Video search terms: "scapular pull-up tutorial", "scapular retraction hang".

## Pull-up (trazione alla sbarra) **[full]**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | vertical pull |
| **Type** | compound · bent-arm |
| **Primary muscles** | latissimus dorsi, teres major, biceps brachii, brachialis |
| **Secondary muscles** | lower/middle trapezius, rhomboids, posterior deltoid, forearm flexors, core (anti-extension) |
| **Equipment** | pull-up bar |
| **Difficulty** | intermediate |
| **Skill prerequisite** | 5 clean scapular pull-ups, 30 s dead hang, no active shoulder or elbow constraint |

**Why it is here:** the backbone of every calisthenics program and the strength
base under the muscle-up, front lever and one-arm work.

### Setup
1. Grip pronated (palms away), hands just outside shoulder width. Thumbs around
   the bar unless grip pathology dictates otherwise.
2. Hang with arms straight. Legs together and slightly forward of the hips, or
   knees bent and crossed — pick one and keep it consistent.
3. Ribs down, glutes and quads lightly engaged, body in a shallow hollow. This
   stops the swing before it starts.

### Execution
1. **Start:** active hang — scapulae already depressed (the scapular pull-up
   position), arms straight.
2. **Concentric:** drive the elbows down and slightly back toward the ribs. Lead
   with the chest, not the chin. The body stays hollow; the legs do not kick.
3. **Top:** chin clearly over the bar, chest close to the bar, elbows pointing
   down. Do not crane the neck to get there.
4. **Eccentric:** lower under control over 2–3 s, arms reaching full extension,
   scapulae releasing last.
5. **Bottom:** full extension. Re-establish the active hang before the next rep.

### Cues
- "Chest to the bar, not chin over the bar."
- "Elbows into your back pockets."
- "Squeeze your glutes — no banana body."
- "Reach the bottom, every rep."

### Breathing
Inhale at the bottom, exhale through the second half of the pull. Braced trunk
throughout.

### Tempo
`2-0-1-1` for strength. `3-0-1-0` when building tissue tolerance or fixing
technique. Never bounce out of the bottom.

### Range of motion standard
Dead-hang start with elbows fully extended; chin clearly above the bar at the
top; no kip, no leg drive, controlled descent. Anything less does not count
toward progression.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Partial range at the bottom | the hardest and most protective part of the range is skipped; biceps tendon stays untrained at length | pause 1 s at full extension every rep; reduce reps by 30% |
| Kipping / leg swing | momentum replaces strength and loads the low back | hollow body, legs slightly forward, pause 2 s between reps |
| Chin craning | neck strain, hides an incomplete rep | "chest to the bar", film front-on |
| Shrugged shoulders at the start | upper trap dominance, shoulder impingement risk | start every rep from a scapular pull-up |
| Elbows flaring wide | shifts load onto the shoulder joint | "elbows toward the ribs" |
| Dropping out of the top | eccentric is where most adaptation is; also a fall risk | 3 s descents for two weeks |

### Risk notes
Loads the biceps distal tendon, the long head of biceps at the shoulder, and the
common flexor origin at the medial elbow. Medial elbow pain, a painful arc, or
front-of-shoulder pinch all mean stop and regress. Athletes with a wrist
constraint should use rings or a neutral-grip bar so the forearm can rotate
freely. High weekly pull-up volume with a pronated grip is the most common cause
of medial elbow pain in this sport — rotate grips across the week.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Ring row / inverted row at a shallow angle.
2. Ring row with feet elevated.
3. Band-assisted pull-up (thickest band → thinner over weeks).
4. Eccentric-only pull-up: jump or step to the top, lower over 5 s. 3–5 reps.
5. Partial-range pull-up from the top half, extending the range weekly.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Add reps to 3x10 with clean form.
2. Tempo pull-up: 5 s eccentric, 2 s pause at the top.
3. Weighted pull-up (belt or vest), +2.5% bodyweight steps.
4. L-sit pull-up.
5. Archer pull-up → one-arm progressions (see
   [skill-progressions](../../skill-progressions/SKILL.md)).

### Substitutes
Lat pulldown (load-matched, useful when bodyweight reps are not yet possible);
ring pull-up for wrist and elbow comfort; neutral-grip pull-up when the medial
elbow is irritable.

### References
- Overcoming Gravity, Steven Low — pulling progressions and volume guidance.
- NSCA Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning — technique standards and
  loading principles.
- ExRx.net exercise directory — muscle involvement per variation.
- Video search terms: "strict pull-up form", "scapular retraction pull-up cue".

## Ring Row / Inverted Row (rematore agli anelli) **[full]**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | horizontal pull |
| **Type** | compound · bent-arm |
| **Primary muscles** | middle trapezius, rhomboids, latissimus dorsi, posterior deltoid |
| **Secondary muscles** | biceps brachii, brachialis, forearm flexors, glutes and hamstrings (isometric), spinal erectors |
| **Equipment** | rings, a low bar, or a suspension trainer |
| **Difficulty** | beginner (angle-adjustable) |
| **Skill prerequisite** | none — this is the entry point for pulling |

**Why it is here:** the single most under-programmed movement in calisthenics.
Horizontal pulling builds the scapular retractors that keep the shoulder healthy
under all the pressing and vertical pulling.

### Setup
1. Set the rings at roughly hip-to-chest height. Grip neutral (palms facing each
   other) or pronated.
2. Walk the feet forward until the body is at the chosen angle — the more
   horizontal the body, the harder.
3. Body in one straight line: heels, hips, shoulders, ears. Glutes squeezed, ribs
   down. Arms straight, shoulders allowed to protract slightly at the bottom.

### Execution
1. **Start:** hanging at full arm extension, shoulder blades released forward.
2. **Initiate:** retract the shoulder blades first — the chest lifts a few
   centimetres before the elbows bend.
3. **Concentric:** pull the rings to the lower ribs / sternum, elbows tracking at
   roughly 30–45° from the torso, not flared to 90°.
4. **Top:** chest touches or nearly touches the rings, shoulder blades fully
   retracted and depressed, body still one line.
5. **Eccentric:** lower over 2–3 s to full extension, letting the scapulae
   protract at the very end.

### Cues
- "Lead with the chest, not the hands."
- "Shoulder blades into your back pockets, then bend the elbows."
- "One straight line — squeeze your glutes."
- "Reach at the bottom."

### Breathing
Exhale on the pull, inhale on the descent.

### Tempo
`3-0-1-1`. The 1 s squeeze at the top is where the retractor work happens.

### Range of motion standard
Full arm extension with scapular protraction at the bottom; chest to the rings
with retraction at the top; body straight throughout. Hips sagging voids the rep.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hips sagging | turns it into an arm exercise and loads the low back | "glutes on", reduce the angle, 3 s hold at the top of a plank first |
| Elbows flared to 90° | shifts load to the shoulder joint and away from the back | "elbows at 45 degrees", cue the ribs |
| Pulling with the arms only | scapular retractors stay untrained | initiate with the shoulder blades, add a 2 s squeeze |
| Short range at the bottom | the protraction range is the useful part | rings lower, ensure full extension |
| Neck jutting forward | compensation for insufficient range | "long neck, chin tucked" |

### Risk notes
Very low risk — one of the safest loaded pulls and usually the correct
substitution when a shoulder constraint blocks vertical pulling. Ensure ring
straps and the anchor are checked before every set. Athletes with elbow
symptoms should use a neutral grip.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Standing row against a band.
2. Ring row with the body nearly vertical (rings high, feet close).
3. Ring row with knees bent, feet flat on the floor.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Lower the rings / walk the feet forward toward horizontal.
2. Feet elevated on a box, body below the rings.
3. Add a weight vest.
4. Tempo: 5 s eccentric, 3 s hold at the top.
5. Archer row → one-arm ring row.
6. Front lever row (see skill-progressions).

### Substitutes
Barbell row, chest-supported dumbbell row, seated cable row, TRX row, table row
(at home, under a sturdy table).

### References
- Overcoming Gravity, Steven Low — row progressions.
- NSCA Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning — rowing mechanics.
- Video search terms: "inverted row form", "ring row scapular retraction".

## Straight-Arm Lat Pulldown (pulldown a braccia tese) **[full]**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | vertical pull — straight-arm |
| **Type** | isolation · straight-arm |
| **Primary muscles** | latissimus dorsi, teres major, long head of triceps (as a shoulder extensor) |
| **Secondary muscles** | posterior deltoid, lower trapezius, core (anti-extension) |
| **Equipment** | cable machine, or a band anchored high |
| **Difficulty** | beginner |
| **Skill prerequisite** | none; mandatory accessory for front lever work |

**Why it is here:** the highest-value accessory for the front lever and for
straight-arm strength generally — it trains shoulder extension with a locked
elbow, at a load you can actually progress in small steps.

### Setup
1. Stand facing a high cable or anchored band, feet hip width, one foot slightly
   back for balance.
2. Grip a straight bar or rope with straight arms, hands shoulder width, arms at
   roughly 45° overhead.
3. Hinge slightly at the hips, ribs down, brace the trunk. Elbows locked — they
   stay locked for the whole set.

### Execution
1. **Start:** arms straight overhead-forward, lats stretched, scapulae slightly
   elevated.
2. **Concentric:** drive the straight arms down in an arc toward the thighs,
   thinking about pulling the humerus back and down rather than about the hands.
3. **End:** hands arrive at or just past the thighs, lats fully shortened,
   shoulder blades depressed. Do not lean back to get further.
4. **Eccentric:** return along the same arc over 3 s, letting the lats lengthen
   fully without shrugging.

### Cues
- "Elbows locked the whole time."
- "Push the bar away in an arc, don't pull it down."
- "Ribs down — no leaning back."
- "Feel it in the armpit, not the triceps."

### Breathing
Exhale on the pull-down, inhale on the return.

### Tempo
`3-0-1-1`. Add a 2 s hold at the bottom when building front lever tolerance.

### Range of motion standard
Elbows locked throughout; full overhead stretch at the top; hands reach the
thighs at the bottom without trunk lean.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Elbows bending | becomes a triceps pushdown, the straight-arm stimulus is lost | reduce load 30%, film side-on |
| Leaning back at the bottom | the trunk does the work, not the lats | brace, stand closer, reduce load |
| Shrugging on the return | trains the wrong pattern for lever work | slow the eccentric, cue "shoulders down" |
| Load too heavy | this is a connective-tissue exercise; heavy loads with a locked elbow spike biceps-tendon stress | keep it in the 10–15 rep range at RIR 2–3 |

### Risk notes
Straight-arm loading is elbow-tendon loading. Progress in small increments and
never take it to failure. Any inner-elbow ache here is an early warning that
front lever or planche volume needs to come down — treat it as a leading
indicator.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Band straight-arm pulldown with a light band.
2. Prone Y-to-I raises on the floor, bodyweight.
3. Straight-arm pulldown seated, to reduce trunk demand.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Add load in small steps (2.5 kg).
2. Add a 3 s isometric hold at the bottom.
3. Kneeling single-arm straight-arm pulldown.
4. Progress to tuck front lever pulls.

### Substitutes
Band pulldown at home; dumbbell straight-arm pullover on a bench (watch the
shoulder-extension end range if there is a shoulder constraint).

### References
- Overcoming Gravity, Steven Low — straight-arm scapular strength.
- ExRx.net — straight-arm pulldown muscle involvement.
- Video search terms: "straight arm pulldown for front lever", "lat pullover
  straight arm technique".

## Compact catalogue — vertical pull

| Exercise | Type | Notes for prescription |
|---|---|---|
| **Chin-up** (supinated) | compound bent-arm | more biceps, often easier; useful when the medial elbow tolerates supination better than pronation, worse when it does not. Same standard as the pull-up. |
| **Neutral-grip pull-up** | compound bent-arm | usually the most joint-friendly grip; first choice when elbows or wrists are irritable |
| **Ring pull-up** | compound bent-arm | rings rotate freely — kindest to elbows and wrists; adds stability demand, reduce reps ~20% |
| **Weighted pull-up** | compound bent-arm, loaded | only above 10 clean bodyweight reps; progress ~2.5% BW per step; belt over vest for heavy work |
| **L-sit pull-up** | compound + compression | adds anterior core demand; stop when the legs drop |
| **Archer pull-up** | unilateral bias | the straight arm must stay straight; entry to one-arm work |
| **Commando / mixed-grip pull-up** | asymmetric | rotational demand on the trunk; alternate sides evenly |
| **Lat pulldown** | machine vertical pull | the correct tool when bodyweight pull-ups are not yet possible — it allows a real progressive load |
| **Assisted pull-up machine** | machine | fine, but band or eccentric work transfers better to the bar |
| **Eccentric pull-up** | bent-arm, eccentric | 5 s descents, 3–5 reps; the fastest route to a first pull-up alongside rows |
| **Kipping pull-up** | ballistic | not programmed unless the athlete's sport requires it; high shoulder demand |

## Compact catalogue — horizontal pull

| Exercise | Type | Notes for prescription |
|---|---|---|
| **Barbell row** | compound, loaded | strong hinge and erector demand; contraindicated with a lumbar flexion constraint |
| **Chest-supported row** (bench or machine) | compound, loaded | removes the low-back cost — first choice when the hinge is limited or fatigued |
| **Single-arm dumbbell row** | unilateral | excellent for asymmetry; brace hard, do not rotate the trunk |
| **Seated cable row** | compound, loaded | easy to load in small steps, kind to the low back |
| **T-bar / landmine row** | compound, loaded | neutral grip, good middle ground |
| **Face pull** | isolation, external rotation | prehab staple — 2–3 sets, 12–20 reps, most sessions |
| **Band pull-apart** | isolation | high-frequency scapular work, warm-up and prehab |
| **Prone Y / T / W raises** | isolation | lower and middle trapezius, useful with shoulder constraints |
| **Ring archer row / one-arm ring row** | unilateral bodyweight | progression toward one-arm pulling |
| **Front lever row** | straight-to-bent arm | advanced; only after a solid tuck lever |
| **Inverted row on a bar** | compound bodyweight | when rings are unavailable; feet elevated to increase load |

## Compact catalogue — straight-arm pull

| Exercise | Notes |
|---|---|

## Grip rotation policy

Program at least two different grips across the training week for vertical
pulling (e.g. pronated on day 1, neutral on day 3). Single-grip, high-volume
pulling is the most reliable way to produce medial elbow pain in an otherwise
healthy athlete.
