# Legs Catalogue

Knee-dominant, hip-dominant (hinge), unilateral and calf work. Bodyweight and
weight-room options side by side.

Legs are not optional in a calisthenics program. Minimum weekly dose for any
athlete: **2 knee-dominant + 1 hinge pattern**, plus calf work if the athlete
jumps, runs or is chasing a handstand press.

---

## Goblet Squat / Bodyweight Squat **[full]**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | knee-dominant (squat) |
| **Type** | compound · bilateral |
| **Primary muscles** | quadriceps, gluteus maximus |
| **Secondary muscles** | adductor magnus, hamstrings (stabilising), spinal erectors, core, calves |
| **Equipment** | none, or a dumbbell/kettlebell held at the chest |
| **Difficulty** | beginner |
| **Skill prerequisite** | knee-to-wall dorsiflexion ≥ 8 cm, or heel elevation |

**Why it is here:** the squat pattern is the base for pistols, jumping and
general lower-body capacity, and the goblet position self-corrects most trunk
faults.

### Setup
1. Feet between hip and shoulder width, toes turned out 10–30° — whatever lets
   the knees track over the toes.
2. Weight distributed across the whole foot: big toe, little toe, heel. Grip the
   floor slightly.
3. If holding a weight, hold it at the sternum, elbows in, ribs down. Brace the
   trunk as if about to be pushed.

### Execution
1. **Start:** standing tall, braced, knees soft.
2. **Eccentric:** break at the hips and knees together, sitting down between the
   feet rather than back behind them. Knees travel forward over the toes — this
   is normal and safe for a healthy knee.
3. **Bottom:** hip crease below the knee if the athlete can hold a neutral spine
   there; otherwise stop at the deepest neutral-spine, heels-down position. Brief
   pause.
4. **Concentric:** drive the floor away, hips and chest rising together. Do not
   let the hips shoot up first.
5. **Top:** stand tall, glutes squeezed, no hyperextension of the low back.

### Cues
- "Sit down between your feet, not back."
- "Knees out over your second toe."
- "Spread the floor with your feet."
- "Chest and hips rise together."

### Breathing
Inhale and brace at the top, hold through the descent and the first half of the
ascent, exhale at the top. Avoid a maximal Valsalva if blood pressure is a
concern.

### Tempo
`3-1-1-0` for learning, `2-0-1-0` for loaded work.

### Range of motion standard
The deepest depth at which the spine stays neutral and the heels stay down. Do
not force depth with a butt wink; do not accept a quarter squat.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Heels lifting | ankle dorsiflexion limit | elevate heels 2 cm, add ankle mobility work, re-test in 4 weeks |
| Knees collapsing inward | hip abductor weakness or foot collapse | "knees out", band around the knees, add glute-medius work |
| Butt wink at the bottom | loaded lumbar flexion at end range | stop above the wink, widen the stance, hip mobility work |
| Hips shoot up first | quads giving up, turning it into a good morning | pause squats, reduce load |
| Chest collapsing forward | trunk or thoracic weakness | goblet position, front-loaded variations |
| Asymmetric shift to one side | old injury or asymmetry | film front-on; unilateral work to close the gap |

### Risk notes
With an anterior knee-pain constraint, program only the pain-free depth and add
isometric quad work. With a lumbar-flexion constraint, keep the trunk more
upright (goblet or front-loaded) and stop above the wink. Depth is a
prescription, not a moral achievement.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Box squat to a high box.
2. Assisted squat holding a rack or TRX.
3. Heel-elevated squat.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Add load (goblet → front squat → back squat).
2. Tempo squat: 5 s down, 2 s pause.
3. Bulgarian split squat.
4. Pistol squat progressions.
5. Jump squat (only with a clean knee screen).

### Substitutes
Leg press or hack squat when balance, back or shoulder constraints interfere;
Spanish squat and wall sit when only isometric quad loading is tolerated.

### References
- NSCA Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning — squat mechanics and
  loading.
- Starting Strength, Mark Rippetoe — detailed barbell squat teaching progression
  (note: its depth and stance prescriptions are one school, not the only one).
- Video search terms: "goblet squat technique", "ankle mobility squat depth".

## Romanian Deadlift (RDL) **[full]**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | hip hinge |
| **Type** | compound · bilateral · eccentric-biased |
| **Primary muscles** | hamstrings (biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus), gluteus maximus |
| **Secondary muscles** | spinal erectors, latissimus dorsi (bar control), grip, adductor magnus |
| **Equipment** | barbell, dumbbells, kettlebell, or a heavy backpack |
| **Difficulty** | beginner–intermediate |
| **Skill prerequisite** | passes the dowel hinge screen; no active lumbar constraint |

**Why it is here:** the posterior chain is what keeps the front lever body line
and the low back healthy under everything else.

### Setup
1. Stand with feet hip width, load held at arm's length in front of the thighs.
2. Knees soft — unlocked but not bent. Shoulders back and down, lats engaged so
   the bar stays against the legs.
3. Neutral spine: dowel test position — head, upper back and sacrum in line.

### Execution
1. **Start:** standing tall, braced, load against the thighs.
2. **Eccentric:** push the hips straight back, letting the torso descend. The bar
   or dumbbells slide down the thighs, staying in contact or within a couple of
   centimetres. Shins stay nearly vertical.
3. **Bottom:** stop at the point where the hamstrings are at full tension **and
   the spine is still neutral** — for most people mid-shin to just below the
   knee. Depth is determined by spinal position, never by the floor.
4. **Concentric:** drive the hips forward, squeezing the glutes, standing tall.
5. **Top:** hips fully extended, ribs down, no lumbar hyperextension.

### Cues
- "Push your hips back to the wall behind you."
- "Bar stays on your legs."
- "Long spine, chest proud."
- "Feel the hamstrings load — stop when your back would round."

### Breathing
Inhale and brace at the top, hold through the descent, exhale at the top of the
ascent.

### Tempo
`3-1-1-0`. Slow eccentrics are the point of the exercise.

### Range of motion standard
The lowest position with a demonstrably neutral spine, hips loaded, shins near
vertical. Any rounding ends the range.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Rounding the low back | loaded lumbar flexion — the highest-risk fault in the whole catalogue | reduce range, use a dowel, film side-on, reduce load 30% |
| Squatting instead of hinging | quads take over, hamstrings untrained | "hips back, not down"; wall-touch drill |
| Bar drifting away from the legs | large lever arm on the low back | "lats on, bar on the legs" |
| Hyperextending at the top | lumbar stress at lockout | "ribs down, squeeze glutes, stop at tall" |
| Chasing the floor | depth beyond the athlete's hamstring length | depth is decided by spinal position |

### Risk notes
With a lumbar-flexion constraint this movement is programmed from a shortened
range (rack pull, blocks, or a high box) and extended as tolerance improves. Any
radiating leg symptoms mean stop and refer.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Dowel hinge drill, bodyweight.
2. Hip hinge to a wall touch (butt taps the wall behind).
3. Kettlebell RDL from a short range.
4. Rack pull / block pull at knee height.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Add load progressively (5 kg steps).
2. Single-leg RDL.
3. Deficit RDL (standing on a plate) — only with a clean lumbar screen.
4. Nordic hamstring curl eccentrics as a companion.

### Substitutes
Hip thrust, back extension to neutral, cable pull-through, seated leg curl,
single-leg RDL with a dumbbell.

### References
- NSCA Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning — hinge mechanics.
- Video search terms: "romanian deadlift technique neutral spine", "hip hinge
  dowel drill".

## Compact catalogue — knee-dominant

| Exercise | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Bodyweight squat** | bodyweight | high-rep base; the entry point for everyone |
| **Split squat** | unilateral | stable, easy to load, low balance demand |
| **Bulgarian split squat** | unilateral | huge quad and glute stimulus; watch the front knee angle |
| **Step-up** | unilateral | knee-friendly, easy to grade by box height |
| **Reverse lunge** | unilateral | kinder to the knee than a forward lunge |
| **Walking lunge** | unilateral | conditioning cost; watch the knee valgus under fatigue |
| **Cossack squat** | unilateral, frontal plane | adductor mobility and strength; contraindicated with a groin constraint |
| **Pistol squat progressions** | unilateral bodyweight | see [skill-progressions](../../skill-progressions/SKILL.md) |
| **Shrimp squat** | unilateral bodyweight | quad-dominant alternative to the pistol; needs knee tolerance |
| **Sissy squat** | knee-dominant isolation | very high patellofemoral load — never with a knee constraint |
| **Wall sit / Spanish squat** | isometric | the first-line loading tool for irritable knees and patellar tendon |
| **Back / front squat** | loaded | the primary weight-room loading tools |
| **Leg press** | machine | when balance, back or shoulder constraints block squatting |
| **Hack squat** | machine | quad-biased, easy to load, low spinal cost |
| **Leg extension** | isolation | quad volume with no spinal load; useful in knee rehab within pain-free range |
| **Jump squat / box jump** | plyometric | only with a clean knee screen; low volume, quality reps, land quietly |

## Compact catalogue — hip-dominant

| Exercise | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Hip thrust** | loaded | highest glute activation with minimal spinal load |
| **Glute bridge** | bodyweight | entry point; useful with lumbar constraints |
| **Single-leg RDL** | unilateral | balance + hamstring; excellent for asymmetry |
| **Trap-bar deadlift** | loaded | less lumbar shear than a conventional deadlift; good default |
| **Conventional deadlift** | loaded | highest systemic fatigue; only with a clean hinge screen |
| **Back extension (45° or GHD)** | bodyweight/loaded | erector and hamstring; stop at neutral, do not hyperextend |
| **Nordic hamstring curl** | eccentric | outstanding hamstring protection; start at 2x3, it is very demanding |
| **Cable pull-through** | loaded | teaches the hinge with light load and no spinal compression |
| **Seated / lying leg curl** | machine isolation | knee-flexion hamstring function, complements the RDL |
| **Kettlebell swing** | ballistic hinge | conditioning + hinge power; requires a clean hinge pattern first |

## Compact catalogue — calf, foot and ankle

| Exercise | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Standing calf raise** | gastrocnemius; full range, 2 s at the top, 3 s down |
| **Seated calf raise** | soleus; essential for Achilles tolerance and knee-to-wall range |
| **Single-leg calf raise** | asymmetry and Achilles loading; 3x12–20 |
| **Tibialis raise** | anterior shin, protects against shin splints, cheap and effective |
| **Ankle dorsiflexion mobilisation (knee-to-wall with a band)** | the direct fix for squat depth limited by ankles |
| **Short foot / toe splay drills** | arch control, useful for knee valgus and balance |

## Compact catalogue — hip prehab

| Exercise | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Copenhagen plank progression** | adductor strength; the best groin-strain prevention there is; start with a short lever |
| **Side-lying hip abduction / clamshell** | glute medius; directly addresses knee valgus |
| **Banded lateral walk** | glute medius under load |
| **90/90 hip transitions** | hip internal/external rotation range |
| **Hip airplane** | single-leg rotational control; excellent for pistol and single-leg work |
| **Couch stretch / hip flexor work** | for athletes who sit all day; after training, not before |
