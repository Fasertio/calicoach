# Pushing Catalogue

Horizontal push, vertical push, straight-arm push, and their weight-room
counterparts. Cards marked **[full]** are the depth standard.

Push volume never exceeds pull volume in a program.

---

## Push-up (piegamenti sulle braccia) **[full]**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | horizontal push |
| **Type** | compound · bent-arm · closed chain |
| **Primary muscles** | pectoralis major (sternal), anterior deltoid, triceps brachii |
| **Secondary muscles** | serratus anterior, core (anti-extension), glutes and quadriceps (isometric), wrist extensors |
| **Equipment** | floor; parallettes or push-up handles if the wrist is limited |
| **Difficulty** | beginner (angle-adjustable) |
| **Skill prerequisite** | 30 s front plank with a flat low back |

**Why it is here:** the foundational push and the diagnostic for trunk control.
A push-up that fails is usually a core failure, not a chest failure.

### Setup
1. Hands under the shoulders or ~2 cm wider, fingers pointing forward or turned
   out 10–15°. Spread the fingers and grip the floor.
2. Arms straight, elbows soft-locked (not hyperextended). Shoulder blades
   protracted — actively push the floor away.
3. Feet together or hip width. Squeeze the glutes, tuck the ribs down, brace the
   trunk so the body is a straight line from heels to head, in a slight hollow.

### Execution
1. **Start:** top of the push-up, body straight, scapulae protracted.
2. **Eccentric:** lower over 2–3 s, elbows tracking back at roughly 45° from the
   torso — not flared to 90°, not pinned to the ribs.
3. **Bottom:** chest arrives at roughly fist height from the floor; upper arms at
   about torso level. The body stays one line — hips do not sag or pike.
4. **Concentric:** push the floor away, elbows extending, shoulder blades
   protracting at the very top.
5. **Top:** full lockout with active protraction (a slight rounding of the upper
   back is correct here — this is serratus work, not a fault).

### Cues
- "Screw your hands into the floor" (creates external rotation torque at the
  shoulder).
- "Elbows at 45 degrees, arrow shape not T shape."
- "Squeeze your glutes, tuck your ribs."
- "Push the floor away at the top."

### Breathing
Inhale on the descent, exhale on the press. Trunk stays braced; do not let the
ribs flare on the inhale.

### Tempo
`3-1-1-1`. The 1 s pause at the bottom removes the bounce and exposes the true
strength level.

### Range of motion standard
Chest to fist height, full lockout at the top, body straight throughout. Hips
sagging, hips piking, or a partial lockout voids the rep.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hips sagging | lumbar extension under load; also masks weak pressing | glutes on, ribs down; regress to incline push-ups |
| Hips piking | shortens the lever, the athlete is avoiding the hard part | film side-on; regress the angle rather than cheat the line |
| Elbows flared to 90° | anterior shoulder stress, common cause of front-shoulder pain | "elbows to 45", narrow the hands slightly |
| Head dropping first | the athlete is measuring depth by the chin | "chest touches, not chin" |
| No protraction at the top | serratus stays untrained, contributes to scapular dyskinesis | add a 1 s "push away" at the top of every rep |
| Wrist pain | wrist extension range or tolerance is the limiter, not the chest | parallettes, fists, or push-up handles + wrist prep protocol |

### Risk notes
Loads the anterior shoulder and the wrist in extension. Flared elbows plus a
depth beyond the shoulder line is the classic anterior-shoulder irritant. With a
wrist constraint, use a neutral-wrist implement from day one rather than "pushing
through". With a painful arc, keep the hands at or below chest level.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Wall push-up.
2. Incline push-up on a bar or bench (higher = easier); lower the surface
   progressively.
3. Knee push-up (keep the straight line from knees to head).
4. Eccentric-only push-up: 5 s down, knees to reset.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Tempo push-up: 5 s eccentric, 2 s pause at the bottom.
2. Feet-elevated (decline) push-up.
3. Ring push-up (adds stability demand).
4. Archer push-up.
5. Pseudo planche push-up (see below) — the bridge to planche work.
6. One-arm push-up progressions.

### Substitutes
Dumbbell floor press, machine chest press, band press — all useful when a wrist
or shoulder constraint blocks the floor position.

### References
- Overcoming Gravity, Steven Low — push-up progressions and the pressing ladder.
- NSCA Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning — pressing mechanics.
- ExRx.net — push-up variations and muscle involvement.
- Video search terms: "push up form elbows 45 degrees", "serratus protraction push
  up".

## Dip (dip alle parallele) **[full]**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | vertical push |
| **Type** | compound · bent-arm · closed chain |
| **Primary muscles** | pectoralis major (sternal/lower), triceps brachii, anterior deltoid |
| **Secondary muscles** | rhomboids and lower trapezius (stabilising), core, wrist flexors |
| **Equipment** | parallel bars, dip station, or rings |
| **Difficulty** | intermediate |
| **Skill prerequisite** | 20 s stable support hold with depressed shoulders, no painful arc, no front-shoulder pain in support |

**Why it is here:** the strongest bodyweight vertical push, and the base for the
muscle-up. Also the most common source of shoulder complaints in calisthenics —
which is why depth is prescribed, not improvised.

### Setup
1. Bars at approximately shoulder width. Wider than shoulder width markedly
   increases anterior shoulder stress.
2. Jump or step to support. Arms locked, **shoulders actively depressed** — push
   down away from the ears.
3. Legs together, slightly in front or straight down, ribs down, glutes engaged.
   Choose a lean: a slight forward lean biases the chest, vertical biases the
   triceps. Keep it consistent within a set.

### Execution
1. **Start:** support hold, elbows locked, shoulders depressed, body still.
2. **Eccentric:** lower over 2–3 s, elbows tracking back and staying close to
   the body — not flaring outward.
3. **Bottom:** stop when the shoulders reach the level of the elbows (upper arm
   roughly parallel to the floor). Go deeper **only** if the screen was clean and
   the depth is symptom-free.
4. **Concentric:** press back up, keeping the shoulders depressed as you rise.
5. **Top:** full elbow lockout; shoulders stay down, no shrug.

### Cues
- "Shoulders away from the ears, top and bottom."
- "Elbows brush your ribs — don't let them flare."
- "Stop at parallel."
- "Chest leads on the way down."

### Breathing
Inhale on the descent, exhale on the press. Do not hold a maximal Valsalva if
there is any blood-pressure concern.

### Tempo
`3-1-1-0`. The pause at the bottom is where control is built — and where you find
out whether the depth is safe.

### Range of motion standard
Shoulders to at least elbow height (humerus parallel); full lockout at the top;
no swing; shoulders depressed throughout.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Going too deep | extreme shoulder extension under load — the classic anterior shoulder and sternoclavicular irritant | stop at parallel; use a tactile marker or film side-on |
| Shrugged shoulders in support | loses scapular control, loads the joint rather than the muscle | 20 s support holds as a separate drill before dips return |
| Elbows flaring wide | anterior shoulder stress | narrow bars, "elbows to the ribs" |
| Swinging the legs | momentum; also destabilises the shoulder at the bottom | hollow body, legs still, pause between reps |
| Partial lockout | the triceps never finish the job | pause 1 s at the top |
| Head forward / chin poke | neck compensation | "long neck" |

### Risk notes
Loads the anterior shoulder capsule, the long head of the biceps, the pec major
tendon and the sternoclavicular joint. Depth is the single biggest risk variable.
Weighted dips progress **slower** than weighted pull-ups. Any front-shoulder
pinch, any AC-joint point tenderness, or any clicking with pain means dips come
out of the program and push-ups or floor presses go in.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Support hold, 3 x 20 s.
2. Bench dip with feet on the floor **(use sparingly — the shoulder position is
   worse than a parallel-bar dip; prefer the next option)**.
3. Box dip: parallettes on boxes with feet on the floor taking part of the load.
4. Band-assisted dip.
5. Eccentric-only dip: step to the top, lower over 5 s.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Add reps to 3x12 with a 1 s pause at the bottom.
2. Tempo dip: 5 s eccentric.
3. Ring dip (feet supported → free).
4. Ring dip with rings turned out at the top (RTO).
5. Weighted dip, belt, +2.5% bodyweight steps.
6. Bulgarian dip / straight-bar dip (higher demand — only with a clean shoulder).

### Substitutes
Push-up variations, dumbbell floor press, machine dip, close-grip bench press —
all appropriate when a shoulder constraint blocks the dip.

### References
- Overcoming Gravity, Steven Low — dip progressions and shoulder health.
- NSCA Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning — closed-chain pressing.
- Video search terms: "dip depth shoulder safety", "ring support hold RTO
  progression".

## Pseudo Planche Push-up (PPPU) **[full]**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | horizontal push with anterior lean |
| **Type** | compound · bent-arm with a straight-arm-like shoulder demand |
| **Primary muscles** | anterior deltoid, pectoralis major (clavicular), biceps (as a shoulder flexor), serratus anterior |
| **Secondary muscles** | triceps, core (anti-extension), wrist flexors, glutes |
| **Equipment** | floor or parallettes (parallettes strongly preferred) |
| **Difficulty** | intermediate–advanced |
| **Skill prerequisite** | 20 clean push-ups, 45 s hollow hold, pain-free straight-arm tolerance test, 8+ weeks of pressing base |

**Why it is here:** the most transferable non-isometric planche accessory. It
trains anterior lean and shoulder flexion under load without the full tendon
exposure of a planche hold.

### Setup
1. Start in a push-up position on parallettes, hands at hip level rather than
   under the shoulders — the further back the hands, the harder.
2. Turn the hands out slightly if using the floor (protects the wrist); on
   parallettes keep the wrists neutral.
3. Protract the shoulder blades hard, lock the elbows, tuck the ribs, squeeze
   the glutes. Lean forward so the shoulders travel in front of the hands.

### Execution
1. **Start:** leaning front support, shoulders ahead of the hands, elbows locked,
   body straight, scapulae protracted.
2. **Eccentric:** lower over 3 s, keeping the lean — the shoulders stay in front
   of the hands throughout. Elbows point backward, staying close to the ribs.
3. **Bottom:** chest near the hands, lean maintained. Do not let the hips drop or
   the shoulders retreat behind the wrists.
4. **Concentric:** press back up while maintaining the lean, finishing with a
   hard protraction.

### Cues
- "Shoulders stay in front of your hands the whole time."
- "Protract hard — push the ground away and apart."
- "Elbows point back, not out."
- "Hips locked, one line."

### Breathing
Exhale on the press. Keep a braced trunk; this exercise fails at the core first
for most people.

### Tempo
`3-1-1-1`. Never fast, never bouncy.

### Range of motion standard
Shoulders in front of the wrists at every point of the rep; body straight; full
lockout with protraction at the top.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Losing the lean at the bottom | it becomes an ordinary push-up, the planche stimulus disappears | reduce the lean, film side-on, check the shoulder-wrist line each rep |
| Hips sagging | lumbar extension under a long lever | more hollow-body work; regress to an incline |
| Elbows flaring | anterior shoulder stress | "elbows back", narrow the hands |
| Wrist pain | the wrist angle in lean is extreme | parallettes, always; run the wrist prep protocol |
| Adding volume too fast | this is straight-arm-adjacent loading on the biceps and anterior shoulder | cap the weekly increase at 10% |

### Risk notes
High demand on the biceps tendon at the shoulder, the anterior deltoid and the
wrist. This is the exercise where planche-related elbow and shoulder problems
usually begin. Introduce at low volume (3 x 5 with a small lean), progress the
lean before the reps, and stop at the first inner-elbow or front-shoulder ache.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Incline pseudo planche push-up (hands on a bench).
2. Planche lean hold, 5 x 10 s, small lean.
3. Standard push-up with hands at hip level, no lean.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Increase the lean (measure shoulder-past-wrist distance in cm and progress it).
2. Feet elevated.
3. Tuck planche push-up.
4. Straddle planche push-up.

### Substitutes
Incline dumbbell press with a strong protraction focus; banded shoulder-flexion
work — neither is a full substitute, but both keep the pattern alive under a
wrist constraint.

### References
- Overcoming Gravity, Steven Low — planche progressions and pressing ladders.
- Video search terms: "pseudo planche push up tutorial", "planche lean
  progression parallettes".

## Compact catalogue — horizontal push

| Exercise | Type | Notes for prescription |
|---|---|---|
| **Incline push-up** | bodyweight | the correct beginner entry; lower the surface to progress |
| **Decline push-up** | bodyweight | shifts load toward the upper chest and shoulders |
| **Ring push-up** | bodyweight + stability | huge serratus and stabiliser demand; reduce reps ~30% versus the floor |
| **Archer push-up** | unilateral bias | keep the straight arm straight; entry to one-arm work |
| **Diamond push-up** | triceps bias | watch the wrist and elbow; not for irritable elbows |
| **Bench press (barbell)** | loaded | best absolute loading tool for the horizontal push; requires safeties when training alone |
| **Dumbbell bench / floor press** | loaded | floor press caps the range — first choice with an anterior shoulder constraint |
| **Incline dumbbell press** | loaded | upper chest and anterior delt; good planche accessory |
| **Machine chest press** | loaded | safe to failure, useful for hypertrophy volume without stabiliser fatigue |
| **Cable fly / pec deck** | isolation | keep out of extreme stretch with a shoulder constraint |
| **Dumbbell pullover** | isolation | trains shoulder flexion range; go carefully at end range |

## Compact catalogue — vertical push

| Exercise | Type | Notes for prescription |
|---|---|---|
| **Pike push-up** | bodyweight | the HSPU entry point; hips high, head to the floor between the hands |
| **Elevated pike push-up** | bodyweight | feet on a box; the higher the feet, the more vertical the load |
| **Wall handstand push-up** | bodyweight | see [skill-progressions](../../skill-progressions/SKILL.md) |
| **Overhead press (barbell)** | loaded | contraindicated with limited overhead reach or a painful arc |
| **Seated dumbbell press** | loaded | allows a neutral grip; kinder to the shoulder than a barbell |
| **Landmine press** | loaded | the best pressing option under most shoulder constraints — the arc avoids the painful range |
| **Z-press / half-kneeling press** | loaded | exposes thoracic and core compensation; excellent teaching tool |
| **Lateral raise** | isolation | deltoid volume without joint stress; keep it light and controlled |
| **Handstand hold work** | isometric | frequency over duration; see skill-progressions |

## Compact catalogue — straight-arm push

| Exercise | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Planche lean** | the primary straight-arm push builder; measure the lean in cm and progress it |
| **Tuck / advanced tuck planche** | see skill-progressions; strict volume caps apply |
| **Frog stand (crow)** | bent-arm entry to planche balance; low tendon cost, good for beginners |
| **Straight-arm protraction holds** | scapular push-up in a front support, 3 x 10 slow reps |
| **Ring support hold with RTO** | shoulder stability and straight-arm tolerance; progress turnout in degrees |
| **L-sit on parallettes** | straight-arm depression plus compression; see core-exercises |

## Depth and elbow policy for pushing

- Dips stop at humerus-parallel unless depth has been screened clean and is
  symptom-free.
- Bench press to the chest is fine for a clean shoulder; use a floor press or a
  board when the anterior shoulder is irritable.
- Elbows track at ~45° from the torso in every horizontal push. "T-shape" flaring
  is a fault, not a variation.
- Wrist discomfort is answered with an implement (parallettes, handles, fists,
  dumbbells), never with "push through it".
