# Core Catalogue

Categorised by function, not by "abs". The core is programmed for what it must
resist: extension, rotation, lateral flexion — plus active compression, which is
what makes calisthenics skills possible.

Minimum weekly dose: **anti-extension + anti-rotation/lateral**, every program.
Skill goals add compression work.

---

## Hollow Body Hold (hollow / posizione cava) **[full]**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Pattern** | anti-extension |
| **Type** | isometric |
| **Primary muscles** | rectus abdominis, external and internal obliques, transversus abdominis |
| **Secondary muscles** | hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris), quadriceps, serratus anterior |
| **Equipment** | floor |
| **Difficulty** | beginner (regressable) |
| **Skill prerequisite** | none — but it is the prerequisite for almost everything else |

**Why it is here:** the hollow position is the body line of the front lever, the
handstand, the pull-up and the planche. Nothing in calisthenics works without it.

### Setup
1. Lie on the back. Press the low back flat into the floor — there should be no
   gap under the lumbar spine. This is the whole exercise; everything else is
   leverage.
2. Posteriorly tilt the pelvis slightly, ribs pulled down toward the hips.
3. Arms overhead by the ears if the shoulder range allows; otherwise across the
   chest.

### Execution
1. **Start:** low back pressed flat, ribs down.
2. **Raise:** lift the shoulder blades off the floor and lift the legs, keeping
   them straight and together, toes pointed.
3. **Find the level:** lower the legs toward the floor **only as far as the low
   back stays flat**. The moment a gap appears under the low back, the legs are
   too low.
4. **Hold:** breathe shallowly but continuously. The position does not change
   while you breathe.
5. **Exit:** bring the knees in and roll out; do not drop the legs.

### Cues
- "Press your low back into the floor and keep it there."
- "Ribs down, pelvis tucked."
- "Legs straight, toes pointed, squeeze everything."
- "Breathe without moving."

### Breathing
Shallow, continuous, through the nose if possible. Breath-holding is the most
common way people fake this hold.

### Tempo
Isometric. Prescribe seconds, not reps.

### Range of motion standard
The low back stays in contact with the floor for the entire hold. The clock stops
the moment it lifts.

### Common faults
| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Low back arching off the floor | the exercise is now a hip flexor hold, and the lumbar spine is loaded in extension | raise the legs, tuck the knees, shorten the hold |
| Holding the breath | cannot be sustained, and does not transfer to skills | count out loud or breathe on a cadence |
| Neck straining | rectus abdominis not doing the work | chin tucked, look at the knees, arms across the chest |
| Legs bent when they should be straight | changes the leverage silently, making progress unmeasurable | pick a leg position and record it |
| Progressing time before position | builds a bad hollow | position first, always |

### Risk notes
Very low risk. With a lumbar-extension-sensitive back, start with the tuck
variation and only lengthen the lever once the flat-back position is
unshakeable. Do not use this to "fix" back pain — it is training, not treatment.

### Regressions (easier → this)
1. Dead bug (opposite arm and leg, slow).
2. Tuck hollow: knees to chest, shoulders raised.
3. Hollow with knees bent at 90° and arms at the sides.
4. Hollow with arms at the sides, legs straight and high.

### Progressions (this → harder)
1. Lower the legs, arms overhead — full hollow.
2. Hollow rock (maintain the shape while rocking; the shape must not change).
3. Weighted hollow (light plate overhead).
4. Hanging hollow hold from a bar.
5. Hollow-body pull-up and front lever tuck holds.

### Substitutes
Ab wheel rollout from the knees, ring/TRX fallout, plank with posterior pelvic
tilt.

### References
- Overcoming Gravity, Steven Low — the hollow body position and its role in all
  gymnastics strength.
- Gymnastics conditioning literature (GymnasticBodies, USA Gymnastics
  conditioning manuals) — hollow and arch body positions.
- Video search terms: "hollow body hold tutorial", "hollow hold low back flat".

## Compact catalogue — anti-extension

| Exercise | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Front plank** | entry point; 60 s with a flat back is the standard before progressing |
| **Ab wheel rollout** | outstanding; from the knees first, and never let the low back arch |
| **Ring / TRX fallout** | same pattern, infinitely adjustable by foot position |
| **Dead bug** | teaches the pattern with the lowest risk; excellent with lumbar constraints |
| **Hollow rock** | dynamic hollow; the shape must not change as you rock |
| **Body saw** | plank on sliders, moving fore and aft; high demand, low load |
| **Hanging hollow hold** | bridges to front lever work |
| **Standing band anti-extension press-out (Pallof overhead)** | good with lumbar constraints |

## Compact catalogue — anti-rotation and lateral

| Exercise | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Pallof press** | the archetypal anti-rotation drill; slow, no trunk rotation |
| **Side plank** | 45 s each side is the standard; check side-to-side symmetry |
| **Copenhagen plank** | adductor + lateral core; short lever first |
| **Suitcase carry** | loaded lateral core, extremely practical; do not let the trunk lean |
| **Half-kneeling anti-rotation chop** | integrates hip and trunk |
| **Single-arm plank / single-arm push-up hold** | anti-rotation under a push-up position |
| **Renegade row** | anti-rotation + horizontal pull; keep the hips still |

## Compact catalogue — flexion and compression

Compression is the ability to actively pull the legs toward the torso — it is
what makes L-sits, leg raises, levers and press-to-handstand possible. It is
trained actively, not by stretching.

| Exercise | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Hanging knee raise** | entry; no swing, controlled descent |
| **Hanging leg raise (straight)** | legs straight, no swing; toes-to-bar is the extension of this |
| **L-sit (floor / parallettes)** | straight-arm depression + compression; the classic compression test |
| **V-sit progression** | advanced compression; requires hamstring length and hip flexor strength |
| **Seated pike compression lift** | sit in a pike, lift the legs off the floor — pure active compression |
| **Straddle compression lift** | same, straddled |
| **Candlestick / rollover** | teaches the compression and the shape for press handstands |
| **Toes-to-bar** | dynamic compression under a hanging load |
| **Reverse hyper / hip extension** | the counterpart — do not train only flexion |

## Compact catalogue — extension (arch) and posterior trunk

Programming only hollow work produces an athlete who cannot arch. Balance it.

| Exercise | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Arch body hold (superman)** | the counterpart to the hollow; same quality rules |
| **Arch rock** | dynamic arch |
| **Back extension to neutral** | stop at neutral, do not hyperextend |
| **Bird dog** | coordination and anti-rotation with light extension |
| **Reverse hyperextension** | glute and erector work with low compression |
| **Bridge progressions** | thoracic extension and shoulder range; approach carefully with a lumbar constraint |

## Programming notes

- **Anti-extension is the priority** for calisthenics athletes — it is the
  limiter for levers, planche and handstands.
- Isometric core holds are prescribed in **seconds of quality**, and the clock
  stops when the position breaks.
- Core work goes late in the session (phase 5), except when it *is* the skill
  work (hollow-body practice before lever training).
- 4–8 hard sets per week for beginners, 6–12 for intermediates, split across
  anti-extension, anti-rotation and compression.
- With any lumbar constraint: dead bug, Pallof, side plank and bird dog first;
  no loaded spinal flexion; no full sit-ups; no toes-to-bar.
