# Realistic Timelines

Use these to sanity-check a stated goal against the athlete's training age. Give
the number once, factually, with the achievable alternative beside it. Never use
them to discourage — use them to make the plan honest.

All ranges assume 3–5 quality sessions per week, adequate sleep and food, no
significant injury interruption, and a bodyweight in a reasonable range for the
skill. Individual variation is large; bodyweight, limb lengths and prior
gymnastics background move these substantially.

---

## Foundational strength (from untrained)

| Milestone | Typical time | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| First strict pull-up | 2–6 months | longer at higher bodyweight; band and eccentric work drive it |
| 10 strict pull-ups | 6–18 months | |
| First strict dip | 1–4 months | shoulder mobility often the limiter, not strength |
| 20 push-ups full ROM | 2–4 months | |
| 60 s hollow hold | 2–4 months | |
| Full-depth bodyweight squat, 20 reps | 1–3 months | ankle mobility is the usual limiter |
| Pistol squat (each side) | 6–15 months | mobility + unilateral strength + balance |
| 5 min total dead hang capacity | 2–4 months | grip and shoulder tissue tolerance |

## Weighted calisthenics

| Milestone | From bodyweight competence | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pull-up with +25% bodyweight | 12–24 months | |

## Skills

| Skill | From untrained | From solid basics (10 pull-ups / 15 dips / 60 s hollow) |
|---|---|---|
| Freestanding handstand 30 s | 8–24 months | 6–18 months — practice frequency matters more than strength |
| Handstand push-up (wall, full ROM) | 18–36 months | 9–18 months |
| Strict muscle-up | 12–30 months | 4–12 months |
| Tuck front lever 10 s | 6–12 months | 2–5 months |
| Straddle front lever 10 s | 18–36 months | 8–18 months |
| Full front lever 10 s | 2–4 years | 12–30 months |
| Tuck planche 10 s | 9–18 months | 4–10 months |
| Straddle planche 5 s | 3–5 years | 18–40 months |
| Full planche 5 s | 4–8 years | 3–6 years |
| Human flag 5 s | 18–36 months | 9–20 months |
| Back lever 10 s | 9–18 months | 4–10 months — easiest of the levers, but hardest on the biceps tendon |
| One-arm pull-up | 3–6 years | 2–5 years |
| Iron cross | 4–8 years | 3–6 years — rings only, extremely high elbow demand |

**Bodyweight sensitivity.** Straight-arm skills scale badly with mass. A 95 kg
athlete chasing a straddle planche should expect the upper end of every range, or
should decide whether a body-composition phase precedes the skill phase. Say this
neutrally, as physics, not as a comment on their body.

**Height and limb length.** Longer limbs lengthen the lever for planche and front
lever and make them genuinely harder; they help nothing except reach. This is
worth stating once so the athlete does not read slow progress as failure.

## Body composition

| Goal | Realistic rate |
|---|---|
| Fat loss without losing strength | 0.5–0.75% of bodyweight per week |
| Aggressive fat loss (accept performance cost) | up to 1% per week, max 8–12 weeks |
| Muscle gain, first year of training | 0.5–1.0 kg per month |
| Muscle gain, 2nd–3rd year | 0.25–0.5 kg per month |
| Muscle gain, advanced | 1–3 kg per year |

Body-composition targets go to
[recovery-and-nutrition](../../recovery-and-nutrition/SKILL.md); if there is any
history of disordered eating, keep nutrition out of scope and say so.

## Return from layoff

| Layoff | Restart at | Time to previous level |
|---|---|---|
| 2–4 weeks | 60–70% of previous volume | 1–3 weeks |
| 1–3 months | 50% volume, regress leverages one step | 3–8 weeks |
| 3–12 months | rebuild from foundation block | 2–4 months |
| 1 year+ | treat as beginner with fast re-learning | 4–8 months |

Muscle memory is real; tendon tolerance is not. Strength returns faster than
connective-tissue capacity, which is precisely why returning athletes get
injured. Regress leverage even when the athlete feels strong.

## Age adjustments

| Age band | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Under 16 | technique and general athleticism; avoid maximal loading and high-volume straight-arm work; growth plates |
| 16–25 | fastest adaptation; the limiter is usually patience, not capacity |
| 26–40 | baseline ranges as written |
| 40–55 | add 20–40% to skill timelines; more warm-up, more prehab, longer between leverage jumps |
| 55+ | add 40–80%; prioritise joint health, balance and strength maintenance; clearance first |

These are planning aids, not verdicts. Plenty of people beat them. Nobody
benefits from a plan built on beating them by default.
