# Onboarding Question Bank

Ask in batches of 3–6. Translate into the athlete's language. Skip anything
already answered. Bold questions are mandatory — the program is not safe to write
without them.

---

## Batch 1a — Who they are

1. **How old are you?**
2. **Sex at birth** (it changes strength baselines and some injury risk profiles),
   and anything else you want me to know about your body.
3. **Height and current bodyweight?** (used for load prescriptions and skill
   difficulty estimates — not for judgement)
4. What sports or physical activity have you done in your life? Include
   childhood — gymnastics, climbing, swimming and martial arts all leave a mark
   on what you can do now.
5. Dominant hand/side? Any known side you trust less?
6. **Why now?** What made you decide to start or restart at this moment?

*Coach note:* "why now" predicts adherence better than any other question. Record
the exact wording.

---

## Batch 1b — What for

Asked in the same breath as 1a: an athlete who says what they want has
already said a good deal about who they are.

1. **What do you want to be able to do that you can't do today?** List as many as
   you like; we will rank them.
2. **By when?** Pick a date or a horizon for the top one.
3. If you could only achieve one of them in the next six months, which?
4. How will we know you got there — what is the test? (a hold time, a rep count,
   a load, a photo, a measurement)
5. Is there anything you want to *keep* while chasing that — bodyweight, running
   ability, a sport, a hobby that costs recovery?
6. Do you compete, or want to? In what, and when is the event?
7. Aesthetic goals? Be honest — they change exercise selection, and there is
   nothing wrong with them.

*Convert every answer into a specific, measurable, time-bound goal before
writing the profile. Cap at two primary goals; label the rest `maintain` or
`parked`.*

---

## Batch 2 — History and health

The batch that prevents injuries. Never compressed, never deferred — it is the
only one whose absence makes a program unsafe rather than merely generic.

**This batch prevents injuries. Do not compress it.**

### Training history

1. **How long have you been training consistently, and what did the last 3
   months actually look like?** (sessions per week, what kind)
2. What is the most you have ever trained, and what happened?
3. Have you had a long layoff? How long, and why?
4. **What can you do right now, honestly?** — max strict pull-ups, max push-ups,
   max dips, deepest squat you can hold, longest plank, any skill holds. Estimates
   are fine; we will test properly later.
5. Have you ever followed a written program? Which one, and how did it go?

### Injury and pain

6. **Any current pain, ache or niggle, anywhere?** Even minor. If yes, run the
   standard six for each site: where exactly, when it appears, how long it has
   been there and the trend, intensity 0–10 at worst and now, what makes it
   better or worse, and any red flags (night pain, numbness, weakness, giving
   way, swelling, recent trauma).
7. **Past injuries** — what, when, which side, how it was treated, and whether it
   fully resolved. Include: shoulder, elbow, wrist, lower back, hip, knee, ankle,
   and any fracture or surgery.
8. **Any surgery, ever?** When, and were you discharged from rehab?
9. Any joint that dislocates, subluxes or feels unstable?
10. **Any diagnosed condition I should program around?** — cardiovascular,
    respiratory, metabolic (including diabetes), hypertension, hernia,
    osteoporosis, hypermobility (EDS), autoimmune or inflammatory conditions,
    epilepsy, pregnancy or postpartum.
11. Any medication that affects training? (beta-blockers blunt heart rate,
    fluoroquinolones raise tendon risk, corticosteroids affect tissue,
    anticoagulants change contact/fall risk, GLP-1 agonists affect appetite and
    lean mass)
12. Have you been told by a clinician not to do something specific?

*Any red flag -> stop that region, record it, advise referral in one plain
sentence, keep interviewing the rest.*

## Batch 3 — Logistics

1. **How many days a week can you realistically train?** Not the best case — the
   number that survives a bad week.
2. **How long is a session, door to door?**
3. What time of day, and how fixed is it?
4. **What equipment do you have access to?** Go item by item — see
   [equipment-checklist.md](equipment-checklist.md).
5. Where do you train — home, gym, park, a mix? Indoor space and ceiling height?
6. Do you travel, work shifts, or have periods where training collapses? How
   often, and for how long?
7. Do you train alone? (this decides whether we can program true failure on
   loaded overhead work)
8. Is there anyone who can film you? Video is how we fix technique remotely.

## Later — Lifestyle and preferences

Not asked before the first program. These shape recovery advice and coaching
tone, not the first block's safety. Record them as `unknown`, carry them in
`Open questions`, and ask when they matter.

1. **How much do you sleep, and how well?** Average hours and whether you wake
   rested.
2. What is your job — sedentary, standing, physical, mixed? Any posture you are
   stuck in for hours?
3. Stress level right now, 0–10, and is it stable or spiking?
4. How do you eat, broadly? Any restriction — vegetarian, vegan, allergies,
   religious, medical? Any history of disordered eating? (if yes, keep nutrition
   out of scope and say so)
5. Do you do other physical activity — running, cycling, climbing, martial arts,
   dance, a physical job? How much per week? (this is recovery cost, and it must
   be budgeted)
6. Alcohol, nicotine, recreational substances — anything that will affect
   recovery? No judgement, just planning.
7. **What do you hate doing?** What have you quit programs over?
8. What do you love doing? (put some of it in the plan — adherence is a training
   variable)
9. How do you want to be coached — detailed explanations, or just tell me what to
   do?
10. Any equipment or exercise you are afraid of? (fear is a real programming
    constraint, and it is workable)

## Follow-up bank — use when the answer is thin

| Vague answer | Follow-up |
|---|---|
| "I'm pretty fit" | "Give me one number: max strict pull-ups today, no kipping." |
| "My shoulder is a bit funny" | run the standard six |
| "I have a gym at home" | "List what's in it — bar? rings? dumbbells and what weights? bench? rack?" |
| "I want to get strong" | "Strong at what, and how will we measure it in 12 weeks?" |
| "Whenever I can train" | "If I schedule three fixed days, which three survive a bad week?" |
| "I sleep okay" | "Hours on a typical weeknight, and do you wake up rested?" |
| "I had a back thing years ago" | "What was it called, when, what treatment, and does anything still trigger it?" |
| "I'm flexible" | "Can you sit in a deep squat, heels down, for 60 s? Can you get your arms overhead against a wall with your back flat?" |
| "I don't have much time" | "What's the shortest session you'd still show up for? 25 minutes done is better than 75 skipped." |

## Re-interview triggers

Re-run the relevant batch when:

- a goal changes or is achieved (Batch 2)
- any new pain appears, or an old injury flares (Batch 3)
- days, session length, equipment or location change (Batch 4)
- sleep, stress, job or other activity load changes materially (Batch 5)
- the athlete returns after a layoff of 3+ weeks (Batches 3 and 4)
- more than 6 months have passed since the last full profile update
