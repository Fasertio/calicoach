# Weight Room Integration

Weights are a tool, not a rival discipline. They earn their place when they load
something bodyweight training cannot load well, isolate something that is
lagging, or provide a progressive route around a constraint.

**Announce it:** "Using weight-room-integration to add loaded work to your
program."

## When weights are the right answer

| Situation | Why weights win |
|---|---|
| **Legs** | bodyweight leg training runs out of overload quickly; a squat, hinge or leg press is simply better |
| **Hip hinge** | there is no good bodyweight deadlift; RDLs and hip thrusts have no bodyweight equivalent |
| **Progressive loading in small steps** | 2.5 kg is a 2% jump; a leverage change can be a 20% jump |
| **A lagging muscle** | rear delts, rotator cuff, hamstrings, calves, biceps, triceps — isolation is faster than hoping a compound covers it |
| **Working around a constraint** | landmine press when overhead is contraindicated; leg press when balance is limited; chest-supported row when the low back is out |
| **Below the bodyweight threshold** | an athlete who cannot yet do a pull-up progresses faster with a loadable lat pulldown alongside rows and eccentrics |
| **Hypertrophy blocks** | when the goal is tissue, machines and dumbbells deliver volume with less systemic fatigue |
| **Prehab** | cuff, scapular and cable work is more precise and more gradable than bodyweight equivalents |

## When bodyweight is the right answer

- The goal **is** the bodyweight skill — specificity wins.
- Straight-arm and scapular work, where leverage control is the point.
- The athlete has no equipment, or adherence depends on training at home.
- Stability and body-position demands are part of the target.

## Interference management

Weight-room work adds fatigue that competes with skill work. Budget it:

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Skill first, always** | never place skill work after a heavy squat or a pulling block |
| **Separate the heavy legs** | put the heaviest lower-body session on a day with no skill work, or at least 24 h before one |
| **Count all sets in the volume budget** | a barbell row is horizontal pull volume; it comes out of the same weekly budget as ring rows |
| **Cap total hard sets** | adding weights is not adding a second program; total weekly hard sets per pattern stay within the landmarks |
| **Watch grip fatigue** | deadlifts and rows tax the grip that the next day's hangs need |
| **Watch shoulder volume** | bench + dips + overhead press + handstand work in the same week is a lot of anterior shoulder |

## Loading and progression

| Movement class | Typical rep range | Progression step |
|---|---|---|
| Barbell lower-body compound | 3–8 | +2.5–5 kg |
| Barbell upper-body compound | 4–8 | +2.5 kg |
| Dumbbell compound | 6–12 | next dumbbell pair |
| Machine / cable compound | 8–15 | one pin |
| Isolation | 10–20 | smallest available step |
| Weighted calisthenics | 3–8 | ~2.5% bodyweight |

**Double progression** is the default: work to the top of the rep range across
all sets at RIR 2, then add load and drop back to the bottom of the range.

Use **fractional plates (0.5–1.25 kg)** for upper-body work. The absence of small
plates is why most people stall on overhead and weighted pull-up progressions.

## Safety rules for the weight room

- **Training alone:** no true failure on any lift that can pin the athlete.
  Use a rack with pins set at the bottom position, dumbbells, or machines.
- **Barbell overhead work** is contraindicated with limited overhead reach or a
  painful arc — use landmine or incline pressing instead. Do not "work up to it".
- **Deadlifts and heavy hinges** require a clean lumbar screen. With any lumbar
  constraint, use trap bar, rack pulls, hip thrusts or back extensions.
- **Valsalva** is a legitimate bracing technique for heavy lifts, but not for
  athletes with hypertension, cardiovascular concerns, or during pregnancy —
  cue continuous exhale on the effort instead.
- **Load progression is slower than motivation.** The moment technique changes to
  accommodate the load, the load is too heavy.

## Substitution map — constraint to weight-room answer

| Constraint | Bodyweight option removed | Weight-room answer |
|---|---|---|
| Painful arc / no overhead | overhead press, HSPU, deep dips | landmine press, incline dumbbell press, low-angle cable press |
| Front-shoulder pain in support | dips, ring support | dumbbell floor press, machine chest press, neutral-grip press |
| Medial elbow pain | high-volume pull-ups | chest-supported row, neutral-grip lat pulldown, machine row (lower grip demand) |
| Wrist pain in extension | push-ups, planche, handstand | dumbbell press (neutral wrist), machine press, cable work |
| Lumbar flexion sensitivity | toes-to-bar, deep hinge | chest-supported row, hip thrust, leg press, back extension to neutral |
| Anterior knee pain | pistols, jumping | leg press to short range, Spanish squat, leg extension in the pain-free range, hamstring curls |
| Balance / vestibular issues | single-leg and inverted work | machine-based bilateral work, seated pressing |
| Cannot yet do a pull-up | pull-ups | lat pulldown with real progressive load, plus rows and eccentrics |

## A hybrid week that works

Example: 4 days, calisthenics-primary with weight-room support.

| Day | Content |
|---|---|
| **Mon — Pull + skill** | wrist/elbow prep · front lever holds · weighted pull-ups 4x5 · chest-supported row 3x10 · straight-arm pulldown 3x12 · face pull 3x15 · hollow 3x40 s |
| **Tue — Legs** | squat 4x6 · RDL 3x8 · Bulgarian split squat 3x10/side · leg curl 3x12 · standing calf raise 4x12 · Copenhagen 3x20 s/side |
| **Thu — Push + skill** | wrist prep · handstand practice 12 min · dips 4x8 · incline dumbbell press 3x10 · pseudo planche push-up 3x6 · lateral raise 3x15 · cuff work 3x15 |
| **Sat — Full body / mixed** | ring rows 4x10 · landmine press 3x10 · trap-bar hinge 3x8 · pull-up 3x AMRAP-1 · carries 3x40 m · core circuit |

Note that skill work is fresh on both skill days, legs sit on their own day, and
the weight-room movements fill patterns that bodyweight covers poorly.

## Definition of done

- [ ] Every weight-room movement has a stated reason it beat the bodyweight option
- [ ] Its sets are counted in the weekly volume budget for its pattern
- [ ] Loading progression is written with a specific step size
- [ ] Safety rules applied (rack pins / no-failure rules if training alone)
- [ ] Skill and heavy work are scheduled so they do not collide
- [ ] Full exercise cards produced, same standard as bodyweight movements
