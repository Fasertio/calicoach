# Energy Systems and Interference

The model behind every conditioning decision, and the one number that decides
how much of it a block can hold.

## The three systems

They are not separate taps. Every effort draws on all three; what changes is
which one dominates and how fast it recovers.

| System | Dominates | Recovers | Trained by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phosphagen** (ATP-CP) | 0–10 s, maximal | 2–5 min, fully | short maximal efforts, long rests |
| **Glycolytic** | 10 s–2 min, very hard | slowly, and it hurts | 20–90 s hard efforts, incomplete rest |
| **Oxidative** (aerobic) | 2 min+, and all recovery | continuously | long steady work, and short work with full rest |

The one that matters most for a calisthenics athlete is the third, and not for
the reason people assume. The aerobic system is what clears the by-products of
everything else: it is the system that decides how fast the athlete recovers
**between sets**, which is to say how much strength work fits in a session.

## What each structure trains

| Structure | Work : rest | System | Costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maximal efforts, full rest | 1 : 10+ | phosphagen | very little; can sit inside a strength day |
| Intervals, hard | 1 : 1 to 1 : 3 | glycolytic | the most of any structure; use sparingly |
| Circuits, moderate | 1 : 1 | mixed | moderate, and joint volume adds up fast |
| Density work | continuous | mixed | moderate |
| Steady state | continuous | oxidative | little, if it is genuinely easy |
| Loaded carries | 1 : 2 | mixed + grip | grip and trunk, which a skill block already taxes |

**The trap is the middle.** Hard intervals and moderate circuits feel the most
like training and cost the most strength per unit of fitness gained. An athlete
who wants a better engine without losing the block usually wants the top row
and the bottom row: short maximal efforts, and genuinely easy steady work.

## Interference

Concurrent training costs strength more than it costs endurance. The cost rises
with how hard, how long, how close to the strength work, and how much the
muscles overlap.

| Factor | Low cost | High cost |
|---|---|---|
| Modality | non-overlapping (row, bike, carry) | same muscles (running before a leg day) |
| Intensity | easy steady, or short maximal | hard intervals |
| Proximity | separate day, or 6 h apart | immediately before strength |
| Duration | under 20 min | over 45 min |
| Frequency | 1–2 a week | 4+ |

**The budget, as a rule of thumb by track:**

| Track | Conditioning it can absorb |
|---|---|
| `strength`, `skill` | up to ~40 min/week, easy or short-maximal, never before the session |
| `hybrid` | 60–120 min/week, with the strength volume set at the lower end of the athlete's band |
| `endurance` | the block's point; strength becomes maintenance — 2 sessions, top-end intensity, low volume |

These are starting points, not laws. What makes them right or wrong is the
marker and the log: if strength markers fall for two weeks running, the
conditioning is too much, whatever the table says.

## The order inside a session

Strength first. Always, for a general athlete.

Conditioning before strength turns the strength work into more conditioning:
the loads that can be moved after a hard piece are not the loads that drive
adaptation, and the technique under that fatigue is not the technique worth
rehearsing. The only reason to reverse it is a deliberate pre-fatigue
intention, which a general athlete does not have.

If the two must share a session and the day is a strength day, put the
conditioning at the end and keep it short. If the conditioning is the point of
the day, give it its own day.

## What to watch in the log

Conditioning fails quietly, because it feels productive while it is doing
damage. Three signals, in order of how early they appear:

1. **Resting heart rate up 5+ bpm across a week** — the earliest and most
   reliable.
2. **A strength marker falling twice in a row** while nothing else changed.
3. **Sleep quality dropping**, or waking early.

Any two of the three: cut conditioning volume by half for a week. All three:
cut it to zero and treat the week as a deload.
