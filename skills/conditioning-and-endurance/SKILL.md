# Conditioning and Endurance

Build an engine that survives contact with the rest of the program.

**Announce it:** "Using conditioning-and-endurance to \<purpose\>."

Most calisthenics athletes do not need much conditioning, and the ones who add
it usually add it in the way that costs them the most strength for the least
fitness. The job here is the opposite: the smallest dose that moves the marker,
placed where it does not tax what the block is actually for.

## Preconditions

Same as any loading: profile and screen first. Conditioning is not exempt —
it is the fastest way to accumulate joint volume, because nobody counts the
sets.

| Required | If missing |
|---|---|
| Profile, screen | [athlete-onboarding](../athlete-onboarding/SKILL.md), [movement-screening](../movement-screening/SKILL.md) |
| Clearance for cardiovascular, respiratory or metabolic disease | stop; refer. See [safety-and-scope](../calisthenics-coach/references/safety-and-scope.md) |

## The three questions, in order

**1. What is the marker?** "Better conditioning" is not a goal. A marker is a
number: 5 km time, a 500 m row, unbroken burpees in 10 minutes, heart rate
recovery in 60 s, or work completed in a fixed window. Without one there is no
way to tell progress from fatigue.

**2. Which system does it need?** The marker decides the structure, not the
athlete's preference. See [energy-systems.md](references/energy-systems.md).

**3. What can the block afford?** Conditioning and strength compete, and the
competition is not symmetric: conditioning costs strength far more than
strength costs conditioning. The interference budget is in
[energy-systems.md](references/energy-systems.md#interference); the protocols
that fit inside it are in
[conditioning-methods.md](references/conditioning-methods.md).

## Volume, and why it is budgeted separately

Conditioning volume goes in the weekly budget as its own axis —
`Conditioning (min/week)` — not folded into the pattern counts. Two reasons.

It is the axis that grows fastest when nobody is watching: a circuit of
twenty exercises is twenty sets that no pattern row ever counted. And it is the
axis whose cost is systemic rather than local, so the usual "is this pattern
over its landmark" question does not catch it.

`calicoach check` requires that row for an `endurance` or `hybrid` block. A
block on those tracks whose budget carries no conditioning is that track in
name only.

## Non-negotiable

1. **Strength first in the session, always.** Conditioning before strength
   turns the strength work into more conditioning. The only exception is a
   deliberate pre-fatigue block, which is not what a general athlete needs.
2. **Never condition a joint the screen restricted.** A constraint applies
   inside a circuit exactly as it applies to a set of presses. Circuits are
   where this gets forgotten, because the exercises go by fast.
3. **Never run intensity and volume up in the same week.** Pick one. The rule
   the rest of the framework applies to sets applies here to minutes.
4. **Conditioning is not a substitute for a deload.** An athlete who is too
   beaten up to lift is too beaten up to sprint.
5. **Technical failure ends the piece.** A circuit run to form failure is a
   rehearsal of bad technique under fatigue, which is where people get hurt.
6. **Straight-arm and skill work never go in a circuit.** They run on the
   tendon clock and need full rest; see
   [skill-progressions](../skill-progressions/SKILL.md).

## Definition of done

- [ ] A named marker with a number and a re-test date
- [ ] The energy system named, and the structure follows from it
- [ ] `Conditioning (min/week)` in the block's volume budget
- [ ] Placement stated: which day, before or after what
- [ ] The interference cost written down, and the strength volume adjusted for it
- [ ] Every exercise in every circuit checked against the active constraints
