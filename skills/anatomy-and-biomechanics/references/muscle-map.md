# Muscle Map by Movement Pattern

Which muscles run each pattern, and where they usually fail first.

---

## Vertical pull (pull-up, lat pulldown, muscle-up)

| Role | Muscles |
|---|---|
| Prime movers | latissimus dorsi, teres major |
| Elbow flexion | biceps brachii, brachialis, brachioradialis |
| Scapular control | lower and middle trapezius, rhomboids, levator scapulae (eccentrically) |
| Assisting | posterior deltoid, pectoralis major (sternal, in the first part of the range) |
| Stabilising | forearm flexors (grip), rectus abdominis and obliques (anti-extension) |

**Fails first:** grip, then scapular control, then the lats. Almost never the
biceps.

---

## Horizontal pull (rows)

| Role | Muscles |
|---|---|
| Prime movers | middle trapezius, rhomboids, latissimus dorsi, posterior deltoid |
| Elbow flexion | biceps, brachialis |
| Stabilising | lower trapezius, erector spinae, glutes and hamstrings (in bodyweight versions), core |

**Fails first:** the trunk in bodyweight versions (the hips sag); the mid-back in
loaded versions.

---

## Vertical push (dips at the top, overhead press, HSPU)

| Role | Muscles |
|---|---|
| Prime movers | anterior and lateral deltoid, triceps brachii |
| Scapular upward rotation | serratus anterior, upper and lower trapezius |
| Stabilising | rotator cuff, core (anti-extension), lats (eccentrically at the top) |

**Fails first:** scapular upward rotation and overhead range, then the triceps.
An athlete who arches their lower back to press overhead has a range problem, not
a strength problem.

---

## Horizontal push (push-up, bench, dip descent)

| Role | Muscles |
|---|---|
| Prime movers | pectoralis major, anterior deltoid, triceps brachii |
| Protraction | serratus anterior |
| Stabilising | rotator cuff, core, glutes and quads in bodyweight versions |

**Fails first:** the trunk in push-ups (hips sag), the triceps at lockout in
loaded pressing.

---

## Straight-arm push (planche, planche lean, front support)

| Role | Muscles |
|---|---|
| Shoulder flexion torque | anterior deltoid, pectoralis major (clavicular), biceps (as a shoulder flexor) |
| Scapular protraction and depression | serratus anterior, pectoralis minor, lower trapezius |
| Elbow stabilisation (isometric, at length) | biceps brachii, brachialis — this is the tendon-cost part |
| Trunk | rectus abdominis, obliques, hip flexors, glutes |
| Wrist | forearm flexors and extensors |

**Fails first:** the wrist, then the anterior deltoid, then the core. The elbow
does not "fail" — it complains, later.

---

## Straight-arm pull (front lever, back lever, straight-arm pulldown)

| Role | Muscles |
|---|---|
| Shoulder extension torque | latissimus dorsi, teres major, long head of triceps, posterior deltoid |
| Scapular depression and retraction | lower trapezius, rhomboids |
| Elbow stabilisation (isometric, at length) | biceps brachii, brachialis |
| Trunk (anti-extension) | rectus abdominis, obliques, hip flexors |
| Line | glutes, hamstrings, quadriceps |

**Fails first:** the anti-extension core (the hips sag), then the lats. Elbow
tendon cost accumulates silently.

---

## Knee-dominant (squat, pistol, lunge)

| Role | Muscles |
|---|---|
| Knee extension | quadriceps (rectus femoris, vasti) |
| Hip extension | gluteus maximus, adductor magnus |
| Stabilising | gluteus medius and minimus (frontal plane), hamstrings, erectors, core, calves |

**Fails first:** ankle range or hip control, then the quads.

---

## Hip hinge (RDL, deadlift, hip thrust, kettlebell swing)

| Role | Muscles |
|---|---|
| Hip extension | gluteus maximus, hamstrings (biceps femoris, semitendinosus, semimembranosus), adductor magnus |
| Spinal stabilisation | erector spinae, multifidus, quadratus lumborum, abdominals |
| Grip and upper back | forearms, lats, mid-traps |

**Fails first:** the motor pattern (they squat instead of hinging), then the
erectors, then grip.

---

## Anti-extension core (hollow, ab wheel, plank, levers)

| Role | Muscles |
|---|---|
| Prime | rectus abdominis, external and internal obliques |
| Deep | transversus abdominis |
| Assisting | hip flexors (iliopsoas, rectus femoris), serratus anterior, quadriceps |

**Fails first:** the lumbar position — the back arches. That is the end of the
set, whatever the athlete says.

---

## Anti-rotation / lateral (Pallof, side plank, flag, carries)

| Role | Muscles |
|---|---|
| Prime | obliques (internal and external), quadratus lumborum |
| Assisting | gluteus medius, latissimus dorsi, transversus abdominis, adductors |

**Fails first:** the glute medius on the down side in side planks; the obliques
in flag work.

---

## Grip

| Role | Muscles |
|---|---|
| Finger flexion | flexor digitorum superficialis and profundus |
| Wrist flexion | flexor carpi radialis and ulnaris |
| Wrist extension (antagonist) | extensor carpi radialis longus/brevis, extensor carpi ulnaris |
| Thumb (pinch) | thenar group, adductor pollicis |
| Elbow flexion assist | brachioradialis |

**Fails first:** the support grip on long hangs. Train grip extension too — the
imbalance contributes to elbow pain.

---

## The commonly under-trained muscles in calisthenics

| Muscle | Consequence of neglect | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Lower trapezius | poor scapular depression, shoulder impingement | prone Y raise, scapular pull-up, face pull |
| Serratus anterior | poor upward rotation and protraction, planche stalls | scapular push-up, wall slide, protraction holds |
| Rotator cuff (external rotators) | shoulder pain under overhead and pressing volume | band and cable external rotation, face pull |
| Wrist extensors | lateral elbow pain, wrist instability | reverse wrist curls, finger extension |
| Hamstrings | hinge weakness, knee and hamstring injuries | RDL, leg curl, Nordic regressions |
| Gluteus medius | knee valgus, poor single-leg control | banded walks, side-lying abduction, hip airplane |
| Adductors | groin strains, weak flag and straddle positions | Copenhagen plank progression |
| Calves (soleus) | Achilles issues, limited dorsiflexion | seated calf raise |
| Neck | discomfort in handstand and inversion work | isometric neck holds |
| Spinal extensors | poor line in levers, hinge weakness | back extension to neutral, arch holds |

---

## A note on "activation"

Claims that a specific exercise uniquely "activates" a specific muscle are weaker
than they sound. What reliably drives adaptation is load, effort, range and
frequency. Use the muscle map to make sure a pattern is not missing from the
program — not to justify a specific exercise as magic.
