# Anatomy and Biomechanics

The reasoning layer under exercise selection. Use it to explain, to substitute
intelligently, and to predict what a position will load.

**Announce it:** "Using anatomy-and-biomechanics to explain \<topic\>."

Explaining anatomy is not diagnosing. Describing what a structure does is fine;
telling an athlete what is wrong with theirs is not — see
[safety-and-scope](../calisthenics-coach/references/safety-and-scope.md).

## The four questions that answer most anatomy requests

### 1. What does this exercise actually train?
Name the **joint actions**, then the muscles that produce them.

> A ring row is shoulder extension/horizontal abduction plus scapular
> retraction and elbow flexion. So: lats and teres major, posterior deltoid,
> middle trapezius and rhomboids, biceps and brachialis, with the trunk working
> isometrically to hold the line.

### 2. Why is this position risky?
Name the **structure under tension** and the **position that puts it there**.

> A dip below humerus-parallel puts the shoulder into deep extension with the
> humeral head translating anteriorly. That loads the anterior capsule, the long
> head of biceps in the bicipital groove, and the AC joint. Depth is the risk
> variable, so we cap it.

### 3. Why is this harder than that?
Answer with **leverage** — moment arm, not effort. See
[leverage.md](references/leverage.md).

> Straddling the front lever shortens the distance from the shoulder to the
> combined centre of mass, which shortens the moment arm at the shoulder, which
> reduces the torque you have to resist. Same body, less lever.

### 4. What is the weak link?
For each pattern there is a usual suspect. Say which, and how to test it.

| Pattern | Usual weak link | Quick test |
|---|---|---|
| Pull-up | grip or scapular control, rarely the lats | scapular pull-up quality; dead hang time |
| Dip | anterior shoulder tolerance, triceps lockout | support hold quality; lockout under fatigue |
| Push-up | trunk (anti-extension), not the chest | does the hip sag before the arms fail? |
| Front lever | anti-extension core, then lats | can they hold a 45 s flat-back hollow? |
| Planche | anterior deltoid tolerance and wrist, then core | lean distance at 10 s, wrist tolerance |
| Handstand | balance and shoulder flexion range | chest-to-wall hold quality |
| Muscle-up | pull height and transition, not strength alone | can they pull to the sternum? |
| Pistol | ankle dorsiflexion, then hip control | knee-to-wall; step-down quality |
| Squat | ankle or thoracic, then quads | knee-to-wall; overhead squat position |
| Hinge | motor pattern and hamstring length, then erectors | dowel hinge test |

## Explaining well

- **One sentence of mechanism, then the practical consequence.** Athletes want
  to know what to do differently, not a lecture.
- **Use the athlete's own experience as the anchor**: "that pinch at the front of
  your shoulder in the bottom of a dip" is more useful than "subacromial space".
- **Say when a model is contested.** Fascial lines, "muscle imbalance"
  explanations for pain, and specific-muscle-activation claims are frameworks,
  not settled fact. Label them.
- **Pain is not a structural readout.** Pain correlates poorly with imaging
  findings and with tissue damage. Explaining that honestly helps athletes more
  than a confident structural story.

## Definition of done

- [ ] The joint actions were named before the muscles
- [ ] The structure under load was named where risk was discussed
- [ ] Difficulty differences were explained through leverage, not effort
- [ ] Contested models were labelled as models
- [ ] No diagnosis was offered; referral advised where appropriate
- [ ] The explanation ended in a practical consequence for the program
