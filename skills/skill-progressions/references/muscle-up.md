# Muscle-up (Bar and Rings)

A pull-up, a transition, and a dip, chained. Most failed muscle-ups are failed
transitions, not failed pulls — but the strength gate still comes first.

---

## Gate

| Requirement | Bar | Rings |
|---|---|---|
| Strict pull-ups | 10+ | 12+ |
| Strict dips | 12+ | 10+ ring dips, feet free |
| Explosive pull height | chest to bar, controlled | sternum to rings |
| False grip | not required (helpful) | **required** — 3 x 20 s false-grip hang, pain-free |
| Straight-arm / shoulder | clean screen, 20 s support hold | clean screen, 20 s ring support hold |
| Wrist | pain-free | false grip is demanding on the wrist — build over 8–12 weeks |

An athlete below the gate is programmed a strength block, not a muscle-up block.
"Nearly there" muscle-up attempts produce shoulder and elbow problems and teach
a kip.

---

## The false grip (rings, and useful on the bar)

The wrist sits **over** the ring, the heel of the hand on top, so the forearm is
already above the ring at the top of the pull — this is what makes the transition
possible without a swing.

**It adapts slowly.** Introduce 8–12 weeks before muscle-up work:

| Week | Dose |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | false-grip hang 3 x 10 s |
| 3–4 | false-grip hang 3 x 20 s |
| 5–6 | false-grip ring rows 3 x 8 |
| 7–8 | false-grip pull-ups 3 x 3–5 |
| 9+ | false-grip pull-ups 3 x 5–8, transitions |

Expect wrist soreness in the first weeks; expect **no** sharp pain. Sharp ulnar-
side wrist pain stops false-grip work and gets screened.

## The ladder

| # | Rung | What it builds | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Strength base** | pull-ups and dips to the gate | see gate table |
| 1 | **Explosive / high pull-ups** | pull height | 5 x 3 chest-to-bar (bar) or sternum-to-rings (rings) |
| 2 | **Straight-bar dips / deep ring dips** | the catch position | 3 x 8 |
| 3 | **Transition drills — bent-arm** | the specific movement | see below |
| 4 | **Jumping muscle-up (low bar/rings)** | full pattern, reduced load | 5 x 3 |
| 5 | **Band-assisted muscle-up** | full pattern | 5 x 2–3 |
| 6 | **Negative muscle-up** | eccentric through the transition, 5 s | 5 x 2 |
| 7 | **Kipping/hip-assist muscle-up** *(optional)* | timing | — |
| 8 | **Strict muscle-up** | the goal | 3 x 1–3 |
| 9 | **Slow / L-sit / wide muscle-up** | mastery | — |

## Transition drills (rung 3)

The transition is elbow flexion combined with rapid shoulder extension-to-flexion
while the trunk passes over the bar or rings.

| Drill | How |
|---|---|
| **Ring transition from a box** | feet on a box, false grip, pull to the sternum then lean forward and roll the elbows over the rings, finishing in support. 5 x 3–5 |
| **Russian dip** *(rings, advanced)* | in support, lower from the elbows to a forearm-supported position and press back — extremely demanding, late-stage only |
| **Bar transition with band assist** | band under the feet, practise rolling the chest over the bar |
| **Negative transition** | start in support, lower slowly through the transition into the hang, 5 s |
| **Hip-to-bar pull-ups** | teaching how high the pull must actually be |

---

## Technical points

- **Pull higher than you think.** The bar or rings must reach at least the
  sternum, ideally lower ribs, before the transition begins.
- **Lean forward through the transition**, do not pull straight up. On the bar,
  the chest goes over the bar; on rings, the elbows roll back and the torso
  leans forward over the hands.
- **Keep the rings/bar close.** Distance is what makes the transition impossible.
- **Elbows are bent through the transition** — this is a bent-arm skill; there is
  no straight-arm phase to protect here, but the shoulder is at end range under
  load, which is why the gate exists.
- **Finish the dip.** A muscle-up that ends at a half-locked support is an
  incomplete rep.

## Weekly structure (development phase)

| Element | Dose |
|---|---|
| False-grip hang / pull work | 3 x 10–20 s or 3 x 5 |
| Explosive pull-ups | 5 x 3, full rest, fresh |
| Transition drills | 5 x 3–5 |
| Ring or straight-bar dips | 3 x 8 |
| Weighted pull-ups | 4 x 5 |
| Scapular and cuff prehab | every session |

Muscle-up attempts themselves: **low volume, high quality, fresh** — 5 x 1–3,
with 2–3 minutes of rest. Grinding failed attempts under fatigue is how shoulders
get hurt.

## Common faults

| Fault | Why it matters | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Pulling too low | there is nothing to transition from | explosive pull-ups to the sternum |
| Losing the false grip | the transition becomes impossible | dedicated false-grip hangs; the grip must be set before the pull |
| Chicken-winging (one arm transitions first) | asymmetric shoulder load, a common injury mechanism | slow down, use band assistance, drill symmetric transitions |
| Bar/rings drifting away | the lever gets long and the transition fails | "keep it close, elbows back" |
| Excessive kip to compensate | high shoulder and low-back load | go back to the strength gate |
| No dip at the end | incomplete rep, and dips are the weakest link for many | ring dip volume |

---

## Injury notes

- The transition puts the shoulder at **end-range extension under load** — the
  reason for the strict gate. Anyone with a painful arc, an instability history,
  or front-of-shoulder pain does not train muscle-ups until cleared.
- **Ring turnout at the top** adds significant stress. Add it after the muscle-up
  is comfortable, not before.
- **Chicken-winging** is a genuine injury mechanism, not just a style issue.
  Correct it before adding volume.
- Bar muscle-ups on a doorway bar or an unrated anchor: never. The dynamic load
  is several times bodyweight.
