# Planche

The highest connective-tissue cost of the common calisthenics skills, and the
one where impatience is punished most reliably. Straight arms, shoulders in front
of the hands, body horizontal, scapulae protracted and depressed.

**Primary demand:** anterior deltoid, pectoralis major (clavicular), biceps as a
shoulder flexor, serratus anterior, long head of biceps and elbow flexor tendons
under tension, plus a very high anti-extension core demand.

---

## Gate — before rung 1

- 20+ clean push-ups, 8+ pull-ups, 12+ dips
- 45 s hollow hold, flat low back
- 60 s pain-free front support on flat hands, or parallettes available
- straight-arm tolerance probe passed with no next-day elbow symptoms
- 8+ weeks of uninterrupted training

---

## The ladder

| # | Rung | Position | Target before advancing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Wrist prep + front support** | plank on straight arms, hard protraction | 60 s x 3, pain-free |
| 1 | **Planche lean** | front support, shoulders travelling in front of the wrists; measure the lean in cm | 5 x 15 s at a lean where the shoulders are clearly past the wrists |
| 2 | **Frog stand / crow** | bent arms, knees on the elbows — balance and protraction, low tendon cost | 3 x 30 s |
| 3 | **Straight-arm frog stand (tuck planche entry)** | arms locked, knees off the elbows, hips low | 5 x 8 s |
| 4 | **Tuck planche** | arms straight, hips at shoulder height, knees tucked to chest, back rounded (protracted) | 5 x 10–12 s |
| 5 | **Advanced tuck planche** | as tuck, but the back is flat and the hips open toward 90° at the hip | 5 x 10–12 s |
| 6 | **One-leg-out tuck planche** | one leg extended, other tucked; alternate sides evenly | 5 x 8–10 s per side |
| 7 | **Straddle planche** | legs straight and straddled wide; the width of the straddle is the progression | 5 x 5–8 s |
| 8 | **Half-lay / full planche** | legs together, body horizontal | 3 x 3–5 s |

**Rate:** one rung per 4–12 weeks for most athletes. Rungs 5→7 are where people
stall for a year; that is normal, not failure.

## Band assistance — the step between the rungs

The rungs above are large jumps: advanced tuck to straddle is a 20–30% increase
in shoulder torque. A resistance band gives you a step a fraction of that size,
and it is the right tool for tendon-limited work.

**Setup:** band anchored overhead (a pull-up bar or rings) and passed under the
hips or the chest in the planche line. Not under the feet — that changes the
shape rather than unloading it.

**Run it as a double progression, one variable at a time:**

```
Prescription: straddle planche, 5 x 5-8" @ RPE 8, loop M under the hips
Progress: every set reaches the TOP of the window with clean position (elbows
          locked, scapulae protracted, hips at shoulder height), two consecutive
          sessions, and no next-day elbow or wrist symptoms
          -> scale the band DOWN one step, reset to the BOTTOM of the window
Ceiling:  no band at the top of the window -> next rung, back onto a band if needed
Regress:  any set under 60% of the window bottom, OR the elbows bend, OR the
          scapulae retract, OR any inner-elbow ache -> back one band step, 2 weeks
```

**The window's upper bound is an instruction to stop a good set.** "Minimum 5 s,
maximum 10 s — above 10 s we scale the band" is what keeps an enthusiastic
athlete from turning a productive hold into an unplanned tendon overload.

Label bands consistently across the block (`loop M`, `loop L`) or record their
approximate assistance in kilograms. A band you cannot name is a progression you
cannot repeat.

## Key technical points

- **Protraction is not optional.** The scapulae must be protracted (pushed apart)
  and depressed. A retracted planche is a shoulder injury waiting to happen and
  a much weaker position.
- **Posterior pelvic tilt.** The hips are tucked under; the low back does not
  arch. The tuck planche's rounded back is correct at rung 4, and it flattens
  at rung 5.
- **Elbows locked.** A bent elbow means the rung is too hard. There is no such
  thing as a "slightly bent-arm planche" — it is a different exercise.
- **Hands turned out** on the floor (roughly 45°) to protect the wrist, or use
  parallettes.
- **Shoulder-past-wrist distance is the real progression variable** in the lean.
  Measure it in centimetres and record it — it is far more precise than "it felt
  hard".

## Weekly structure (development phase)

| Element | Dose | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wrist prep | 3–5 min, every session | non-negotiable |
| Planche lean or current hold | 5 x 10–12 s, 120–180 s rest | first thing after warm-up |
| Pseudo planche push-ups | 3 x 5–8, tempo `3-1-1-1` | the dynamic complement |
| Straight-arm protraction work | 3 x 10 slow scapular push-ups in front support | keeps the serratus honest |
| Hollow / anti-extension | 3 x 30–45 s | the core is the limiter more often than the shoulders |
| Elbow prep (biceps isometric 5 x 30 s + wrist curls) | every session | the insurance policy |

**Weekly TUT cap:** 120–240 s of planche holds. Add the lean time to this budget
— it is straight-arm load too.

## Accessories that actually transfer

| Exercise | Why |
|---|---|
| Pseudo planche push-up | dynamic shoulder flexion under lean |
| Straight-arm cable/band press-down in the planche line | loads shoulder flexion with a locked elbow, progressible in small steps |
| Ring support hold with turnout | straight-arm stability |
| Weighted dips and push-ups | raw pressing strength under the skill |
| Hollow body and ab wheel | the anti-extension demand |
| Banded shoulder flexion (front raise, straight arm, light) | tendon-friendly volume |
| Wrist and elbow protocol | tolerance for the position itself |

Bench press and overhead press do **not** transfer meaningfully to the planche.
Program them for other reasons or not at all.

## Advance / hold / regress

```
Advance: current rung held 5 x target time with clean quality (locked elbows,
         protracted scapulae, hips at shoulder height), two consecutive sessions,
         AND no elbow, wrist or shoulder symptoms in the 24 h after either.
         -> next rung, restart at 5 x 6-8 s, cut weekly TUT 30% for that week.

Hold:    quality is good but times are short -> stay, add 2 s per set per week.

Regress: cannot hold 60% of target, OR the elbows bend, OR the scapulae retract,
         OR any inner-elbow / front-shoulder symptom -> previous rung for
         2 weeks minimum, and run the elbow protocol if there were symptoms.
```

## Injury notes specific to the planche

- **Distal biceps tendon and brachialis** at the elbow: the classic planche
  injury. Warning sign is a deep ache at the front of the elbow during or after
  holds. Response: stop all straight-arm work, isometric elbow-flexor loading,
  rebuild over 4–6 weeks. Do not "train through".
- **Long head of biceps at the shoulder** (front-of-shoulder ache in the
  bicipital groove): reduce lean, check that the scapulae are protracting, reduce
  volume 40%.
- **Wrist extension load** is extreme. Parallettes are not a crutch; for many
  athletes they are the correct long-term tool.
- **Anterior shoulder capsule**: if there is any history of instability, the
  planche is a late-stage goal, after a full strength base and a clean screen.

---

## Bodyweight and leverage honesty

Planche difficulty scales with mass and with limb length. A taller, heavier
athlete is not doing anything wrong when their timeline is twice as long. State
this once, as physics, and then coach the ladder.
