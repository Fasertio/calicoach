# Handstand, Handstand Push-up, Press to Handstand

The handstand is a **skill** before it is a strength feat. It responds to
frequency and to patient balance practice, not to volume.

---

# THE HANDSTAND

## Gate

- 60 s pain-free flat-hand front support (or parallettes available)
- wall overhead reach: hands to the wall with a flat low back, or a documented
  plan to work toward it
- 45 s hollow hold
- **bail technique learned before any freestanding attempt** — see below
- no active wrist or shoulder constraint

## Learn the bail first

Non-negotiable. Before any freestanding work:

1. **Pirouette bail** — when falling forward, turn the shoulders and step one
   hand around, coming down sideways on the feet. Practise 20 times against the
   wall before the first freestanding attempt.
2. **Forward roll out** — from a handstand, tuck the chin, bend the arms, and
   roll. Practise on a mat.
3. **Never** bail by collapsing straight down onto the head or by arching over
   backwards uncontrolled.

## The ladder

| # | Rung | What it is | Target before advancing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Wrist prep** | the daily protocol | every session, 3–5 min |
| 1 | **Plank + hollow + wall plank** | body line and shoulder position | 60 s each |
| 2 | **Pike push-up / wall walk** | pressing and inversion tolerance | 3 x 8 pike push-ups; wall walk to hands 30 cm from the wall |
| 3 | **Chest-to-wall handstand (facing the wall)** | the correct alignment position — this is where the shape is built | 3 x 60 s, ribs down, no arch |
| 4 | **Back-to-wall handstand** | easier for entry, worse for shape; use for volume only | 3 x 45 s |
| 5 | **Wall handstand with heel pulls / toe pulls** | learning to find balance off the wall | 10 x 3–5 s off the wall |
| 6 | **Freestanding handstand** | kick up, find balance | 30 s accumulated, then 10 s single hold |
| 7 | **Freestanding handstand 30 s+** | consistent balance | consistency across 5 attempts |

## Key technical points

- **The line:** hands, wrists, elbows, shoulders, hips, knees, ankles stacked.
  Shoulders fully open (ears "in front of" the arms when viewed side-on), ribs
  down, pelvis posteriorly tilted, legs together, toes pointed.
- **Balance comes from the fingers**, not from the hips. Falling forward → press
  through the fingertips. Falling backward → release finger pressure and let the
  heel of the hand take load.
- **Chest-to-wall is where the shape is learned.** Back-to-wall lets an athlete
  hold a banana shape for months and reinforce it. Use back-to-wall only for
  accumulating time.
- **Look between the hands** or slightly forward, not at the floor beneath the
  face.
- **Fingers spread, hands shoulder width**, arms straight and actively pushing
  the floor away (elevated scapulae — the opposite of the pull-up cue).

## Frequency and dose

The handstand is the one skill where **daily short practice wins decisively**.

| Level | Prescription |
|---|---|
| Learning | 5–10 min, 4–6 days per week, always fresh |
| Developing | 10–15 min, 4–5 days per week, split into short sets |
| Consolidating | 15–20 min, including entries and shapes |

Practise in short sets (20–40 s) with full rest. Fatigue destroys balance
learning and teaches compensation.

## Common faults

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Banana back (arched) | ribs flared, no pelvic tilt, or limited shoulder flexion | chest-to-wall work, hollow holds, thoracic and lat mobility |
| Shoulders not open | shoulder flexion range limitation | wall slides, lat and thoracic work, keep back-to-wall volume low |
| Legs apart / bent | inattention, or hip flexor and adductor weakness | cue "squeeze a coin between the heels", straddle-to-together drills |
| Elbows bending | insufficient pressing strength, or over-balancing forward | pike push-ups, wall handstand holds, more balance work |
| Constant over-balancing forward | not using the fingers | fingertip pressure drills against a wall |
| Wrist pain | tolerance not built | wrist protocol, parallettes, reduce daily volume by 40% |

## Accessories

Pike push-ups, wall handstand holds, shoulder-flexion mobility (wall slides,
banded lat stretches), hollow body, wrist protocol, thoracic extension work,
straight-arm scapular elevation (shrug in a handstand position against a wall).

# HANDSTAND PUSH-UP (HSPU)

## Gate

- 3 x 45 s chest-to-wall handstand hold
- 3 x 8 elevated pike push-ups with the head touching the floor
- no shoulder constraint; full pain-free overhead range

## The ladder

| # | Rung | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pike push-up, feet on the floor | 3 x 10 |
| 2 | Elevated pike push-up (feet on a box) | 3 x 8 |
| 3 | Wall HSPU, partial range (head to a stack of books/pads) | 3 x 5 |
| 4 | Wall HSPU, full range (head to the floor) | 3 x 5 |
| 5 | Wall HSPU, deficit (hands on parallettes, head below hand level) | 3 x 5 |
| 6 | Freestanding HSPU | 3 x 3 |

## Technical points

- **Head and hands form a triangle** — head slightly forward of the hand line —
  for balance and for shoulder position.
- Elbows track forward and slightly out (~45°), not flared to a T.
- Full lockout with scapular elevation at the top.
- Back-to-wall is the standard for training; chest-to-wall deficit HSPU is
  harder and closer to the freestanding version.

## Accessories

Overhead or landmine press, seated dumbbell press, pike push-ups, triceps work,
handstand holds. Overhead pressing strength transfers well here (unlike the
planche).

# PRESS TO HANDSTAND

Late-stage. Requires compression, straight-arm strength and shoulder flexion
range simultaneously.

## Gate

- 30 s freestanding handstand
- 20 s L-sit
- straddle compression lift: sit in a straddle and lift the hips off the floor

## The ladder

| # | Rung |
|---|---|
| 1 | Straddle compression lifts (seated, hips off the floor) |
| 2 | Elevated pike/straddle press with feet on a box, hips over shoulders |
| 3 | Negative press: from handstand, lower slowly to a straddle stand |
| 4 | Band-assisted press |
| 5 | Straddle press to handstand |
| 6 | Pike (legs together) press to handstand |

Compression is the usual limiter, not strength. Program compression work
(seated pike and straddle lifts, candlesticks, L-sit and V-sit progressions)
before adding press attempts.

---

## Injury notes for all handstand work

- **Wrists** are the limiting tissue for almost everyone. Run the wrist protocol
  every session, use parallettes when symptomatic, and increase weight-bearing
  time by no more than ~10% per week.
- **Shoulders**: an athlete without full pain-free overhead range should not be
  accumulating handstand volume — they will compensate with a lumbar arch. Fix
  the range first.
- **Low back**: persistent banana shape means an anti-extension and mobility
  problem, not a balance problem. It also loads the lumbar spine every session.
- **Falls**: the bail is the safety system. If the athlete cannot bail reliably,
  they train against a wall.
