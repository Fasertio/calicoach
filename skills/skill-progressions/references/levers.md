# Front Lever and Back Lever

Both are straight-arm holds. The front lever is a pulling skill with an enormous
anti-extension core demand; the back lever is a shoulder-extension hold with the
highest distal-biceps tendon cost of any common calisthenics skill.

---

# FRONT LEVER

Hanging from a bar, body horizontal and face-up, arms straight, scapulae
depressed and retracted, body in a straight line.

**Primary demand:** latissimus dorsi, teres major, long head of triceps (shoulder
extension), lower trapezius, rectus abdominis and obliques (anti-extension), plus
the elbow flexor tendons under a straight-arm load.

## Gate

- 8+ strict pull-ups, 10+ ring rows with feet elevated
- 45 s hollow hold with a flat low back
- 5 clean scapular pull-ups with a 2 s hold
- straight-arm tolerance probe passed, no elbow symptoms
- no active shoulder or elbow constraint

## The ladder

| # | Rung | Position | Target before advancing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Scapular pull-ups + hollow hold** | the two prerequisites | 5 x 5 with 2 s hold; 45 s hollow |
| 1 | **Tuck front lever** | knees to chest, back parallel to the floor, arms straight | 5 x 10–12 s |
| 2 | **Advanced tuck front lever** | hips open toward 90°, back flat, shins parallel to the floor | 5 x 10–12 s |
| 3 | **One-leg front lever** | one leg extended, one tucked; alternate sides evenly | 5 x 8–10 s per side |
| 4 | **Straddle front lever** | legs straight, straddled; narrow the straddle to progress | 5 x 6–10 s |
| 5 | **Full front lever** | legs together, body horizontal | 3 x 5–10 s |

**Rate:** one rung per 4–10 weeks. Rung 3→4 is the common long plateau.

## Key technical points

- **Scapulae depressed and retracted, arms locked.** If the elbows bend, the rung
  is too hard.
- **Posterior pelvic tilt.** The body is in a hollow, not an arch. A sagging
  lower back means the core failed, not the lats — regress and add anti-extension
  work.
- **Horizontal means horizontal.** Athletes drift to 20–30° above horizontal and
  call it a lever. Film side-on; the shoulders and hips and heels should be one
  line, parallel to the floor.
- **Actively pull down on the bar** with straight arms throughout the hold — it
  is not a passive hang in a shape.

## Weekly structure (development phase)

| Element | Dose |
|---|---|
| Current lever rung | 5 x 8–12 s, 120–180 s rest |
| Straight-arm pulldown | 3 x 10–15, tempo `3-0-1-1` |
| Front lever raises / negatives (from inverted hang) | 3 x 3–5 slow |
| Weighted or tempo pull-ups | 3–4 x 5 |
| Hollow body / ab wheel | 3 x 30–45 s |
| Elbow prep | every session |

**Weekly TUT cap:** 120–240 s of lever holds; count negatives and raises against
the same budget.

## Accessories that transfer

| Exercise | Why |
|---|---|
| Straight-arm pulldown | the single best accessory — shoulder extension with a locked elbow, load progressible in 2.5 kg steps |
| Tuck front lever raises | dynamic strength through the position |
| Front lever negatives from an inverted hang | eccentric overload, high tendon cost — cap at 3 x 3 |
| Ice-cream makers | advanced; extremely high biceps-tendon load, late-stage only |
| Ab wheel and hollow work | the core is the limiter for most people at rungs 2–3 |
| Weighted pull-ups | the bent-arm base under the skill |

## Advance / hold / regress

```
Advance: current rung 5 x target time, body demonstrably horizontal, arms locked,
         no next-day elbow or shoulder symptoms, two consecutive sessions.
         -> next rung at 5 x 6-8 s, weekly TUT -30% that week.
Hold:    quality good, time short -> +2 s per set per week.
Regress: under 60% of target, OR elbows bend, OR the hips sag, OR any elbow
         symptom -> previous rung, 2 weeks minimum.
```

## Injury notes

- The **distal biceps tendon** and the **long head of biceps at the shoulder**
  are the tissues at risk. Warning sign: a deep ache at the front of the elbow or
  in the bicipital groove, appearing during holds or the next morning.
- **Do not combine** a front lever block with a back lever block. Both load the
  same tissue in the same way, and the volumes add.
- Rotate grip occasionally (rings allow the forearm to rotate and are kinder to
  the elbow than a fixed bar).

  and injury guidance.

# BACK LEVER

Hanging from a bar or rings, body horizontal and face-down, arms straight behind
the body, shoulders in extension.

**Primary demand:** shoulder extension range under load, biceps and elbow flexor
tendons (very high), anterior deltoid and pec at end range, spinal erectors and
glutes to hold the line.

**Warning:** the back lever looks like the easiest of the levers and carries the
highest per-second elbow-tendon cost. Torn distal biceps tendons in calisthenics
cluster around back lever and its entries. Treat it with more caution than the
front lever, not less.

## Gate

- everything in the front lever gate, plus
- comfortable, pain-free **supported German hang** for 3 x 20 s with feet taking
  part of the load
- no history of biceps tendon symptoms
- **rings preferred over a fixed bar** — the free rotation dramatically reduces
  elbow stress

## The ladder

| # | Rung | Position | Target before advancing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Supported German hang** | feet on the floor, gradually shifting load | 3 x 30 s pain-free |
| 1 | **Full German hang (skin the cat bottom position)** | full shoulder extension, controlled | 3 x 20 s |
| 2 | **Tuck back lever** | knees tucked, back parallel | 5 x 10–15 s |
| 3 | **Advanced tuck back lever** | hips open, back flat | 5 x 10–15 s |
| 4 | **One-leg back lever** | one leg extended | 5 x 8–10 s per side |
| 5 | **Straddle back lever** | legs straight, straddled | 5 x 8–10 s |
| 6 | **Full back lever** | legs together, horizontal | 3 x 5–10 s |

## Key technical points

- **Elbows locked, but never hyperextended.** Athletes with hypermobile elbows
  must keep a soft lock and should approach this skill very slowly, or not at
  all.
- **Enter and exit slowly.** Most back lever injuries happen in the transition,
  not the hold. Never drop into the position.
- **Rings over bar.** On a bar the forearm cannot rotate, and the elbow takes the
  difference.
- **Squeeze the glutes and keep a mild hollow-to-neutral line** — do not hang in
  a lumbar arch.

## Weekly structure

| Element | Dose |
|---|---|
| German hang progression | 3 x 20–30 s |
| Current back lever rung | 4 x 8–12 s, long rests |
| Biceps isometric and eccentric work | 5 x 30 s isometric, then slow eccentric curls |
| Straight-arm pulldown | 3 x 12 |
| Posterior chain and glute work | to hold the line |

**Weekly TUT cap:** 90–180 s. Lower than the front lever, deliberately.

## Advance / hold / regress

```
Advance: 4 x target time, arms locked, body horizontal, absolutely no elbow
         symptoms during or in the 48 h after, two consecutive sessions.
Regress: ANY elbow discomfort -> stop back lever entirely for 2 weeks, run the
         elbow protocol, return one rung lower.
```

The regress trigger here is deliberately stricter than for any other skill.
