# Skill-Track Session Templates

A skill block is not a strength block with holds added at the top. It runs on
the tendon clock, so it is high frequency, low fatigue and quality-capped: the
skill leads every session it appears in, the volume is time under tension
rather than sets, and the exit criterion is a hold, not a rep.

These are shapes, not prescriptions. The dose comes from the stage table in
[SKILL.md](../SKILL.md); the interference costs come from its "Programming a
skill alongside strength" table. What is here is the order of a day and what
the rest of the session is allowed to be.

Three families, because the failure mode differs. Straight-arm skills fail at
the elbow, balance skills fail at the wrist and at attention, transition skills
fail at the shoulder under speed.

---

## A. Straight-arm — planche, front lever, back lever, human flag

**The constraint:** connective tissue at the elbow and the long head of the
biceps. This is the family where a good week does the damage.

| Slot | Content | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Prep | wrist and elbow prep, scapular activation | 8–10 min, non-negotiable |
| 1 | **The skill**, unpaired, fully rested | fresh or not at all; 150–180 s rest |
| 2 | Straight-arm accessory at a lower rung | only if the week's TUT budget has room |
| A1/A2 | Bent-arm primary pair — pull against push | the base that keeps progressing |
| B1/B2 | Secondary pair | reduced per the interference table |
| P | Elbow flexor isometric, cuff, scapular | the elbow protocol is part of the session |

**Rules that bind here:**

- Skill TUT climbs ≤ 10% a week. `calicoach check` computes this from the
  volume budget and fails the block if it does not hold.
- One leverage step per 2–3 weeks, and the plan's weekly columns are checked
  for it. Volume drops 30% in the week of an advance.
- Never change the band and the hold window in the same week.
- Any inner-elbow ache ends the skill session — not the set, the session.
- Two straight-arm exposures a week is usually right; three only in
  consolidation, never with a leverage advance in the same week.

## B. Balance — handstand, handstand push-up

**The constraint:** the wrist, and attention. Balance is a motor skill before it
is a strength skill, and a tired nervous system learns nothing.

| Slot | Content | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Prep | wrist prep, thoracic and shoulder flexion | wrists first, always |
| 1 | **Balance practice**, short sets, long rests | stop on quality, not on failure |
| 2 | Line drills against the wall | quality work, not conditioning |
| A1/A2 | Primary pair — pressing and pulling | overhead volume counts against slot 1 |
| B | Secondary, core | anti-extension serves the line directly |
| P | Wrist protocol | twice daily on the days between, too |

**Rules that bind here:**

- Daily short exposures beat two long ones. This is the one family where
  frequency is the whole method.
- Total practice time is capped by attention, not by the shoulder: when the
  line degrades, the session is over even if nothing hurts.
- Overhead pressing and handstand work draw on the same shoulder-flexion
  budget. Count them together or the week quietly doubles.

---

## C. Transition — muscle-up, and the explosive pull that feeds it

**The constraint:** the shoulder under speed, and the elbow on the catch.

| Slot | Content | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Prep | shoulder prep, scapular, false-grip tolerance | false grip is its own adaptation |
| 1 | **Transition work** — explosive pull, catch drills | fresh; low reps, full rest |
| 2 | Straight-arm or hold work only if the week allows | rarely in the same session |
| A1/A2 | Pull primary, paired with a light push | pull volume stays high |
| B | Dip volume, reduced | the catch already loads it |
| P | Elbow and cuff | the catch is an eccentric the athlete did not plan |

**Rules that bind here:**

- Never program a transition attempt when fatigued. A failed catch is an
  unplanned eccentric at the bottom of a dip.
- Explosive work is low-rep and fully rested: 3–5 sets of 2–3, never to
  failure, never as conditioning.
- Keep pull volume high and trim dips rather than pulls.

---

## What all three share

1. **The skill goes first, or it does not go.** If the session cannot afford it
   fresh, the skill is not in that session.
2. **Quality clock.** Hold time counts only while the position is correct.
3. **The bent-arm base keeps progressing.** A skill block that abandons
   pull-ups and dips produces a temporary skill and a weaker athlete.
4. **Film one side-on clip per week per skill.** Skills fail invisibly.
