# Human Flag, Pistol Squat, One-Arm Pull-up, L-sit / V-sit

---

# HUMAN FLAG

Body horizontal, held sideways on a vertical pole or two bars — the top arm pulls,
the bottom arm pushes, and the obliques hold everything together.

## Gate

- 10+ pull-ups, 15+ push-ups, 20 s side plank each side at full quality
- 3 x 20 s pain-free overhead hold with load (the top arm's position)
- no shoulder constraint; the top shoulder works at end-range abduction under load
- grip: pinch and support grip developed (plate pinches, dead hangs)

## The ladder

| # | Rung | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Vertical flag / chamber hold** — body vertical, hands set, take the weight | 5 x 10 s |
| 2 | **Support drill** — feet on a box, take load through both arms in the flag line | 5 x 10 s |
| 3 | **Tuck flag** — knees tucked, body horizontal-ish | 5 x 8–10 s per side |
| 4 | **Advanced tuck flag** — hips open | 5 x 8 s per side |
| 5 | **One-leg-out flag** — one leg extended (usually the top leg first) | 5 x 6–8 s per side |
| 6 | **Straddle flag** | 5 x 5–8 s per side |
| 7 | **Full flag** — legs together, horizontal | 3 x 3–5 s per side |

## Technical points

- **Bottom arm pushes, top arm pulls.** Most beginners try to hold it entirely
  with the top arm; the push is at least half the work.
- **Hand spacing:** roughly shoulder-to-hip distance on the pole. Wider is easier
  at first but changes the mechanics.
- **Press the bottom shoulder down and away** — do not let it collapse into the
  joint.
- **Both sides, always.** The flag is the most asymmetric common skill; train
  both sides equally even if one feels hopeless.
- **Entry matters:** enter by pressing up from a chamber (tucked) position, not
  by kicking.

## Accessories

Side plank progressions, Copenhagen plank, suitcase carry, overhead press,
lateral raise, straight-arm pulldown, pinch grip work, oblique-focused hanging
work (hanging windshield wipers, late-stage).

## Injury notes

Very high demand on the **top shoulder at end-range abduction** and on the
**obliques and lateral trunk**. Rib and intercostal strains are common in
athletes who progress too fast. Progress by holding time at the current rung, not
by attempting the next rung on a good day.

## References

- *Overcoming Gravity* (2nd ed.), Steven Low — flag progressions.
- Video search terms: "human flag tuck progression", "human flag bottom arm push
  cue", "chamber hold flag entry".

# PISTOL SQUAT

Single-leg squat to full depth with the free leg extended forward. Limited more
often by ankle dorsiflexion and hip control than by leg strength.

## Gate

- knee-to-wall dorsiflexion ≥ 10 cm (or accept heel elevation)
- 20 bodyweight squats to full depth, heels down
- 10 controlled box step-downs per side without knee valgus
- no active knee constraint

## The ladder

| # | Rung | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Box squat to a high box, single leg** | 3 x 8 per side |
| 2 | **Lower the box progressively** (5 cm steps) | 3 x 8 at each height |
| 3 | **Assisted pistol** (holding a TRX, doorframe or pole) | 3 x 8 per side |
| 4 | **Counterweight pistol** (holding a 5–10 kg plate in front) | 3 x 5 per side |
| 5 | **Heel-elevated pistol** | 3 x 5 per side |
| 6 | **Full pistol squat** | 3 x 5 per side |
| 7 | **Weighted pistol / shrimp squat** | 3 x 5 |

## Technical points

- **Ankle range is usually the limiter.** If the heel lifts, either elevate it
  deliberately (a legitimate long-term option) or run ankle mobility work and
  re-test.
- **The free leg is active** — hold it straight and off the floor; it is a
  hip-flexor endurance task in itself.
- **Knee tracks over the second toe.** Valgus collapse at the bottom means glute
  medius work is needed before more pistol volume.
- **Counterweight is the best teaching tool** — a 5 kg plate held forward shifts
  the centre of mass and makes the pattern accessible immediately.
- **Control the descent.** A dropped pistol is a knee-loading event.

## Accessories

Split squats, Bulgarian split squats, step-downs, hip airplane, glute medius work
(banded lateral walks, clamshells), ankle mobility, calf raises, hamstring work.

## Injury notes

High patellofemoral load at the bottom. With an anterior-knee constraint, work
the box progression only within the pain-free range, add isometric quad work
(wall sit, Spanish squat), and do not chase depth.

## References

- NSCA *Essentials of Strength Training and Conditioning* — single-leg mechanics.
- *Overcoming Gravity* (2nd ed.), Steven Low — pistol progressions.
- Video search terms: "pistol squat counterweight progression", "pistol squat
  ankle mobility", "box pistol progression".

# ONE-ARM PULL-UP (OAP)

The hardest common bent-arm skill, and one of the highest elbow-injury risks in
calisthenics. Timeline is measured in years.

## Gate

- 15+ strict two-arm pull-ups, or a weighted pull-up with +50% bodyweight x 3
- clean elbow screen, no history of medial elbow symptoms
- 3 x 20 s one-arm hang per side, pain-free
- solid scapular control and no shoulder constraint

## The ladder

| # | Rung | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One-arm hang** (free hand off) | 3 x 20 s per side |
| 2 | **One-arm scapular pull-up** | 3 x 5 per side |
| 3 | **Archer pull-up** (straight arm stays straight) | 3 x 5 per side |
| 4 | **Assisted OAP — towel/band on the free arm**, assistance reducing over months | 3 x 3 per side |
| 5 | **Assisted OAP — free hand on the wrist, then forearm, then a finger** | 3 x 3 per side |
| 6 | **OAP negative** — 5–8 s eccentric | 3 x 2 per side |
| 7 | **One-arm pull-up** | singles |

## Technical points

- **The body rotates.** Accept a small rotation; fighting it entirely wastes
  energy, but a large swing means the rung is too hard.
- **Scapular control first.** The one-arm scapular pull-up is the honest gate for
  rung 3 onward.
- **Both sides equally**, even though one will always be behind.
- **Low volume, high quality, long rests.** 3 x 3 per side is a full session's
  worth of one-arm work.

## Injury notes

Treat OAP volume like straight-arm volume: cap the weekly increase at 10%, and
stop at the first medial-elbow symptom. Elbow prehab (wrist curls, reverse wrist
curls, pronation/supination, biceps isometrics) is mandatory alongside it.

## References

- *Overcoming Gravity* (2nd ed.), Steven Low — one-arm pull-up progressions and
  the elbow warnings.
- Video search terms: "one arm pull up progression assisted", "archer pull up
  form", "one arm scapular pull up".

# L-SIT AND V-SIT

Straight-arm support plus active compression. The foundation for the press to
handstand and a good general test of core and hip-flexor strength.

## Gate

- 20 s support hold with depressed shoulders
- 45 s hollow hold
- hamstring range sufficient to sit in a pike with a neutral spine

## The ladder

| # | Rung | Target |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Foot-supported L-sit** on parallettes, feet on the floor | 5 x 15 s |
| 2 | **Tuck L-sit** | 5 x 15 s |
| 3 | **One-leg-out L-sit** | 5 x 10 s per side |
| 4 | **Full L-sit** (legs straight, parallel to the floor) | 5 x 15 s |
| 5 | **L-sit on the floor** (much harder — shorter arms) | 5 x 10 s |
| 6 | **Advanced L-sit** (legs above parallel) | 5 x 10 s |
| 7 | **V-sit** | 5 x 5–8 s |

## Technical points

- **Shoulders depressed, arms locked** — this is a straight-arm skill; if the
  shoulders shrug, the rung is too hard.
- **Actively pull the legs up**, do not just hold them. Compression is trained,
  not endured.
- **Point the toes and lock the knees** — it changes the whole shape.
- **Parallettes first.** Floor L-sits demand more shoulder depression and more
  compression because the arms are effectively shorter.

## Accessories

Seated pike and straddle compression lifts, hanging leg raises, hollow holds,
straight-arm support holds, hamstring mobility (active, not passive).

## References

- *Overcoming Gravity* (2nd ed.), Steven Low — L-sit and compression progressions.
- Video search terms: "L-sit progression parallettes", "compression drills
  straddle lift", "V-sit progression".
